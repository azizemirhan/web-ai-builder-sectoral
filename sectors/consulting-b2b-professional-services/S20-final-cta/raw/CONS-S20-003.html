Before you go

Four reasons not to, and one ask.

If you have read this far you are not undecided. You have a reason.

There is no gap between the objections and the ask. The ask is not a separate move after them — it is what is left when three of the four are agreed with.

  • Do not write

    No decision waiting

    Come back when there is one. It is the first thing we would ask anyway, and without it there is no proposal.

  • Do not write

    It is not your call

    Take the five questions to whoever’s call it is. They work perfectly well without us in the room.

  • Do not write

    You think we are wrong

    Say which, rather than booking an hour. We have revised a published piece on a reader’s argument before.

  • Write

    You want to check us first

    Ask for the reference call. It is a client of ours, and we are not on it.

One hour, with the person who would run the work.

Not a salesperson; we do not have any. It can end in a no from either side, and roughly one in four does.

Nothing here is new. It is the same ask, made once, at the point where you know enough to answer it.
