# Section Role

What the firm has published, and whether it still believes it.

# The Trap

The most content-farm-shaped section on any professional-services site. The default is a grid of
cards with a stock photograph, a category pill, a date and a reading-time badge, under titles like
*Navigating the future of digital transformation*.

Each of those is a signal about volume or retention rather than about thinking — and the title itself
cannot be wrong, which is exactly why it is worthless.

# The Governing Idea

> **A piece that could not have been wrong was not worth writing.**

So every piece carries two things a normal insights card does not: **the claim it makes**, stated
plainly enough to disagree with, and **whether the firm still believes it.**

# The Five Pieces

| Title | The claim | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The reorganisation will not move the decision rights | Authority follows budget, not reporting lines | **Still holds** |
| Cost programmes rebuild the cost they removed | Removing cost without removing the work buys a delay | **Still holds** |
| Outsourcing a capability you do not understand transfers the confusion | You cannot specify what you have never run | **Still holds** |
| Data quality is a governance problem, not a tooling one | A tool cannot decide who owns a definition | **Partly wrong** |
| A central function should own the standard, not the delivery | Central delivery becomes a queue | **We were wrong** |

All five studies carry the same five, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

# The Status Field

**A publication list that admits an error is not a confession. It is the only evidence that the other
entries were ever falsifiable.** A list where everything still holds is a list of claims that were
never claims.

It also answers, about the firm itself, the question `S11` puts to the client: *what would change
your mind?*

# Nothing Is Taken Down

> **A piece we no longer believe keeps its address and gets a note at the top saying so.**

Deleting the withdrawn piece would leave a publication list that has never been wrong — the thing
this section exists to argue against.

# The One Place A Card Grid Belongs

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` lists the card grid as a device to build with, and warns that **a
card without media is a table cell with rounded corners.** Most sections here cannot fill that image
area honestly, which is why `S11` used a rail and `S12` used none.

This one can. **A published piece has a cover**, and it is the firm's own artefact rather than a
photograph bought to fill a slot. The reserved area is always the same thing: *the opening spread of
the piece.*

# Titles Are Placeholder Data

The five titles are marked `data-placeholder="true"` — the firm's own published output, the same line
that lets `S06` placehold its own team and forbids `S09` from placeholding a client. The claims,
statuses and publishing policy are not placeholder data.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No stock photograph.** The only reserved image is the piece's own opening spread.
- **No reading-time badge, view count, share count or *most read* marker.** Retention metrics dressed
  as courtesy.
- **No category pill, date or byline.** Filing, not argument.
- **No title that could not be wrong.** If the claim cannot be disagreed with, the piece does not
  belong on the page.
- **No deletion.** A withdrawn piece keeps its address and carries a note.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
