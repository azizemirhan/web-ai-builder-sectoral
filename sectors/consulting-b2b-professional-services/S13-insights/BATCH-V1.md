## The Section Role

What the firm has published, and whether it still believes it.

## The Trap

This is the most content-farm-shaped section on any professional-services site. The default is a grid
of article cards carrying a stock photograph, a category pill, a date and a reading-time badge, under
titles like *Navigating the future of digital transformation*.

Every element of that is a signal about volume or retention rather than about thinking:

- **The stock photograph** — abstract nodes, a city at dusk, a laptop and a coffee. All three are
  named in the anti-patterns in `../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md`, and this is the section that
  reaches for them hardest.
- **The reading-time badge and the view count** — retention metrics dressed as courtesy.
- **The category pill and the date** — filing, not argument.
- **The title itself.** *Navigating the future of digital transformation* cannot be wrong, which is
  exactly why it is worthless.

## The Governing Idea

> **A piece that could not have been wrong was not worth writing.**

Most consulting thought leadership is published to be seen publishing rather than to be read, and the
tell is that it never says anything that could turn out false.

So every piece here carries two things a normal insights card does not:

1. **The claim it makes**, stated plainly enough that a reader could disagree with it.
2. **Whether the firm still believes it.**

## The Status Field, Which Is The Section

Three still hold, one is partly wrong, one the firm now thinks was wrong outright.

**A publication list that admits an error is not a confession. It is the only evidence that the other
entries were ever falsifiable.** A list where everything still holds is a list of claims that were
never claims.

It also answers, about the firm itself, the question `S11` puts to the client: *what would change
your mind?* This section is what that answer looks like when somebody actually keeps it — which is
the kind of continuity that makes the assembled page read as one mind rather than as twenty-seven
briefs.

## Nothing Is Taken Down

Every study closes on the same policy:

> **Nothing is taken down. A piece we no longer believe keeps its address and gets a note at the top
> saying so.**

Deleting the withdrawn piece would leave a publication list that has never been wrong, which is the
thing this section exists to argue against. The note is also the artefact `005` reserves an image
for.

## The Media Question, And The One Place A Card Grid Belongs

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` lists the card grid as a device to build with — *each card
carrying its own image area above a short label and a title* — and warns in the same breath that **a
card without media is a table cell with rounded corners.**

Most sections in this sector cannot fill that image area honestly, which is why `S11` used a rail and
`S12` used none at all. **This section can.** A published piece has a cover, and it is the firm's own
artefact rather than a photograph bought to fill a slot. So `003` uses the card grid as intended, for
the first time since `S02`.

The reserved area is always the same thing: **the opening spread of the piece.** Never a mood shot.

`001` and `004` carry none, for opposite reasons: in `001` the claim is the content and a cover would
be a lid on it; in `004` a cover would slow the scan the study is built for.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

The default action for an insights section is *subscribe* — it asks for an address and offers more of
the same. This study asks for something else.

> **It asks the reader to argue.**

Five claims are laid out to be scanned rather than read, precisely so a reader can find the one they
think is wrong, and the panel invites exactly that with a precedent that makes it credible: a piece
has already been revised on a reader's argument, and **the note at the top of it carries their
name.**

That converts the disagreeing reader — the most valuable one on the page, and the one every other
insights section quietly filters out.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — **shape C.** Four pieces on two columns, then the withdrawn claim taking the full width
in the accent, then the policy. The retraction is placed last so it reads as the payoff rather than
as an apology.

**`002`** — the lead piece gets its cover and the flagship position; the other four run beneath as an
editorial column. The status is always the last line in a block, because it is the part every other
firm omits.

**`003`** — **shape A**, and the only correct card grid in the sector since `S02`. See above. The
tally is stated in a line rather than left to be counted off the badges — the same discipline
`S10-003` and `S12-003` apply.

**`004`** — **the invitation.** See above. Nothing is featured, ranked or held larger than its
neighbours except the one that was withdrawn, because a reader disagreeing with a claim needs to
reach it in one pass.

**`005`** — **the piece we got wrong is the biggest thing on the page.** Display scale, the
correction note beside it, and the one reserved cover in the study given to the retraction rather
than to the best work. The four that still hold sit beneath it in a small row. That ordering *is* the
argument — the retracted piece is the only entry that proves the others could have been wrong, so it
earns the space. It is inverted prominence **between** items, where `S12-005` inverts which field
leads **within** one.

## Three Corrections Made During Authoring

**Content parity was broken in `005`.** The study was drafted with four pieces while the other four
carried five — the same failure `CONS-S07-003` had. The missing piece was restored and the row
widened from three tracks to four. This is the second time content parity has slipped in a study that
deliberately gives one item a larger position, which is worth watching for.

**A class name confused the collision checker.** `CONS-S13-004` fingerprinted as `HEAD2>PANEL>PANEL`
— no list at all — because the checker matches `/argue|challenge|claim|panel|.../` against a class
name *before* it tests whether the element is a `ul`, and the list was called `claims`. Renamed to
`cards`, and the fingerprint is now the accurate `HEAD2>LIST5>PANEL`. **The composition was never
wrong; the tool was reading the name rather than the structure.**

**A status pill wrapped to four lines in `004`.** The revised status carried its full reason inside a
`border-radius: 999px` chip, which is fine in the wide cards of `001` and unusable in the narrow
five-across row here. Split into a short pill plus a note line beneath.

## The Remaining Near Match, And Why It Is Not Being Chased

The check reports `S01` and `S13` at 80% at variant 004 — `HEAD2>PANEL` against `HEAD2>LIST5>PANEL`.

**This is an artefact of `S01`'s two-token fingerprint**, not a resemblance. Any three-token sequence
containing `HEAD2` and `PANEL` scores 0.8 against it, and the same pair already appears in the
standing list as `S01` and `S06` at this variant. Visually the two are a full-bleed hero and a
five-across claim row above a dark invitation panel; they share nothing.

Recorded rather than fixed, because restructuring a sound composition to satisfy a similarity metric
on the shortest fingerprint in the sector would be the tool driving the design.
