How we approach it

A framework is a claim to already know the shape of your problem.

So we arrive without one. No named model, no trademark, no pyramid — five questions instead, and we ask them before anything gets drawn.

  1. One

    What decision is this actually for?

    Most engagements are commissioned without one. If no decision is waiting, there is no engagement, and we would rather establish that in the first hour.

  2. Two

    Who has already made it?

    Sometimes it is made and the work is meant to support it. That is a legitimate job, but it is a different one, and it is better said in week one than discovered in week six.

  3. Three

    What would change your mind?

    If nothing would, the evidence stage is theatre and we will say so.

  4. Four

    What happens if you do nothing?

    The most common alternative, and the one nobody costs.

  5. Five

    Who has to live with it afterwards?

    The people in the room are rarely the people who operate the result.

We will draw you a diagram.

After these, not before — and it will be a picture of your problem rather than a model we brought with us.
