## The Section Role

The long-form work, and what it costs to get it.

## The Trap

The gated download is the most transactional and most resented pattern in this sector: a form asking
for name, email, company, phone number, job title and company size in exchange for a PDF, with the
word **free** printed above it.

It is resented because it is untrue rather than because it is expensive. The file is not free — it
costs an address, and then whatever follows the address: a call, a list, a score in a system the
reader cannot see.

## The Governing Idea

> **A gate is a price. Here is what each one costs.**

So every report on this page carries its price in plain words, and the section is arranged around
that price rather than around the material.

## The Four Reports And Their Prices

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| Report | What it is | Price | What the price means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The decision-rights audit | A method for finding who actually decides | **No gate** | The file is a link. Nothing is collected |
| What cost programmes cost | Six patterns of savings that came back | **No gate** | The file is a link. Nothing is collected |
| The stage-03 exits | What we found in engagements the client stopped | **One email address** | You get the file, and one message when the next one is published |
| The counterfactual problem | Why nobody can price consulting by outcome | **A conversation** | We do not send this one. It is useless without the context, so we walk you through it |

**Two of the four cost nothing**, which is what makes the price on the other two credible. A page
where everything is gated is a page where the word *price* means nothing.

The fourth is not a sales tactic dressed as generosity. Some material is useless without the
interpretation, and posting it as a file would be a worse deception than gating it — so the section
says which one and why rather than quietly omitting it. Its subject is also the argument `S08` and
`S12` already rest on, which is why it is the one that needs a conversation.

## The Two Commitments That Make The Price Real

A price nobody can check is a slogan. So every study carries both of these:

**The six fields never asked for** — the ones every gated form in this sector collects:

> Phone number · Job title · Company size · Budget · Timeline · How you heard about us

**What happens to an address once given:**

> One list. Not sold, not enriched, not scored. Unsubscribing removes it rather than pausing it.

Naming the fields is more useful than a privacy assurance, because a reader can check a list of six
things against the form in front of them and cannot check a promise.

## What `003` Found By Sorting

Every reports section in this sector is ordered by date, or by what the firm most wants read. `003`
is ordered by **what it costs the reader**, cheapest first, and says so above the row.

That is the one sort order no firm uses, because it puts the ungated material at the front and the
lead-generating material last. A list sorted by publication date tells a reader nothing they can act
on; a list sorted by price puts the two that cost nothing where a reader deciding whether to bother
will actually reach them.

The single reserved cover goes to the report at the front of that sort — **the media plan agreeing
with the sort** rather than with the commercial value.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

The conversion device in a reports section *is* the gate. Every one of them trades a file for an
address, and the design work goes into making the trade feel generous.

This study converts the other way. **The two reports that cost nothing are the largest things on the
page**, filled in the accent, with the two that cost something beneath them at half the size.

> A reader who has already taken something without being charged for it is a reader who believes the
> next price when they read it.

So giving the ungated material the best position is what makes the gated one credible. The section
spends its most valuable space on the thing it earns nothing from, and says so in the panel.

## The Media Question

The honest image is the same one `S13` established: **the report's own cover.** Never a stock
photograph, never a mood shot of a laptop.

`001` and `004` carry four each and build on them. `003` carries one. `002` and `005` carry none, and
for the same stated reason in both: **a cover is a picture of the product, and the argument in those
two studies is about the terms.**

## The Badge Over Media, Used For The First Time

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` lists *badge over media* among the devices to build with, and it
had not been used anywhere in this sector across thirteen sections. It belongs here: the price is the
first thing a reader wants and the last thing these sections normally show, so in `001` it sits **on**
the cover rather than under the title.

## What Was Drafted And Rejected

**Drawn empty input boxes in `005`.** The obvious move for *the form that does not exist*, and
dropped: it would read as an actual form, which is confusing rather than pointed, and it would import
a UI idiom into a section that has none. The fields are set as type, which is what they are — six
things nobody will be typing.

## Placeholder Demo Data

**The four report titles** are placeholder demo values marked `data-placeholder="true"` in every
study — the firm's own published output, the same line that applies in `S13`.

Prices, the never-asked field list and the address policy are **not** placeholder data. They are
statements about the firm's own terms.

Verified absent across the batch: percentages, currency figures, survey figures, respondent counts,
page counts, download counts, *exclusive* claims, company suffixes and the phrase family *"leading
global"* / *"world-class"*.

**On the word *free*:** a scan flags it in `001` and `002`, and both hits are the sentence *"Nothing
here is described as free"* — the disclaimer rather than the claim. The scan cannot tell the
difference; a reader can.

## The Correction, And The Pattern It Belongs To

**The price badges in `001` were unreadable.** `.cover > span` set the slot label's colour and font
size, and the badge is also a direct `span` child of the cover — so the more specific rule won and
painted the badge dark-on-blue at label size. Scoped to `.cover > span:not(.price)`.

This is the **third cascade collision of the same family** in three batches: `S12-001` had
`.pays span` swallowing a nested placeholder span, `S13-004` had a class name the collision checker
matched before it checked the tag, and now this. All three were invisible in the markup and only
appeared on screen.

The pattern is worth stating: **a rule written for the common child of a container will find the
uncommon one too.** Rendering catches it; reading does not.
