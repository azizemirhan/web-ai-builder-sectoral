## Full Capacity — What Changed In This Batch

The instruction for this batch was explicit: **if a section role requires certain elements, include
them. Sections should be at full capacity, and still look modern.**

That is a correction to how the text budget had been applied. `S02`–`S04` were trimmed hard to sit
inside the 90–170 band, which was right for those roles. A process section is not that kind of role:
it carries **structured fields**, and cutting one to hit a number produces a section that looks tidy
and cannot be used.

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` now carries a **structured content band of 170–230** and the rule
behind it — *the band measures prose, not completeness*. Every stage in every study here carries all
four fields a real firm's version would carry:

| Field | Every study |
| --- | --- |
| What happens in the stage | yes |
| What you get — the artefact | yes |
| The exit — what you keep if you stop here | yes |
| How long it runs | yes, as placeholder demo data |

## The Section Role

How an engagement actually runs, stage by stage.

## The Governing Constraint — Every Five-Box Process Is The Same Five Boxes

Discover, Analyse, Design, Implement, Review. This is the most interchangeable section in
consulting, and a reader learns nothing from it because nothing in it could have been different.

> **A process is only credible if it says where you can get off.**
> Every stage names its exit: stop here, and this is what you keep. A process with no stated exit is
> a sales ladder drawn as a diagram.

| # | Stage | What happens | You get | Stop here and |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Frame | What is actually being decided, and by whom | A one-page decision statement | You keep the decision written down |
| 02 | Evidence | The work that makes an answer arguable | The model and the interviews, assumptions visible | You keep the evidence base, and can re-run it without us |
| 03 | Options | Two or three real choices, costed against each other | Options, with what each one forecloses | You keep the options, and the right to pick none |
| 04 | Decision | The room where somebody chooses | The recommendation, with the dissent recorded | You keep a paper your board can defend |
| 05 | Delivery | Standing it up, or handing it to whoever will | Named owners and the first ninety days | This is the end — there is no sixth stage to buy |

**Why the exits are not fabrication:** they are policy commitments a firm can honour, the same
category as the limits in `S03`. None is a claim about past performance.

Every study also closes on the same line — *most engagements stop at 03, and that is a result rather
than a failure.* A firm willing to print that is a firm a buyer can start with.

## Still No Diagram

A process section is where this sector reaches for a flow chart, a funnel, a maturity ladder or five
boxes with arrows between them. All of those are ruled out in the theme contract. **None of the five
studies contains a connector, a spine or an arrow between stages.** The sequence is carried by
numbering and by the order of the items, which is all a sequence needs — and in `005` by the rail
itself, where the reader's gesture is the same shape as the content.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — five alternating rows with no card background and no spine. Separation is air, an
oversized accent numeral and two tinted fact blocks. The exit block is tinted in the accent so the
eye finds it before the artefact.

**`002`** — the plates interrupt the column rather than heading it: a wide one after the statement,
a narrower one offset right between stage 03 and 04. That is what an editorial spread does with a
photograph. The facts are pills rather than a label column.

**`003`** — a sticky index carrying the whole shape of the engagement in one glance, with durations,
beside five panels carrying what happens inside it. The duration lives in the index rather than in
the panel so the reader can see the engagement's length without reading five panels first.

**`004`** — the exit is the largest type in every card, because the generic process section converts
badly: five boxes read as a commitment ladder where buying box one appears to commit you to box five.
A buyer who can see where they may stop is a buyer who can start.

**`005`** — the rail is the timeline. A process is a sequence and a rail is a sequence you move
through one step at a time. The only study in this batch with script: **~10 lines of vanilla
JavaScript** step the rail by one card; native scroll, touch and trackpad work without it.

## V3 — Media Reduction Pass

Reviewed against the assembled page rather than the section, and rejected:

> Not every section should have an image area either.

The count made the case. **Twenty-four of the catalog's twenty-five studies carried a reserved image
area.** Shape C existed in the theme contract as a rule and almost never in practice, because the
rule was written as "at least one C in any run of six" — satisfiable by ignoring it. It is now per
variant: read down a variant's five sections, and if none is C, one of them is wrong.

A second rule was added with it, and it is the one that decides these cases:

> **A section is not entitled to media just because it could have some.** Ask what the photograph is
> *of*. If the honest answer is "something related to this topic", there is no photograph — there is
> a grey rectangle standing in for one, and the section is better built from type and colour.

That test is decisive for abstractions. There is no honest photograph of *quantitative modelling*,
of *financial services*, or of a process stage called *Options*: only an illustration, and an
illustration of an abstraction is a stock photograph — the first thing this sector's anti-patterns
rule out.

### What changed here

`CONS-S05-005` moved from its previous shape to **C — type and colour**, each rail card now opens on a colour field carrying the stage numeral at display size, where the image was.
