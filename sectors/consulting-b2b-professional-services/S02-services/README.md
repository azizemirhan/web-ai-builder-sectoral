# Section Role

What the firm sells — and, the part that decides whether the section works, **whether the reader can
find themselves in it.** This section answers the first of the sector's four questions: *do you
understand my problem?*

# The Governing Constraint — A Capability List Is Not A Services Section

    A capability list tells the buyer what the firm sells.
    A situation list tells them whether the firm has met their problem.

The default is six nouns — *Digital Transformation*, *Operational Excellence*, *Growth Strategy* —
and it fails for a specific reason: **it asks the buyer to translate their problem into the firm's
vocabulary before they are allowed to proceed.** They cannot do that reliably, so they pick wrong or
leave.

So every study leads with the situation and demotes the discipline to a label.

# The Six Situations

| Situation | Discipline |
| --- | --- |
| The market moved and the plan did not. | Strategy and positioning |
| The strategy is agreed and nothing has changed. | Operating model |
| It costs more to run than it should. | Performance and cost |
| Growth stalled and the reasons contradict each other. | Growth and commercial |
| The programme is late and reporting says it is fine. | Transformation delivery |
| A decision is coming and the evidence is thin. | Diligence and decision support |

All five studies carry the same six, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

# The Reader Who Fits None Of Them

The most valuable reader in a services section is the one who cannot place themselves, because they
are the most likely to leave and the cheapest to keep. Every study gives them somewhere to go rather
than nothing — explicitly as its own route in `004`, and as the foot question in `001`.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No 2×2 matrix, quadrant chart, pyramid, funnel or maturity ladder.** The sector's signature
  ornament, ruled out in `../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md`. Where `003` needs a division it uses one
  real axis — is the problem the decision or the running — rather than two invented ones.
- **No jargon compounds.** If a discipline name cannot be said plainly, it is the wrong name.
- **No invented proof.** No engagement counts per service, no named clients, no logos, no awards.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
