## The Section Role

Where the firm actually is.

## The Trap

Three reflexes, and all three are unavailable here.

**The map.** An embedded one is a remote runtime dependency, which this catalog forbids outright. A
drawn one is worse: a world map with four pins is a picture of a network, and **four pins is not a
network.**

**The skyline.** Glass towers and city skylines at dusk are named in the anti-patterns in
`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md`. This is the section that reaches for them hardest.

**The inflation.** A registered address becomes an office, a person who lives somewhere becomes a
presence, and twelve cities become a global footprint.

## The Governing Idea

> **An address is not an office.**

Most locations lists are a count of cities, and most of those cities are one of four things: a floor
with people on it, somebody who lives there, a registered address nobody sits at, or a desk borrowed
inside a client's building.

Printing **which is which** is unheard of, and it is the only version of this section a reader can
check.

## The Four Entries

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| Kind | City | What is actually there | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The office** | `London` | A floor with `18` desks. The room of twelve is this room | Meet us here |
| **No office** | `Manchester` | `Three` of us live here and work from home | We will meet you in the city, and say where when you ask |
| **Registered address** | `Frankfurt` | Nobody sits here. It exists so we can invoice in euros | Post something to it. That is all |
| **Borrowed desk** | `Singapore` | Inside a client's building, for one engagement. Not ours | Nothing — it comes off this page when the engagement ends |

**One of the four answers is *nothing*,** and it is printed at the same size as the rest.

## The Entry With An Expiry

The fourth comes off this page when the engagement ends, and the removal policy is printed next to
the thing it will remove.

A locations list that only ever grows is a list nobody maintains — which is why the closing line in
every study is:

> **We do not list a city because someone flew there. This page loses an entry when a place stops
> being one.**

That is the same discipline `S10` applies to its logo wall, where `two` marks came off at the last
review. Applied here it is stronger, because a city is easier to leave on a page than a logo.

## Why Only One Image, In Four Of Five Studies

**Three of the four entries cannot honestly be photographed at all.** There is no office in the
second, only a door in the third, and the fourth sits inside a client's building — which `S07`'s
confidentiality rule puts out of reach.

So the maximum honest media count for this section is **one**, and the asymmetry is left visible
rather than evened out with stock photography. In `001` three of the four cards simply have no image
above them, and the unevenness in the grid is the argument.

`002` carries none at all, for a different reason: its subject is the taxonomy rather than the
premises, and a photograph of the one real floor would turn the section into an office tour — quietly
re-establishing the thing the copy argues against, that a firm is its buildings.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

A reader in a locations section is asking one question: **are you near me?** Every other firm answers
it by inflating the list.

This study answers a different question, on the record.

> **Proximity is not a service.**

What a client needs is people in the room on the days that matter, and that number is knowable before
signing and belongs in the proposal. A firm with an office in your city that sends nobody is worse
than one three hours away that turns up for every stage gate. So the action is not *find your nearest
office* — it is **tell us where the work is, and we will tell you how many days we would be there.**

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading. The four kinds are named in a line first, then four cards across a row.
Only the first has an image, and the three empty card-tops beside it do the work a paragraph would.

**`002`** — **shape C.** The four kinds read left to right as a taxonomy, each set in the accent with
the city as the caption beneath it. A reader scanning the page takes away *the office · no office ·
registered address · borrowed desk* rather than four city names.

**`003`** — **sorted by how much of a place each one is**, descending, with the arithmetic stated
above the row. The section gets weaker as it goes, which is the opposite of how a locations list is
built and the only order in which a reader can tell where the substance stops. The first module takes
half the row and carries the only floor.

**`004`** — **the premise refused.** See above.

**`005`** — **one place enormous, the other three a line each.** The floor takes almost the whole
section; the people with no office, the registered address and the borrowed desk sit underneath in a
single strip at caption size. **Not the same move as `S13-005`**, which hands one item the flagship
position to make a point about *which* item deserves attention. This one is not choosing a favourite,
it is rendering a real ratio: one of four is a place.

## Placeholder Demo Data

**The city names, the desk count and the headcount** are placeholder demo values marked
`data-placeholder="true"` — the firm's own premises and its own people.

The kinds, what is at each address and the removal policy are **not** placeholder data. They are
statements about the firm's own arrangements.

Verified absent across the batch: maps, iframes, pins, skylines, *global presence*, country counts,
square footage, telephone numbers, percentages, currency figures and rankings.

**On the phone-number scan:** it flags all five, and every hit is the string `17-001` … `17-005`
inside `data-study-id`. The same class of false positive as *free* in `S14` and *hurry* in `S15`.

## Corrections

**Two structural echoes, both fixed.** The first drafts collided with the two nearest batches:

- `S17-003` against `S14-003` at 80% — both were *declared line → wide band → four modules → dark
  policy bar*. Genuinely the same composition with different content. The band was removed and the
  floor moved **inside** the first module, which now takes half the row; the sort argument is
  stronger for it.
- `S17-002` against `S13-002` at 89% — both a two-column editorial run closing on a panel. The run
  was changed to **four across**, which suits the taxonomy better than a stacked column did.

A third, `S17-001` against `S14-001` at 86%, was cleared by adding the line that names the four kinds
before the row — which the safe variant was missing anyway.

**Content parity slipped in `004`.** It was drafted without the *what you can do there* line the
other four carry. Restored. That is now the fourth batch running in which the compressed variant lost
a field rather than only size; in `S13`, `S14` and `S15` it was the art-directed `005`, and here it
was the conversion-led `004`. **The pattern is not specific to `005` after all — it is specific to
whichever study compresses its items**, and that is the one to check first.

**A responsive ladder broke while patching.** The fix that widened `003`'s grid also matched inside
its `980px` block, leaving a two-column grid whose first child spanned three. Rewritten to three
tracks with the first spanning all three. Found by reading the ladder after the patch, not by the
render — a reminder that a search-and-replace across a stylesheet reaches into the media queries too.

**A pill wrapped in `003`.** *Registered address* broke to two lines in the narrow column and pushed
that card's title out of line with its neighbours. Font size reduced and `white-space: nowrap` added.
