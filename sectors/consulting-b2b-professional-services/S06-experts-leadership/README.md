# The Governing Constraint — A Headshot Answers A Question Nobody Asked

Every consulting leadership section is a grid of faces and job titles. The problem is not that the
format is tired; it is that **it answers the wrong question.**

> **Will the person in the pitch be the person on the work?**
> Senior partners sell; juniors deliver. A photograph cannot answer that. Only a commitment can.

# The Five, At Full Capacity

Every person in every study carries four fields: name, role, what they are in the room for, and the
commitment — which stages of `S05` they are actually on, **including where they are not.**

| Role | In the room for | On your engagement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Managing partner | The decision itself, and the argument with your board | **Stages 01 and 04. Not the middle.** |
| Partner, operating model | Whether the decision survives the organisation | All five stages, named in the contract |
| Director, quantitative | The model, and every assumption inside it | Stages 02 and 03, full time |
| Director, research | The interviews, and what the market actually said | Stage 02, and the readout |
| Principal, delivery | The first ninety days after you decide | Stage 05, and only if you buy it |

The managing partner's line is what makes the rest believable. Every firm implies its most senior
name is on everything; **"not the middle"** is the only version of that claim a buyer can check.

# Names And Portraits

Recorded under *The firm's own people* in `../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md`:

- **The firm's own team may carry placeholder names**, marked `data-placeholder="true"`. An initial
  and a surname, so they read as placeholders rather than as people.
- **Third-party people may not** — named clients, testimonial attributions, quoted analysts,
  referees. The difference is who the invention is about.
- **The portrait stays reserved.** A name can be placeheld; a face cannot. Every slot label names the
  **role**, never the placeholder person.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No credential ladder.** No qualifications, universities, prior employers, certifications or
  years-of-experience counters. All are either unverifiable here or a third-party claim.
- **No "meet the team" copy** with nothing under it. If a card cannot say what that person decides,
  the card is the problem.
- **No percentage of time, utilisation figure or availability score.** The stage strip in `004` is a
  policy statement rendered visually: a block is on or off, and never a measurement.
- **No social links or contact details per person.** `S18` owns contact.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
