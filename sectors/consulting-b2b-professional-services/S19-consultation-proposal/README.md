# Section Role

What the first conversation is, and what the document it produces contains.

# The Trap

**"Free consultation, no obligation."** `S14` established why that is untrue: a gate is a price. An
hour of somebody who can sign is the most expensive thing in the process, and calling it free
misprices the only scarce resource in the room.

The second failure is quieter: **nobody says what is in the proposal.** It is the only document the
buyer will be asked to sign, and every section in this sector describes the meeting that produces it
while leaving the document a mystery until it lands.

# The Governing Idea

> **A proposal is the only document you will be asked to sign, and nobody tells you what is in it
> first.**

So this section prints **its contents page before the proposal exists.**

# The Six Sections

| # | Section | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | **The decision** | The one it exists to serve, in your words. Without this line there is no proposal |
| 02 | **The stages** | What happens in each, and where you may stop |
| 03 | **The people** | Named, with the stages they are not on |
| 04 | **The fee model** | Which of the four, and what it pays us to do |
| 05 | **What we would not do** | The boundary, so the scope argument happens now rather than in week six |
| 06 | **What would make us wrong** | The assumption it rests on, written down so you can test it |

All five studies carry the same six. Every entry resolves into the section that owns it — `S11`'s
first question, `S05`'s stages and exits, `S06`'s people, `S12`'s four models. **The proposal is the
assembled page, folded into nine pages.**

# The Two Nobody Writes

`05` and `06` are the argument, and every study marks them. **`06` is the harder one:** `S13` applies
the falsifiability standard to what the firm has published; this applies it to what the firm is about
to be paid for.

# What The Hour Costs

- **One hour of yours.** We do not call it free.
- **Nothing else.** The five questions and whatever we draw stay with you either way.
- **It can end in a no from us.** Roughly one in `four` does.

# Nine Pages

> **`Nine` pages, and it has been nine for years. A proposal that needs thirty is describing work
> nobody has framed.**

# The Media Question

`S13` established that the firm's own published output is a legitimate reserved area. A proposal is
the firm's own output too, so three studies reserve **the proposal, open at the page that names the
decision** — a photograph of a real artefact, not a document metaphor.

`002` and `005` carry none: a photograph of a document sells the object, and the argument is about
the contents.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No *free consultation*, no *no obligation*.** The hour is named as a cost.
- **No turnaround promise.** *Your proposal in 48 hours* is unverifiable, like the response-time
  promise `S18` refuses.
- **No form, no multi-step wizard, no booking widget.** This section's version of `S18`'s form.
- **No proposal without section 05 and section 06.** The boundary and the assumption are what make
  the other four worth reading.
- **No document metaphor.** The reserved area photographs the artefact; nothing borrows memo,
  letterhead or contract styling.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
