## The Section Role

What the first conversation is, and what the document it produces contains.

## The Trap

**"Free consultation, no obligation."**

`S14` already established why that is untrue: a gate is a price. An hour of somebody who can sign is
the most expensive thing in the whole process, and calling it free misprices the only scarce resource
in the room.

The second failure is quieter and worse: **nobody says what is in the proposal.** It is the only
document the buyer will be asked to sign, and every section in this sector describes the *meeting*
that produces it while leaving the document itself a mystery until it lands.

## The Governing Idea

> **A proposal is the only document you will be asked to sign, and nobody tells you what is in it
> first.**

So this section prints **its contents page before the proposal exists.**

## The Six Sections

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| # | Section | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | **The decision** | The one it exists to serve, in your words. If we cannot write this line, there is no proposal |
| 02 | **The stages** | What happens in each, and where you may stop |
| 03 | **The people** | Named, with the stages they are not on |
| 04 | **The fee model** | Which of the four, and what it pays us to do |
| 05 | **What we would not do** | The boundary, so the scope argument happens now rather than in week six |
| 06 | **What would make us wrong** | The assumption it rests on, written down so you can test it |

Every entry resolves into the section that owns it: `S11`'s first question, `S05`'s stages and exits,
`S06`'s people, `S12`'s four models. **The proposal is the assembled page, folded into nine pages.**

## The Two Nobody Writes

`05` and `06` are the batch's argument, and every study marks them — held in the accent in `001`,
promoted to a wide pair in `003`, tinted in `004`, coloured in `002`, and in `005` the sixth is the
only variation on the page.

**`06` is the harder one.** *What would make us wrong* is the assumption the proposal rests on,
written down so the client can test it. `S13` applies that standard to what the firm has published;
this applies it to what the firm is about to be paid for, which is the half nobody volunteers.

## What The Hour Costs

Three lines, in every study:

- **One hour of yours.** We do not call it free. An hour of somebody who can sign is the most
  expensive thing here.
- **Nothing else.** The five questions and whatever we draw stay with you either way.
- **It can end in a no from us.** Roughly one in `four` ends with us saying we are not the right
  firm.

The third is the same position `S12` takes on the models it will not sell, and it is what makes the
other three conversations worth having.

## Nine Pages

> **`Nine` pages, and it has been nine for years. A proposal that needs thirty is describing work
> nobody has framed.**

A length is a claim about clarity. Printing it is the cheapest honest signal in the section.

## The Media Question

`S13` established that the firm's own published output is a legitimate reserved area — the opening
spread of a piece. **A proposal is the firm's own output too**, so three studies reserve one:

> **The proposal, open at the page that names the decision.**

This is a photograph of a real artefact, **not a document metaphor**: no memo styling, no letterhead,
no signature block, no monospace anywhere in the batch.

`002` and `005` carry none, for the same stated reason: **a photograph of a document sells the
object, and the argument is about the contents.** A reader who sees a handsome bound proposal has
been told it is well made, which is not the claim.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

The standard sequence is **pitch, then proposal, then contract** — the reader is asked to want the
thing before being told what it costs or contains. Every consultation section in this sector runs it
that way, with the booking button at the top and the terms nowhere.

**`004` reverses the order.** What the hour costs, what the document contains, and the fact that the
firm may say no all come first. The invitation is the last line, at the smallest size in the study,
because a reader who has read the terms does not need to be sold the meeting.

> Reordering costs the firm the enquiries it would have won on enthusiasm, and keeps the ones it can
> serve.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading. The document, the page count, then the six sections on three tracks
with `05` and `06` filled in the accent, closing on the hour in a dashed block.

**`002`** — **shape C, and the document deliberately dropped.** Six section names set at the size a
magazine sets a subhead, two columns, `05` and `06` in the accent.

**`003`** — **sorted by rarity rather than by order.** The two nobody writes go first, at double
width; the four every proposal has sit beneath, smaller, because they are table stakes rather than an
argument. The image is at the foot rather than the head, because a reader who sees the object first
judges the binding.

**`004`** — **the terms before the pitch.** See above.

**`005`** — **six names at one size, and one colour change.** Every other `005` in this sector varies
something structural: `S09` type scale, `S11` indent, `S13` flagship position, `S15` column width,
`S16` a paired offset, `S17` area, `S18` making the deliverable the artwork. This one varies
**nothing except colour** — all six entries identical, in one grid, at one size, and the sixth is
violet.

That works because the content earns it. **A contents page where every entry looks the same is what a
contents page is**, and a single colour change says *this is the one nobody else writes* without
adding a badge, a rule or a size jump to say it.

`S18`. Every one is the study saying it does not do the thing.
