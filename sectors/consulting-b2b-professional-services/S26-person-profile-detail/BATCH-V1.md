## The Section Role

One person: who they are, what they work on, and how to reach them.

## The Trap

A consultant profile is a CV — firms, years, degrees, sectors served — wrapped around a headshot. The
README forbids inventing any of it: no fabricated qualifications, registrations, memberships, awards
or credentials, and no invented handles. That constraint is usually treated as a limitation. It is
the design brief: **when you cannot list credentials, what is actually left that a buyer can use?**

## The Governing Idea

> **You are not choosing a person. You are choosing how much of them you get.**

`S06` named the sector's oldest complaint — senior partners sell, juniors deliver — and answered it
with a commitment naming where each person is *not*. A profile page is where that gets specific,
because the buyer is now looking at one individual and the only question left is **how much of them
there is.**

So every study carries **the load**:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **`Two` at a time** | Never three, which is why the answer is sometimes no |
| **`Two` running now** | One of them in its last month |
| **`Three` days a week, live** | The other two are for the pieces and the reference calls |

These are the firm's own countable facts, checkable by asking, and they are the only numbers on a
profile page that mean anything.

## What Is Deliberately Not Here

The README says nothing in this role may be invented and that a placeholder study must leave
credentials as reserved fields rather than fill them. **These studies do neither: they say the
credentials are not listed, and why.**

> **No qualifications, memberships or awards are listed here.** Ask, and we will send what is
> verifiable.

That is the same move `S17` makes about an address and `S20` about the reasons to leave. A profile
with a credential list is asking to be trusted on a claim the reader cannot check from the page; a
profile that says *ask* is offering the check instead.

## Names, Portraits And Pronouns

Per `S06` and the theme contract:

- **The firm's own people may carry placeholder names** in the form initial-and-surname, so
  `A. Whitfield` and `R. Adeyemi` are marked `data-placeholder="true"`.
- **The portrait stays reserved, and its label names the role** — *the partner, operating model* —
  never the placeholder person, so no study presents a fabricated individual as a real one. A scan
  confirms no portrait label carries a name.
- **Neither person is given a pronoun the studies have no basis for.** Every reference is they/them,
  and a scan confirms zero gendered pronouns across the batch. On a page about a placeholder
  individual, inventing a gender is the same class of error as inventing a credential.

Two of five reserve a portrait; three carry none. The README makes the slot optional and requires the
page to read completely without it — a batch where every study reserved one would not have tested that.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — identity and portrait paired, the load as three display numerals with no boxes, the
  attachments across, and the route at the foot.
- **`002`** — every profile page sets the name at display size above a job title in grey. That order
  is wrong for the reader: a buyer arriving here was sent **by role**, and the name is how they will
  refer to that role afterwards. So the headline is *Partner, operating model* and the name is the
  caption. The page says so in a line.
- **`003`** — *on all five stages* is exactly the sentence every firm writes about its most senior
  name, and the buyer cannot price it. So the commitment breaks across `S05`'s five stages and says
  how much of this person each one gets — full time in framing, two days a week in evidence, in the
  room for the decision, alongside somebody else for the first ninety days. **A commitment that does
  not vary by stage was not a commitment.**
- **`004`** — every profile answers the seniority complaint with adjectives, which is the one form of
  answer that cannot be wrong and therefore cannot be checked. This page hands over **three checks the
  reader can run**: read the contract, ask on the reference call, ask what else is running. Each one
  the firm loses if it is lying. **A commitment a buyer can falsify is worth more than one they have
  to believe.**
- **`005`** — **there is no career history on this page.** A career is a list of rooms somebody was
  in, and none of it tells you how they think. What replaces it is a revision record: one position
  that still stands, and one they changed their mind about in public with the piece left up.

`005` is the sector-native reading because `S11` asks the client *what would change your mind?*, and a
firm that asks that and publishes no answer of its own is asking a question it has not passed. `S13`
established the status field for the firm and `S25` gave the retracted piece its own page; this makes
it personal, which is the only place it becomes a hiring criterion.

### Not `S24-004`'s move

`S24-004` asks whether a case study is evidence *about you*. `S26-004` asks whether a commitment is
true *at all*. One bounds the relevance of evidence; the other hands over the means of verification.
