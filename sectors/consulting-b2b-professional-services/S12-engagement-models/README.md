# Section Role

How the firm can be hired, and what each arrangement does to its own behaviour.

# The Trap

The default here is a three-tier pricing table — Bronze / Silver / Gold, a tick matrix, a *most
popular* badge. Every part of it is unavailable:

- **A tick matrix is a table**, ruled out with the rest of the document idiom.
- **Tiers imply that more money buys a better firm.** In consulting the same people do the work under
  every arrangement, so a tier ladder is a claim the firm cannot make.
- **`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` omits currency figures entirely.** There is no price in any
  study.

So the section has to be useful without a number, a tier or a comparison grid.

# The Governing Idea

> **Every fee model pays us to do something. Read the one that pays us to do the wrong thing.**

None of these incentives is hidden or shameful — they are arithmetic. But a buyer is almost never
shown them, and the misalignment is usually what decides how an engagement ends.

# The Four Models

| Model | Suits | Pays us to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed fee** | A question whose shape is already known | **Finish early.** If the question changes in week three, the scope becomes the argument |
| **Time and materials** | A problem nobody has framed yet | **Take longer.** The defence is a weekly stop-or-continue, written into the contract |
| **Retainer** | A decision that returns every quarter | **Still be here.** Capped at `six` months, then you re-sign |
| **Secondment** | Wanting the capability rather than the answer | **Be agreeable.** Someone inside your organisation stops saying the unwelcome thing |

All five studies carry the same four, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy. Each
defence is a real mechanism: the weekly stop-or-continue is the same exit `S05` sells, and the
retainer cap is a policy with a date on it.

# The Declined Model

> **Outcome-based fees. We do not offer them.**
>
> Paying on outcome requires knowing what would have happened otherwise, and nobody runs the company
> twice. A fee resting on a number neither of us can produce is a dispute with a date on it.

It is the most requested commercial model in the sector, so refusing it costs something. The reason
is **the position `S08` already took about the firm's own metrics, applied to its own fees** — the
metrics section's epistemics decide this section's terms.

# What Sorting Them Reveals

`003` sorts the models by who absorbs it when the work takes longer. **One puts that risk on the
firm. Three put it on the client.** And the one that carries risk for the firm only works when the
question is already clear:

> **The safest model is available on the easiest problems.**

# The Media Question

A fee model has nothing to photograph, and a photograph of a contract would be the document metaphor
the contract bans. What the section does describe is one recurring physical thing:

> **The weekly meeting where the engagement is stopped or continued.**

Three studies carry it; two carry none.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No price, day rate or currency figure.** Sector-wide.
- **No tiers.** No Bronze / Silver / Gold, no Starter / Enterprise, no *most popular* badge.
- **No tick matrix or comparison grid.** It is a table whatever the corner radius.
- **No model without its misaligned incentive.** Listing what a model suits and omitting what it pays
  the firm to do is the omission this section exists to correct.
- **No outcome-based fee.** See above.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
