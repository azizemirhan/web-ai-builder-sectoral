## The Section Role

What the firm is in a position to count.

## The Problem This Section Has, Which No Other Section In The Sector Has

Every rule this catalog has written forbids the ordinary contents of an outcomes section. Value
delivered, ROI multiples, savings, retention and satisfaction scores are all excluded in
`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` under *What May Not Be Invented*.

That looks like a section with nothing left to say. It is not. It is a section with an argument to
make, and the argument is stronger than the numbers it replaces.

> **Consulting cannot measure its own effect, because the counterfactual does not exist. Nobody runs
> the company twice.**

A firm publishing *"£2.4bn of value delivered"* is claiming to know what would have happened
otherwise. It did not run the company twice, so it does not know. Doubting the figure is not
conservatism, it is arithmetic.

So the section **counts the work, not the effect.**

## The Five Counted Figures

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| Figure | What it is | Source | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `340` | Engagements completed | Our record | Whether any of them worked |
| `212` | Decisions taken in the room | Our record | That the decision was right |
| `4,180` | Interviews conducted | Our record | Nothing — it is the unglamorous one, and it predicts the rest |
| `61 of 84` | Models still in use a year later | **The client** | Only that the client kept using it, or stopped answering |
| `47` | Engagements the client ended early | Our record | Printed because stopping early is a result |

Four of the five are the firm's own record of its own activity, which is the only thing it is in a
position to count. The fifth is sourced to the client and labelled as such. **Every study carries a
source chip on every figure**, because a count without a source is an assertion.

`340` matches the counter in `CONS-S01`, so the assembled page is internally consistent.

## The Fifth Figure Is The Point

`47` engagements the client ended early is the only figure here a competitor would never print. It
stays for two reasons:

1. It is true of any firm that takes stage gates seriously, and `CONS-S05` sold the client the right
   to stop at every stage. **A methodology with exits and an outcomes section with no early exits
   contradict each other.** This figure is what makes `S05` credible.
2. It is the most testable claim on the page.

`004` is built on the second reason — see *The Conversion Insight* below.

## The Missing Number

Every study closes on a **refusal**: the four metrics the firm will not publish, and the reason.

> `Value delivered` · `ROI` · `Savings` · `Satisfaction`
>
> Each would require knowing what would have happened otherwise.

**This is not the same device as the withheld line in `S07`.** That one says *we cannot tell you* — a
confidentiality limit, someone else's secret. This one says *it cannot be known* — an epistemic
limit, nobody's secret. Different reason, different claim, and the two sections sit far enough apart
in every variant that the pair reads as a position rather than a mannerism.

Each study also names what to do instead: **ask the references.** A refusal with no alternative is a
dead end; a refusal that hands over a person to call is a stronger offer than the number would have
been.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

A metrics section normally converts by making the numbers as large as possible. Here every
impressive number is forbidden. So the conversion device is the opposite:

> **The least flattering figure on the page is the one the action points at.**

In `004` the `47` card is the only one filled in the accent, and the call to action reads **"Ask
about the 47."** Any firm can publish a flattering total. A firm that volunteers its early exits and
offers to walk a buyer through them is making a claim the buyer can actually test — **the offer is
being audited**, and that is a thing only a confident seller does.

## The Media Problem

Ask this section's photograph what it is **of**. A number cannot be photographed, and a chart of a
number is not a photograph of anything.

What remains honest is the work the figures came from — the room, the model on a screen, the
interviews. Every slot label in this batch says exactly that, and two studies carry no slot at all:

- `001` and `005` at **zero** reserved media. The numerals are the visual.
- `002`, `003`, `004` at **one** wide artefact area each.

Three slots across five studies. This is the most media-light batch in the sector, by argument
rather than by rhythm.

## Not A Dashboard

A metrics section is one step from a KPI dashboard: gauges, deltas, green and red arrows,
sparklines, a trend line. Every one of those implies a target and a direction of travel, which
implies a measurement system nobody here has — and the technical-document idiom is out under the
theme contract regardless.

**There is no chart anywhere in this batch.** A figure, a label, the sentence saying what it does not
prove, and its source.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — **shape C.** The safe reading, and the only one that gives the figures different weights:
a six-track grid where the first three span two tracks and the last two span three, so `61 of 84` and
`47` get the wider cells. The refusal is an indigo panel closing the section.

**`002`** — no cards. The numerals sit directly on the ground with their labels beneath, the way a
magazine sets a figure it wants read rather than skimmed. Five figures on three columns leaves a hole
in the second row, so **the fifth figure takes the empty width**: `47` at display size with its
reason alongside it, closing the field. What separates this from a stat strip is that every figure
carries the sentence saying what it does not prove.

**`003`** — five equivalent modules on one row. A uniform five-across grid was the wrong answer
everywhere else in this sector because the items were not equal; here they are — five counts, one
source each, no hierarchy to express — and the even row is the honest shape.

**`004`** — **the conversion study.** See above. The `47` card is filled, the refusal panel is
near-black, and the action is the audit invitation.

**`005`** — **shape C, and the ghost.** Every other study states the refusal in a panel of words.
This one *draws* it: the sixth slot in the row exists at the same size as the other five, and holds
`?,???` in outline. An empty slot in a row of full ones says *this cannot be known* faster than a
sentence can. It is typography — a text stroke on a transparent glyph, carrying a visible label and
an `Unknowable` source chip — and **deliberately not a dashed box**, which would read as a reserved
media area and put an image where the argument is.

## Placeholder Demo Data

**Every counted figure** is a placeholder demo value marked `data-placeholder="true"`, as is the
`1998` in the counting-period line. Every study declares `<meta name="placeholder-data">`.

The ghost in `005` is **not** placeholder data. It carries no value and never will — it is not a
field awaiting content, it is the argument.

Labels, reasons, sources and the refusal list are not placeholder data — they are statements about
the firm's own counting and disclosure policy.

logo, testimonial attribution, ranking, award or certification anywhere. `Satisfaction` appears only
in the list of metrics that will **not** be published.

## Drafting Correction

The five studies were first drafted at 125–162 visible words while their comment headers claimed
188–203. The claim was never measured; the other seven sections were, and check out within a few
words.

The gap was not padding-shaped — it was three pieces of missing content, all of which a metrics
section owes the reader:

- **the counting period** — a figure with no period is not a count;
- **the source of each figure** — four are the firm's own record, one is the client's, and the
  difference matters more here than anywhere;
- **what to do instead of the refused metrics** — ask the references.

Added to all five, which brought them to 186–208. The `Density` line in every header now carries the
measured number.
