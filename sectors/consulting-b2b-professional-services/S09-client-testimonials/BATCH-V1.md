## The Section Role

What clients said — and what the reader may do about it.

## The Problem

Testimonials are the least trusted element on any professional-services site, and the reason is
structural rather than aesthetic. The reader cannot verify a sentence in quotation marks, and knows
the firm chose which sentences to print.

The usual fix is an attribution — a name, a job title, a company, a headshot. **That fix is not
available here and would not have worked anyway.** Not available, because
`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` forbids naming clients in every section and forbids inventing a
third party. Would not have worked, because a name the reader cannot ring is decoration.

> **A quote you cannot check is an advertisement.**

So this section does not try to make the quote more convincing. It attaches to every quote **the
thing a reader can actually act on: what they are permitted to do to check it.**

## The Check States

Three, and they are the batch's real content. Each is a statement about the **firm's own disclosure
policy**, which is the firm's to make — not a claim about anybody else.

| State | What it means | How many |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| **Will take a reference call** | That client agreed to speak to a prospective one | 2 |
| **Named privately** | The name is given in the room, never on a website | 2 |
| **Words only** | Permission covers the sentence and nothing else | 1 |

**A ladder where every rung is the top rung is not a ladder.** Two of the five sit below the top
state and one sits at the bottom, because no firm holds reference-call permission across its whole
book, and a page claiming otherwise is a page nobody believes. Printing the weak ones is what makes
the strong ones worth reading.

Every study also says, in words, that the bottom rung is **a stricter legal team, not a lesser
client** — so the descending treatment cannot be read as a judgement about the client.

## The Five Quotes

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| # | Quote opens on | From the situation | Said at | Check |

| 01 | four weeks that felt slow | The strategy is agreed and nothing has changed | Stage 02 · Evidence | Will take a call |
| 02 | advice nobody wanted to hear | The programme is late and reporting says it is fine | Stage 03 · Options | Will take a call |
| 03 | having already used four firms | A decision is coming and the evidence is thin | Stage 01 · Frame | Named privately |
| 04 | having checked whether the pitch team showed up | It costs more to run than it should | Stage 04 · Decision | Named privately |
| 05 | not renewing | The market moved and the plan did not | Stage 05 · Delivery | Words only |

**Every quote opens on a reservation.** A testimonial that opens on praise is indistinguishable from
copy the firm wrote about itself; one that opens on a complaint and continues anyway is the only
kind a buyer finishes. Quote 05 is the strongest thing on the page precisely because the client did
not come back.

The situations are the six from `CONS-S02` and the stages are the five from `CONS-S05`, so a reader
who recognised themselves earlier on the assembled page recognises themselves again here.

## What Is Attached To A Quote, And What Is Not

This needed deciding rather than assuming, and the decision is now recorded in
`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` under *Client quotes*.

**Not attached, anywhere in this batch:** a name, a job title, a role, a company, a sector, an
industry, a headshot, a logo. Not even placeheld. The contract's rule on third-party people is
explicit — *a named client contact, a testimonial attribution, a quoted analyst, a referee: all
still forbidden, because a placeholder version of those is a claim about somebody else* — and a
placeholder job title is a smaller version of the same claim.

**Attached instead:** the situation the quote came out of, the stage it was said at, and the check
state. All three are the firm's own vocabulary or the firm's own policy.

The quote sentences themselves are placeholder demo text, marked and clearable. They carry no
attribution of any kind, so they make no representation about a person or a company.

## Not The Same Device As `S07` Or `S08`

Three consecutive sections could easily have become three variations on *here is what we will not
tell you*. They are not, and the axis is deliberate:

| Section | Says | Kind of limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `S07` Case Studies | *We cannot tell you* | Confidentiality — somebody else's secret |
| `S08` Outcomes & Metrics | *It cannot be known* | Epistemic — nobody's secret |
| `S09` Client Testimonials | **Here is how you check** | **Not a limit at all** |

`S07` and `S08` are refusals. **`S09` is the opposite move** — the point where the firm stops
asserting and hands over the means of verification. It is the payoff of the line `S08` closes on,
*ask the references we will give you*, and the closing panel in every study here is that
introduction being offered.

## One Ladder, Five Channels

## The Media Problem

Ask this section's photograph what it is **of**. It cannot be the client's face — they are anonymous,
and the contract keeps portrait areas reserved regardless. It cannot be their premises or their
product. A stock photograph of a handshake is decoration pretending to be evidence.

What remains honest is **the room a reference call is taken in** — the firm's own place, and the
place the section's offer actually leads. Two studies carry one such area; three carry none.

Two slots across five studies, which is lighter than `S08`. Not a competition for restraint: `S08`
had nothing to photograph because a number cannot be photographed, and this one has nothing to
photograph because the subject is a person who may not be shown.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading. The strongest quote takes the full row at feature size, the other four
run beneath as compacts, and the badge sits at the foot of every card so the ladder is legible
without a key.

**`002`** — **shape C.** No cards, no boxes, no quotation-mark ornament. The sentences sit on the
paper at pull-quote size, and the alternation between the full measure and an indented narrower one
gives the column its rhythm. The small line beneath is not a name — it is the situation, the stage,
and the permission.

**`003`** — **shape C, and the colour system.** A filled module means that client will take a call,
an outlined one means the name is given privately, a flat one means words only. The section is
legible before it is read: the amount of saturation on the grid *is* the amount of evidence on offer.
A key states the three, so the colour is a system rather than a decoration.

**`004`** — **shape C, the conversion study.** The section is sorted by the buyer's actual question,
which is not *what did they say* but *can I speak to one of them*. The two callable clients sit in a
filled band at the top **with the action inside it**, so the eye lands on the strongest available
proof and finds the button already there. Nothing is saved for the end.

**`005`** — **the type size of a quote is the permission it carries.** Callable clients at display
scale, named-privately at half, words-only the smallest thing on the page. The cascade runs on a
diagonal, and it does not read as a ranking because the smallest quote is the least promotional
sentence in the batch — *we did not renew* — which is the opposite of what a firm hiding behind its
type scale would do.

## Contract Amendment This Batch Produced

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` forbade *testimonial attributions* and forbade placeholder names
for third-party people, but never said what a testimonials section may therefore contain. Read
strictly it appeared to forbid the section outright; read loosely it permitted a placeholder job
title, which is the same claim in smaller type.

A new clause, **Client quotes**, now records the line this batch drew: the quote sentence may be
placeholder demo text because unattributed it makes no representation about anyone, and **nothing
identifying may be attached to it — not a name, a role, a title, a company or a sector, placeheld or
otherwise.** What may be attached is the firm's own vocabulary and the firm's own disclosure policy.
