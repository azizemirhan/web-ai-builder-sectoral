## The Section Role

The detail page for one offering, in enough depth that a visitor can decide whether it is theirs.

## The Trap

**A service page that describes the service and not the engagement.** *We help clients redesign their
operating model to unlock value and drive sustainable performance* is the register, and it survives
because nobody has to defend it. What the buyer needs is what the weeks look like: who is in the room,
what lands, what does not, and what it takes from them.

## The Governing Idea

> **The fee is not the price. Your own people's time is, and no service page has ever said how much.**

Every consulting service page describes what the firm does across twelve weeks and says nothing about
what the client has to put in — which is the larger number, and the one that actually decides whether
the work happens. **A COO who finds out in week two that this needs a day a week of their own time
did not buy the thing they were shown.**

So every study carries **What it costs you** at the same weight as the stages or above them:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Your COO** | About a day a week, for the whole of it |
| **`Eight to twelve` interviews** | An hour each, with people who will not enjoy them |
| **One decision you have already made** | Reopened. There is always one |

It is not a fabricated figure — it is a requirement the firm sets, which is why it can be printed.
**A page that will not name the buyer's side of the bill is describing a purchase rather than a
project.**

## What The README Ruled Out, And What That Changed

The out-of-scope list names four things — **fabricated pricing, guarantees, turnaround times and
availability** — and those four are exactly what a service page reaches for when it wants to feel
concrete. They were designed around, not worked around:

- The fee section names **which model** — `S12` owns the four — and never a figure.
- `003` states each stage as **what it follows** (`After the interviews.`) where a scope document
  would state a duration. The dependency carries the information a duration would have carried, and
  none of it is invented.
- The engagement length stays where `S21` put it: the length of the work, never a promise about when
  anything arrives. `S19` already refused turnaround promises for the whole sector.
- **No form, in any study.** `S18` established that a form is a way of not giving you an address, and
  `S19` and `S20` both refuse one. A service page is where that refusal is most tempting to break,
  because a form here would measure well.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe complete page. Description at full measure, the four stages as a big-numeral
  run with no boxes, the costs as pill-labelled blocks, the limits paired, and the portrait at the
  foot beside the ask.
- **`002`** — one measure, read to the end. The stages sit at reading size and **the costs are the
  largest block on the page**, because that is the part the reader cannot find anywhere else. Columns
  would let the eye choose an order; the argument depends on arriving at the cost *after* the stages
  and *before* the limits, so the study spends its structure on sequence.
- **`003`** — the README warns about this study by name: *any supporting rail should release to normal
  flow before it competes with the body.* The rail is **not sticky**, duplicates nothing, and holds
  only what a reader checks rather than reads. The dense move is the dependency clause on each stage.
- **`004`** — **the bill before the brochure.** The reader meets what the work costs them in their own
  people's time before they meet what we do in ours. A COO who cannot give a day a week leaves on the
  first screen, which is the intended outcome — they were going to leave in week two instead, after a
  proposal had been written. **This page loses every enquiry from a reader who never priced their own
  side**, and those enquiries convert into engagements that stall.
- **`005`** — **the page is written twice, and the client's half is bigger.** Our four stages run
  small across the top, complete but compressed; yours arrives underneath at display size, taking
  three times the vertical room. Nothing is cut from our side — all four stages keep their
  descriptions — so the argument is made by scale alone, which is the only honest way to make it.

`005` is the sector-native reading because this catalog has spent twenty-three sections converting by
subtraction. **A service page that gives more room to the buyer's obligation than to its own offer is
that move at the point of sale**, and the point of sale is where it costs the most to make.

### A tooling gap, recorded

`review/check-composition-collisions.js` returns **empty sequences for every `S23` study**. Its
`fingerprint()` classifier matches section-level block names and does not recognise page-level ones
(`ctx`, `lede`, `runs`, `costs`, `limits`, `foot`, `page`, `mine`, `yours`, `bill`), so the checker is
not covering this section at all — it reports no collisions because it sees nothing. Distinctness was
verified by rendering instead. This joins the open tooling item about the classifier testing class
names before `tag === "ul"`.

- `003` then measured **247**, over the detail band. The dependency column is legitimate density, but

  the column was narrowed and the ask moved to the head of the left column.

- `003` — the rail at `0.38fr` was too narrow and wrapped nearly every label; widened to `0.5fr`.
