# Section Role

Who the firm has worked for, where permission exists.

# The Constraint, Which Forbids The Literal Deliverable

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` does not merely constrain this section:

> **Named clients or logos** — Forbidden in every section.

A mark cannot be placeheld the way a count can. A drawn, approximated or invented logo is a claim
about a real company — the same line that forbids a placeholder testimonial attribution.

> **A count may be placeheld; a mark may not.**

**Every mark in this batch is a reserved area and stays one.** 29 across five studies, all labelled
empty areas, none filled. No `<svg>` or `<canvas>` appears anywhere in the batch.

# The Governing Idea

> **A logo wall shows you the clients who did not mind.**

The engagement a client is least willing to have named is almost always the one that mattered most —
the restructuring, the regulatory response, the deal not yet announced. A wall of marks is a map of
the firm's **least sensitive** work, presented as its best.

The batch prints the wall anyway, because a buyer expects it, and then says so out loud.

# The Four Absences

| Reason | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **A live transaction** | Naming them moves a price |
| **A regulated process** | The regulator has not published, so neither have we |
| **The contract forbids the mark** | Signed before the work started, and we do not go back and ask |
| **They said no** | No reason was given and none was asked for |

The fourth is the one that matters. Three plausible legal reasons and no fourth would read as a
tidied list; **a client who simply refused is what makes the other three credible.**

All are placeholder demo values marked `data-placeholder="true"`. The last does the most work: **a
permission is not permanent**, and a firm that re-checks annually and reports what it lost is
describing a process rather than making a boast. It also explains why the wall is small.

# Why Shape A Dominates Here

Every other section had to be asked what its photograph was *of*. This one does not — the reserved
areas hold logos, and everyone knows what a logo is. It is the only section in the catalog where a
grid of reserved areas is the correct answer rather than a failure of nerve.

What the batch refuses is the *dense* wall. A dense wall is a volume claim, and volume is the one
thing this section cannot honestly claim: **the number shown is set by permissions, not by the
quantity or quality of the work.**

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No drawn, filled, approximated or placeheld mark.** Every logo area is reserved and stays
  reserved. No `<svg>`, no `<canvas>`, no initials standing in for a wordmark.
- **No client name, sector name or industry label** beside a mark.
- **No dense wall.** The count shown is set by permissions, not by volume of work.
- **No tiering.** Cell sizes in `003` are attributed to artwork shape, and the block header says so.
  A size hierarchy of clients is a ranking the firm has no business publishing.
- **No unit chart or count graphic.** Drafted for `005` and rejected — accurate, but it reads as a
  data widget.
- **No global header or navigation.** The section-shell rule.
