## The Section Role

Who the firm has worked for, where permission exists.

## The Problem, Which Is Larger Than S09's

`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` does not merely constrain this section. It **forbids its literal
deliverable**:

> **Named clients or logos** — Forbidden in every section.

`S09` had a section whose usual solution was unavailable. This one has a section whose usual
*content* is unavailable. Everything on a logo wall is a third-party mark, and a mark cannot be
placeheld the way a count can — a drawn, approximated or invented logo is a claim about a real
company, which is the same line that forbids a placeholder testimonial attribution.

So **every mark in this batch is a reserved area and stays one.** Eight, three, eight, four and six
of them across the five studies, all labelled empty areas, none filled.

> **A count may be placeheld; a mark may not.**

That leaves the question of what the section is *about*, since it cannot be about the marks.

## The Governing Idea

> **A logo wall shows you the clients who did not mind.**

The engagement a client is least willing to have named is almost always the one that mattered most to
them — the restructuring, the regulatory response, the deal that had not been announced. A wall of
marks is therefore not a sample of the firm's work. **It is a map of the firm's least sensitive
work**, and every firm that prints one is quietly presenting its most forgettable engagements as its
best.

The batch prints the wall anyway, because a buyer expects it, and then says the above out loud.
Saying it is the only thing that makes the wall worth trusting.

## The Four Absences

Every study names why the rest are missing. These are statements about the firm's own permissions,
not claims about anybody else.

| Reason | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **A live transaction** | Naming them moves a price. It lifts when the deal completes, or it never lifts |
| **A regulated process** | The regulator has not published, so neither have we |
| **The contract forbids the mark** | Signed before the work started, and we do not go back and ask |
| **They said no** | No reason was given and none was asked for |

The fourth is the one that matters. Three plausible legal reasons and no fourth would read as a
tidied list; **a client who simply refused, with no explanation offered, is what makes the other
three credible.**

The last is the batch's most specific detail and the one that does most work. **A permission is not
permanent**, and a firm that re-checks annually and reports what it lost is describing a process
rather than making a boast. It also explains, without special pleading, why the wall is small.

## The Media Question, Which Answers Itself

Every other section in this sector had to be asked what its photograph was *of*. This one does not:
the reserved areas hold logos, and everyone knows what a logo is. It is the only section in the
catalog where a grid of reserved areas is the correct answer rather than a failure of nerve, which is
why **three of the five studies are shape A** — the first time A dominates a batch.

What the batch does refuse is the *dense* wall. `002` shows three marks and says why it is not
building a wall; `004` shows four and puts them after the offer; `005` shows six and leaves most of
the field dark. A dense wall would be a volume claim, and volume is the one thing this section cannot
honestly claim — **the number shown is set by permissions, not by the quantity or quality of the
work.**

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

A reader scanning a logo wall is doing exactly one thing: **looking for their own problem in somebody
else's name.** They cannot find it, because a logo says who paid and not what was wrong. Every logo
section in this sector is therefore a question the page refuses to answer, placed just above a
contact form.

`004` answers it instead of decorating around it. The offer goes first, at the top, filled — *tell us
the situation and we will name the closest client we are allowed to* — and the marks come after,
small, as evidence that such clients exist rather than as the argument.

This is deliberately a third distinct conversion basis in three consecutive sections:

| Study | Converts on |
| --- | --- |
| `S08-004` | **Audit** — ask about the number we would rather not print |
| `S09-004` | **Verification** — the quotes sorted by who will take a call |
| `S10-004` | **Relevance** — the page admits it cannot tell you whether it has met your problem |

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading, and the one that puts the gaps *inside* the wall. Eight reserved marks
and four accent-tinted cells carrying a reason each, interleaved so the wall cannot be read without
reading the absences. A wall with no gaps claims a permission rate no firm has.

**`002`** — **the editorial refusal.** Three marks at plate size, and the space a twelve-cell wall
would have taken is given to the reasons the others are absent. Three marks make no volume claim at
all; they are examples, and the caption says so.

**`003`** — **two declared blocks**, each carrying its own count in its header, so nothing is implied
by adjacency. The wall is a bento of four cell sizes, and the block header states why: **the grid is
sized for the artwork, not for the client** — a wordmark needs a wide cell, a monogram a square one.
The absent block is given the same visual weight as the present one rather than tucked underneath.

**`004`** — **the conversion study.** See above.

**`005`** — **the empty field is the content.** One field, six tracks, four rows, and more than half
the cells left dark. The marks are small and the reasons are set at display size, which inverts what
a logo section normally does: the interesting information is not which clients consented, it is why
the others could not. The composition *is* the ratio — twelve marks out of a hundred and eighty six
is a sparse field, and a dense wall would be the lie.

## What Was Drafted And Rejected

**A unit graphic in `005`** — twelve filled cells against a hundred and seventy four empty ones. It
would have been accurate and it would have read as a data widget, which the theme contract rules out
with the technical idiom. The sparseness is expressed by composition instead, and the exact figures
are given as text in the field.

**Tiering the bento in `003`** — an uneven module system drifts toward platinum, gold and silver. The
cell sizes here are explicitly attributed to artwork shape, and the block header says so, because a
size hierarchy of clients is a ranking the firm has no business publishing.

## Two Corrections Made During Authoring

**The batch was drafted under band.** All five came in at 143–177 words against a floor of 170. The
gap was not padding-shaped — two pieces of real content were missing, and both are things a logo
section owes a reader who is being asked to trust a small wall:

- **the scope of the permission** — each mark is used with written permission for this page and
  appears in no pitch deck;
- **how the permission is kept current** — permissions are confirmed every twelve months, and `two`
  came off at the last review.

Added to all five, which brought them to 179–199.

**`CONS-S10-005` collided with `CONS-S09-005`.** The collision check reported an 86% sequence match
between them — `HEAD1>LEDE>LIST6>FOOT` against `HEAD1>LIST6>FOOT`, both dark, both on six tracks,
both closing on a two-column foot. They are **adjacent sections**, so on the assembled variant-005
page they would have run back to back as the same layout twice.

The fix improved the study rather than merely dodging the checker: the foot was removed and **the
arithmetic moved into a cell of the field itself**, sitting inside the empty space it describes. The
sequence is now `HEAD1>LEAD>LIST6` and the pair no longer matches.
