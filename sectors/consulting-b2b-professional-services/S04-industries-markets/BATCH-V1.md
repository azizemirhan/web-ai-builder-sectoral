## V1 Was Rejected, And Why It Matters

V1 was five studies each carrying six reserved images — a card grid, a masonry, a bento, inverted
cards, and landscape panels. Every one of them passed its own checks. The rejection was about
something none of those checks could see:

> Not every section should carry image areas. One could have **the image on the left and the points
> on the right**. Let each design be different — because when the sections come one under the other
> they have to form a whole.

That is a page-level defect, and it was self-inflicted. The *Composition Devices* correction made
after the `S02` rework said *a card without media is a table cell with rounded corners*, and it was
read as **put an image in every card**. `S02`, `S03` and `S04` were then all authored as six-image
grids. Stacked at the same variant, the reader met four consecutive bands of grey rectangles.

The fix is a rule above the section, now in the theme contract: **three section shapes — A item
grid, B anchored, C type and colour — with B as the default, never more than two consecutive A, and
at least one C in any run of six.**

## The Section Role

Which markets the firm works in — and whether the reader believes it.

## The Governing Constraint — Everybody Lists Every Industry

The default is a grid of twelve industry icons, and it fails because **nothing on it could have been
left off.** Worse, an industry claim is specifically a claim to know that market's constraints, and
an icon makes no such claim.

> **Naming an industry is not knowing it.**
> Each entry names the binding constraint that makes work in that market different — which
> demonstrates knowledge instead of claiming it, and is checkable in one sentence by the only reader
> who matters.

| Industry | The binding constraint |
| --- | --- |
| Financial services | The regulator is a stakeholder in every decision, including the ones about cost |
| Healthcare and life sciences | Clinical staff cannot be reallocated by a spreadsheet |
| Energy and utilities | The price control cycle sets the horizon, not ambition |
| Industrials and manufacturing | The plant sets the pace; a shorter changeover is a wish, not a plan |
| Retail and consumer | Margin is decided in the supply chain, not in the deck |
| Public and regulated sectors | Defensible or it does not happen; speed is the second virtue |

**Why this is not fabrication:** the constraints are general structural facts about how those
industries operate — the same category as generic vocabulary, which the sector direction permits as
real. None is a claim about this firm's record, a named client, a figure, or a measurement.

**How it differs from the `S03` device:** `S03` states a **limit** — what the firm will not do, a
policy it must accept. `S04` states a **constraint** — what the market imposes on anyone working in
it, which the firm did not choose. One is a promise about conduct; the other is a demonstration of
knowledge.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the shape the review asked for by name: one photograph on the left with room to be a
photograph, the six markets as blocks on the right. The image is `position: sticky`, so it holds
while the markets scroll past it. **No hairlines anywhere** — each block is separated by air and by
an accent numeral in a pill, and gets its own hover surface.

**`002`** — an editorial opening of two unequal plates, then the six as blocks on two columns with a
bronze numeral. An editorial spread opens on photography and then gets out of its own way.

**`003`** — the bento survives from V1, with four of its six images removed. That improved it: with
six images the size hierarchy was competing against six identical grey rectangles, and with two the
hierarchy is legible immediately. Tiles with no image centre their text instead of top-aligning it.
Size remains the only ranking claim — no scores, no coverage bars, no maturity scale.

**`004`** — **shape C, no reserved media at all.** At this variant `S02` and `S03` are both image-led,
so a third grid would have given the reader three consecutive bands of rectangles. The content here
is six short sentences, which colour and scale carry alone: a near-black challenge panel, six
colour-field blocks, two of them filled in the accent on a diagonal. It is not a study that quietly
became text-only — it would be *worse* with photographs in it.

**`005`** — one image at full frame rather than six at a third of it, then the markets as a
three-column typographic sequence. That is the more art-directed answer in any case: V1 was six
copies of a device, this is one composition.

## V3 — Media Reduction Pass

Reviewed against the assembled page rather than the section, and rejected:

> Not every section should have an image area either.

The count made the case. **Twenty-four of the catalog's twenty-five studies carried a reserved image
area.** Shape C existed in the theme contract as a rule and almost never in practice, because the
rule was written as "at least one C in any run of six" — satisfiable by ignoring it. It is now per
variant: read down a variant's five sections, and if none is C, one of them is wrong.

A second rule was added with it, and it is the one that decides these cases:

> **A section is not entitled to media just because it could have some.** Ask what the photograph is
> *of*. If the honest answer is "something related to this topic", there is no photograph — there is
> a grey rectangle standing in for one, and the section is better built from type and colour.

That test is decisive for abstractions. There is no honest photograph of *quantitative modelling*,
of *financial services*, or of a process stage called *Options*: only an illustration, and an
illustration of an abstraction is a stock photograph — the first thing this sector's anti-patterns
rule out.

### What changed here

`CONS-S04-002` moved from its previous shape to **C — type and colour**, its two opening plates replaced by one saturated pull block interrupting the market columns.
