## The Section Role

Evidence of having done the work — under the sector's hardest constraint.

## The Governing Constraint — Confidentiality

The sector brief records it: **client confidentiality applies**, and `S24` is scoped to anonymised
engagement context. This section has to prove the firm has done the work **without naming who it was
for.**

The default failure is *"a leading global financial services organisation"*. Everybody writes it,
nobody believes it, because the anonymisation strips out exactly the part that made it evidence and
replaces it with an adjective.

> **Anonymity removes the client, not the problem.**

Two consequences, and every study in the batch applies both:

**1. The situation is stated in full.** It is the reader's situation, not the client's identity, so
there is nothing to protect in it. The four situations are the ones from `CONS-S02`, so a reader who
recognised themselves two sections earlier recognises themselves again here.

**2. The redaction is made visible.** Every case ends on a **withheld** line naming what cannot be
said *and why*:

| Case | Situation | Withheld, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | The strategy was agreed and nothing had changed | The sector. Two firms operate in it; naming one identifies the other |
| 02 | It cost more to run than it should | The site count. It is public, and it would name them |
| 03 | The programme was late and reporting said it was fine | Every date. The programme is still running |
| 04 | A decision was coming and the evidence was thin | The decision. It has not been announced yet |

**A case study that conceals its own anonymity asks to be trusted twice. One that names the
redaction asks once** — and the discretion is itself the thing being sold.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

Every other treatment of this section apologises for the anonymity. `004` sells it:

> **The reason you cannot see their name is the reason you would not see yours.**

A buyer whose own engagement would be sensitive reads a firm's discretion as a promise about
themselves. So in that study the withheld line is the **header** of every card, set at the largest
size on it, with the story underneath — the usual hierarchy exactly inverted.

## The Media Problem, Which Only This Section Has

Ask this section's photograph what it is **of**. It cannot be the client's premises, their people or
their product — that is the whole constraint. What remains honest is **the firm's own artefact**: a
model on a screen, a wall of interview notes, the room a decision was taken in. Every slot label in
this batch says so explicitly, and `004` carries none at all.

That is why this batch is media-light by nature rather than by rhythm: two studies at 4 slots, two
at 1–2, one at zero.

## Not A Document

A redaction sits one hairline from a classified-memo metaphor, which
`../CONSULTING-THEME-CONTRACT.md` rules out with the rest of the document idiom. **There is no black
censor bar in this batch, no stamp, no "CONFIDENTIAL" watermark.** Every withheld line is a
saturated colour field with a plain sentence in it — a design device, not an imitation of an
artefact.

## V2 — Composition Rework, And The Check It Produced

Reviewed and rejected:

> This was in the previous one too. Section designs cannot be the same.

Correct. `CONS-S07-002` was drafted as a **near-copy of `CONS-S05-002`**: statement, offset second
voice, wide plate, one column of numbered blocks, a second plate, an underlined text link. The only
change was moving the second plate from the middle of the column to the end — and the study's own
comment header claimed it had varied the device.

The theme contract already forbade this: *two studies at the same variant that share a layout fail
as surely as two that share no palette.* The rule was sound; the checking was not. It was being done
by eye across thirty-five studies, and **eyes do not scale past about four sections.**

### The check

`review/check-composition-collisions.js` now builds a structural fingerprint per study — the
sequence of top-level blocks, the column count of each item container, where the media sits inside
an item, and which devices are present (rail, sticky, overlap, scrim, bento, alternate, masonry,
featured) — and reports same-variant matches, exact and near. It is now the fourth test in
*Composition Devices* in the theme contract.

### What changed here

| Study | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CONS-S07-002` | Statement, plate, one column of cases, plate, link — i.e. `S05-002` | A two-column editorial spread: statement, artefact and action held in a **sticky standing column**, the four cases running past it on the right |
| `CONS-S07-005` | Four full-width rows — a 100% match with `CONS-S06-005`, the section **directly above it** | One banner case at full width, three compact cases across a three-column row |

Content did not change in either. Word counts stayed in band: `002` at 205, `005` at 225.

### Known near matches still standing

The check reports fifteen near matches across the sector. Most are non-adjacent or separated by
device. Two are worth carrying forward as open items, both between **adjacent** sections and
therefore the kind that stack badly:

- **variant 002 — `S03` and `S04`** at 100% sequence (`HEAD1>VOICE>LIST2>FOOT`). They differ by
  media placement, `S03` carrying an image per card and `S04` none, so they do not read identically.
  Worth revisiting when `S03` or `S04` is next touched.
- **variant 003 — `S05` and `S06`** at 100% sequence (`HEAD2>LIST2>FOOT`, both media-band). Both are
  a narrow left element beside a content column. This is the closer of the two and should be
  reworked; it was left alone here because both sections were already reviewed and accepted, and
  changing an accepted section on a check's say-so is a bigger call than the check can make.
