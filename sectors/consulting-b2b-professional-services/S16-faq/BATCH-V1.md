## The Section Role

The questions a buyer actually arrives with.

## The Trap, Which Is Two Traps

**The questions are invented.** *What makes you different.* *Why choose us.* Nobody has ever typed
those into anything. They are marketing copy wearing a question mark, and *frequently* is the word
doing the lying.

**The answers are hidden.** Every FAQ in this sector is a stack of collapsed rows, so the reader pays
in interaction to find out whether the answer was worth reading — which is also how a section
conceals that its answers are thin.

## The Governing Idea

> **"Frequently" is a claim, and almost no FAQ can support it.**

So every entry carries **when it is actually asked**, which is the field that makes the claim
checkable — and two of the six are entries the firm says are *not* frequently asked, which is the
part no competitor will print.

## The Six Questions

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| When it is asked | The question | The answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First call, every time | How much will this cost? | Not until we know which model fits. In the first hour we can say which one we would refuse to sell you |
| First call, every time | Who will actually do the work? | The people in the room when you meet us. Each names the stages they are not on |
| Right after the first call | Can you show me a client like us? | Not on this website. Tell us the situation and we will name the closest one we can |
| **Rarely, and it should be first** | What happens if it is not working? | You stop at the next stage gate. The exits are in the contract, and clients use them |
| Once the proposal is out | Will you put your fee at risk on the outcome? | No. That needs a counterfactual nobody can produce. For our money at risk, ask for the fixed fee |
| **Never out loud** | Is the webinar a sales call? | The broadcast is a broadcast. The room of twelve is work. We label which is which |

The four states are a description of the buying process rather than a category scheme, and the two at
the bottom of it are the useful ones. **A question nobody asks is the one worth putting in front of
them.**

## The FAQ As An Index

Every answer resolves into the section that carries the detail: the engagement models, the people and
their stages, the naming policy, the stage-gate exits, the counterfactual, the difference between a
broadcast and a room.

**An FAQ is an index to the page it sits on**, and this one is written that way rather than as a
self-contained brochure. On the assembled page it is the section that proves the other fifteen agree
with each other.

## Why There Is No Accordion

Stated at the foot of every study rather than left as an absence:

> **Nothing here is collapsed. A question worth answering is worth reading without a click.**

Every answer is short enough to print in full, and printing them in full is the only proof of that.
This is also why the batch carries no JavaScript: the one interaction this section normally has is
the one it is refusing.

## The First All-C Batch In The Sector

A question has nothing to photograph, and an FAQ that reaches for a stock image of somebody looking
thoughtful has already lost. **All five studies carry zero reserved media** — the first batch in
`CONS` where every variant is shape C.

The cost is recorded rather than hidden: at variant 003 this puts two zero-media sections back to
back, since `S15-003` is also C. It is not a rule breach — the cap is on consecutive shape A — and
the two compositions share nothing, but it is the thinnest stretch on that variant's assembled page
and worth knowing about.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

An FAQ converts, if at all, with *still have questions? contact us* — a line that assumes the reader
knows what to ask.

This study assumes the opposite. **The question at the top is the one almost nobody asks** — *what
happens if it is not working?* — filled, at the largest size on the page, labelled as rarely asked,
with the action being to ask *it* rather than to make contact in general.

> The conversion is not the answer. It is the admission that the reader was about to skip the
> important one.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading. Six questions on two columns, the *asked when* line as a tag above each
question, the two rare ones tinted in the accent.

**`002`** — the question set at the size a magazine sets a subhead, in the accent, because **the
question is the part the reader arrived with** — and every other treatment buries it in a row of
collapsed grey bars. The answer follows at ordinary size, which is the correct relationship for
someone scanning for their own question.

**`003`** — **the band leads the module.** The *asked when* line is not a tag beside the question but
the filled header of the card, running its full width. A reader scanning this section is not looking
for a question, they are looking for whether these are real questions, and the band answers that
first. *First call, every time* and *never out loud* are the two most informative strings on the
page.

**`004`** — **the promoted question.** See above.

**`005`** — **the beat.** Question on the left at display size, answer on the right beginning lower,
always by the same distance. That gap is the pause between being asked something and answering it
honestly; a normal FAQ sets the two flush, which reads as a form being filled in. The constant
distance keeps it a rhythm rather than a decoration, and the two rarely-asked pairs carry an accent
rule instead of a grey one.

## Why `005` Is Not Another Cascade

Four studies in this sector already descend or divide: `S09-005` by type scale, `S11-005` by indent,
`S13-005` by flagship position, `S15-005` by column width. This one **offsets a pair** — nothing gets
larger or smaller down the page, and the only variable is the vertical relationship between two
things that belong together.

## The Correction: Four Of Five Drafted Over The Ceiling

`002` at 232, `003` at 254, `004` at 236 and `005` at 241 — all above the structured band's 230.

The cause is structural to the section rather than careless: **six questions with six answers and a
metadata line each is the most content-dense role in the sector**, and unlike every other section
here the content cannot be thinned by dropping an item, because a five-question FAQ answers fewer
questions.

So the trim was applied to the shared answers — all five at once, keeping content parity intact —
rather than to any one study. Three answers lost a clause each; the accordion commitment lost its
second sentence; `003` lost four words of its taxonomy line and `005` two of its lead.

**This batch sits closer to the ceiling than any other in the sector** (212–229 against a limit of
230), which is worth recording: the next FAQ-shaped role in another sector should be drafted with
five questions, not six, or with the *asked when* line only.
