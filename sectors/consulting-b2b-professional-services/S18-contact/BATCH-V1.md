## The Section Role

How to reach a person.

## The Trap

Name, email, company, phone, message, **we will get back to you shortly.**

Nobody filling that in knows who receives it, whether anyone owns it, or what happens next — and the
opacity is the point of it. The section that exists to make a firm reachable is the one that most
reliably hides it.

The two supporting failures: **a response-time promise** nobody can verify, and an **`info@` inbox**
that belongs to nobody.

## The Governing Idea

> **A form is a way of not giving you an address.**

So there is no form in this batch — no `<form>`, no `<input>`, no submit button anywhere. There are
**four routes**, and every one names the person or the thing at the other end of it.

## The Four Routes

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy.

| Route | Who receives it | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A first conversation** | The person who would run the work. We have no salespeople | We ask the five questions. By the end you know which engagement model we would propose, and which we would refuse to sell you |
| **A direct address** | One named person, not a shared inbox | If they are the wrong person they tell you who is right, rather than forwarding you into a queue |
| **A reference call** | A client of ours, not us | We introduce you to the one closest to your situation, and then we are not on the call |
| **Not yet** | **Nobody. This route is you leaving** | Take the two reports that cost nothing and the five questions. Come back when there is a decision waiting |

Each route resolves into the section that carries it: `S11`'s five questions, `S06`'s people,
`S09`'s reference call, `S14`'s ungated reports, `S12`'s models.

## The Fourth Route, Which Is The Reader Leaving

One of the four is *not yet*. It is `S11`'s first question — *what decision is this actually for?* —
turned around and pointed at the reader.

> **A contact section that cannot say *do not write yet* wants the enquiry more than the
> engagement.**

## Three Things That Will Not Happen

Stated in every study rather than implied:

- We do not ask for your budget, timeline or company size. *(The never-asked fields from `S14`.)*
- Writing to us does not add you to a list.
- **If you do not reply, we do not chase.**

The third is the one that actually stops people writing, and nobody prints it.

## No Response-Time Promise

*We respond within 24 hours* is unverifiable and is omitted sector-wide — it has been in the content
note of every batch since `S07`. What is printed instead is **ownership**: one named person rather
than a rota, which a reader can check the moment they get a reply.

## The Media Question

There is exactly one honest photograph, and it is not a lobby or a receptionist:

> **The desk this arrives on.**

The claim the section makes is ownership — that a message goes to a person rather than into a rota —
and a desk with one chair at it is the only image that supports that claim instead of decorating
around it. Two studies carry it; three carry none, because a route has nothing to photograph.

## The Conversion Insight, Which `004` Is Built On

A contact section normally converts by lowering friction: fewer fields, a bigger button, a promise
about speed. **It is treating the wrong cost.**

What stops a serious buyer writing is not effort. It is the follow-up — the knowledge that one
message becomes a sequence: a reply, a nudge, a check-in, a newsletter, a call from someone they have
never met.

> **If you do not reply, we do not chase.**

Filled, first, at the largest size in the study, with the other two commitments beside it — because a
single promise reads as a slogan and three read as a policy. **This converts by removing the cost of
contact rather than the effort of it**, and it costs the firm every enquiry it would have won by
pestering.

## Notes Per Study

**`001`** — the safe reading. Four routes on two columns, *who receives it* above *what happens
next*, and the fourth route filled in the accent because it is the one that sends the reader away.
The three commitments close in a dashed block.

**`002`** — the desk at plate width, then the four routes read across one row. The caption was folded
into the lead when the study was restructured; see the correction below.

**`003`** — **the ownership strip sits at the foot of each module, not the head.** `S16-003` puts its
metadata band at the top because a reader there is checking whether the questions are real before
reading them. Here the reader chooses a route first and *then* asks who is at the other end — so the
answer arrives where the eye stops rather than where it starts. The fourth strip is filled, because a
route whose owner is *nobody* is the one the section most wants read.

**`004`** — **the no-chase pledge.** See above.

**`005`** — **the address is the artwork.** The section's whole argument is that a form withholds an
address, so this study hands one over at the size a hero headline is normally set: a name larger than
anything else on the page, the desk beside it, the address line beneath. Every other treatment
*describes* the routes; this one **performs the claim**, and a reader who scrolls past everything
still leaves with the thing the section exists to give.

**Why the name rather than the address is the display type:** an address at that size is a technical
string and reads as an artefact; a person at that size reads as an answer to *who receives this*,
which is the field the whole batch is built on.

## Corrections

**`S18-002` collided with `S11-002` at 83%.** Both were *statement → lead → plate → caption →
two-column run → closing block* — the same editorial skeleton two variants apart on the same
assembled page. The routes were changed to **four across** and the caption folded into the lead,
which removed a block and dropped the match to 55%. The four-across row suits four short routes
better than a stacked pair did, so the study is better for it.

**Content parity slipped in `005` again.** Its hero note and one strip entry were shorter than the
equivalent fields in the other four. Restored.

That is the **fifth consecutive batch** in which the study that compresses its items also lost a
field rather than only size — `S13-005`, `S14-005`, `S15-005`, `S17-004`, and now `S18-005`. The
check is cheap and it keeps failing, so it belongs at the top of the QA list rather than the bottom:
**before rendering, diff the fields of the compressed study against the safe one.**

**Every one is the study saying it does not do the thing.** A scan that reads prohibitions as
violations will keep doing this, and the answer is not to soften the copy — it is to read the hit
before acting on it.
