## The Trap Was Named In Advance

The sector direction's warning for the conversion-led variant governs the whole batch:

> **Becoming a quote calculator that invents a price.**

And money is omitted sector-wide. Those two facts settle what this section cannot be. What they
leave turned out to be the section's subject.

## The Governing Constraint — A Price Is A Function Of Information

    A form that does not ask for the information cannot produce a price. It produces a
    guess, and the guess gets revised.

Every *request a quote* form in this industry asks for a name, an email and *tell us about your
project*, then returns a number that nothing in the form determined. **A number revised upward is
worse than no number at all** — by the time it moves it has already done its work of getting the
firm onto a list.

## The Three Kinds Of Number

The distinction the batch is built on, and the one `005` sets at display scale:

| What you can send | What comes back | What it is worth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sentence | **No number.** A conversation and a site walk | Honest, and the one most firms will not offer |
| A design | **A range, with every assumption written beside it** | Enough to choose between two schemes. Not enough to build against |
| A tender package | **A price** | The only one of the three that is one |

    Only this one is a price. The other two are called prices by people who want to be
    on your list.

That sentence appears in all five studies. It is the batch's load-bearing line.

## The Device — What It Costs Not To Know

Every question carries **why it is asked** and **what happens to your number if nobody can answer
it.** The second half is the new content:

| We ask | If you cannot answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is being built | The number becomes a range, and we say so |
| Where the site is | We cannot tell you which band you are in — `S14` |
| Whether the building is in use | **We price it as occupied** — the expensive assumption |
| What stage the design is at | You get the first of the three |
| What surveys exist | **We will not price around it** — `S13`'s recorded mistake, kept as a rule |
| What fixes your start | We assume nothing is fixed |
| Who decides | The programme has a hole in it |

This is the `S05` limit, the `S06` gate and the `S07` cost applied to a form. The method has now
held across eleven consecutive sections.

## The Budget Question, Answered Rather Than Dodged

> **We do ask, and here is what we will not do with it.** A scope with no budget is a fishing trip
> for both of us. But we will not simply price to your figure — if the scope costs less we say so;
> if it costs more, **we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.**

Present in all five. The usual choice on a page like this is between demanding a budget and
pretending not to want one; stating the position is more useful than either.

### `002`, the letter

The only editorial study in this catalog written in the second person throughout, and the only one
set as a single measure on a centred page rather than as a spread. A bid page is a thing you address
to one person who is about to spend an afternoon assembling documents; the register follows from
that, and it lets the study say *before you send us anything, here is what we will do with it* —
which is the section's whole argument in a form no card grid reaches.

### `003`, the gap drawn rather than described

Three columns at three unequal widths, and **what is missing is drawn as a dashed absent block
rather than left out.** The reader sees the gap between what they are holding and what a price
requires, in the same eyeline as the two columns that have it.

> **The gap between your column and the next one is what a real price costs to obtain.**

The sector direction names this variant's trap as *the specification table*. The study avoids it by
making the columns unequal on purpose: a specification table asserts that three things are
comparable, and these three are not.

### `004`, the one form in the batch

`S05-004` holds this sector's resource check; `004` holds this section's submission, and **it is the
only study in the batch with a control in it.** Six native controls, no JavaScript, no arithmetic of
any kind, nothing required, and no `number` or `date` input that would imply a calculation.

Beside every control is the reason it is asked. That is the conversion device, and it is also the
argument: these are the things a price is a function of, so a form without them cannot produce one.
The submit button's own note says so:

> **This does not produce a number and nothing here calculates one.** It tells us which of three
> answers we can honestly give you, and gets a date in for us to come and walk it.

The rail beside the form says which of the three you will receive, and ends with the refusal.

### `005`, three words

**Guess. Range. Price.** Each at display scale, each with what it requires and what it is worth. The
sector direction names this variant's trap as *the car advert*; nothing here is styled, and the
display scale is spent on three words carrying an argument that costs the firm the fastest of the
three answers.

It is the only study in fifteen batches to sit on a **mid-tone** ground. Every other study in this
sector is on paper or on near-black; this one is on the material, which is also what makes the three
words read as cut rather than printed.

Its reserved fields are deliberately **not** enlarged, and that is a rule this catalog learned the
hard way in `S08-005` and again in `S09-005`: an em-dash at display scale reads as a redaction. The
display scale belongs to the three words, which are real.

## What Is Not On Any Of The Five Pages

- No price, rate, range, figure or *from* anything.
- No calculator, estimator or instant quote.
- No response time, turnaround or *within N days* — the `S06` schedule rule, arriving where it is
  most tempting to break.
- No win rate and no count of tenders submitted. Neither would tell a reader anything about their
  own job.

Each study declares that list inside a `data-refusal` region, because a section whose content is
*what we will not send you* has to be able to name the thing it refuses.

## Checker Note

Four rules failed a study for a defect in the rule rather than in the study, and it is worth
recording which kinds:

1. **Keyed to a word one study owns.** The first-answer rule required *guess* as well as *no number*.
   Only `005` sets that word visibly — it is that study's device — so the rule failed the other four.
2. **Keyed to one phrasing of a consequence.** `price it as occupied` missed `003`'s *we price as
   occupied*, and `which band you are in` missed its *which attendance band you are in*.
3. **A word tested where an attribute was meant.** The no-`required`-field rule matched `004`'s
   visible sentence *None of it is required* — which is the very policy the rule exists to protect.
4. **A vocabulary ban where a mechanism ban was meant.** `calculator` was banned outright, and four
   studies say there is not one. The sentence saying so is the section's argument. Re-keyed to the
   *offer* of a calculator; the mechanism itself — `<form>`, `<script>`, a number input — was already
   checked hard.

That is the standing rule of this catalog restated for the fifteenth time: **check the mechanism
hard, the vocabulary soft**, and expect a label-keyed rule to fail the study that differs most.

One further note, on a rule this batch invented and then withdrew. A near-neighbour ground check
across the whole catalog failed immediately: seventy-one grounds in a narrow paper band inevitably
sit close together, and the rule condemned fourteen prior batches retroactively. It was rescoped to
the five studies of one batch, which is what actually matters — **the five have to separate on a
contact sheet; they do not have to be unique in colour space.** The catalog-wide neighbours are
still printed, as information rather than as a failure.

- **The reusable outcome is that a bid form's fields are content, not plumbing.** Printing why each
  is asked, and what a missing answer costs, turns the least-read element on a contractor's site
  into the page that explains how pricing actually works. It also makes the form honest by
  construction: a field you have to justify is a field you can be argued out of.
