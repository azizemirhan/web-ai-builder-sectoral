# Section Role

How to put work to this firm, what it needs in order to price it, and what kind of number comes back.

# The Trap Was Named In Advance

The sector direction's warning for the conversion-led variant governs this whole section:

> **Becoming a quote calculator that invents a price.**

And money is omitted sector-wide — no price, rate, range or *from £X* appears anywhere in this
catalog. Those two facts settle what this section cannot be, and the useful part is what they leave.

# The Governing Constraint — A Price Is A Function Of Information

    A form that does not ask for the information cannot produce a price. It produces a
    guess, and the guess gets revised.

Every *request a quote* form in this industry asks for a name, an email and *tell us about your
project*, then returns a number that nothing in the form determined. The number is revised the
moment somebody walks the site, and **a number revised upward is worse than no number at all** —
it has already done its work of getting the firm onto a list.

So the section is built from the thing that actually decides a price:

**Every field says why it is asked, and what happens to your number if you cannot answer it.**

That is the `S05` limit, the `S06` gate and the `S07` cost applied to a form — and the new content
is *the cost of not knowing*.

# The Three Kinds Of Number

The section's core distinction, and `005` sets it at display scale:

| What you can give us | What comes back | What it is worth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sentence | **No number.** A conversation and a site walk | Honest, and the only one of the three most firms will not offer |
| A design | **A range, with every assumption written beside it** | Useful for a decision, not for a contract |
| A tender package | **A price** | The only one of the three that is one |

    Only the third is a price. The other two are called prices by people who want to be
    on your list.

# What Decides A Price, And What It Costs Not To Know

| We ask | Because | If you cannot answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is being built | Scope is the price | The number becomes a range and we say so |
| Where the site is | It decides the access band, and whether we bid at all — `S14` | We cannot tell you which band you are in |
| Whether the building is in use | It decides phasing, hours and sequence — `S04` | We price it as occupied, which is the expensive assumption |
| What stage the design is at | It decides which of the three numbers you get | You get the first one |
| What surveys exist | **Missing surveys are the largest single cause of a price changing** — `S13` | We will not price around it |
| What fixes your start | A date outside your control changes the sequence, not the price | We assume nothing is fixed |
| Who decides | It decides how long the answer takes — `S06`, `S11` | The programme has a hole in it |

# The Budget Question, Answered Rather Than Dodged

The most contentious field on any form of this kind, and the section takes a position:

> **We do ask, and here is what we will not do with it.** A scope with no budget is a fishing trip
> for both of us. But we will not simply price to your figure — if the scope costs less, we say so;
> if it costs more, **we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.**

# What This Section Will Not Do

Carried into every study:

- **No number from this page.** Anything computable from a web form would be revised at the site
  walk.
- **We will not price a job we have not walked** — `S06`'s signature line, arriving where it is most
  expensive to mean.
- **We will not price around a missing survey** — `S13`'s recorded mistake, turned into a rule.
- **No number given to get on a list.**

# One Mechanism, In One Study

`S05-004` carries this sector's resource check. **`004` carries this section's form and it is the
only one in the batch** — native controls only, no computation, no JavaScript, and no field whose
answer would be used to produce a figure on the page.

The other four studies present what to send and why as content. A bid section is about what makes a
price possible; collecting an email address is not the hard part of it.
