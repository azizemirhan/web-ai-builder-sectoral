Putting work to us

The same information, at three depths

Find the column that matches what you are holding. The gap between it and the next one is what a real price costs to obtain, and it is drawn here rather than described.

Depth

You send a sentence

You get no number

A conversation and a date to come and look. The honest answer, and the one most firms will not give.

  • What is being built

    In your own words is enough at this stage.

  • Where the site is

    So we can tell you which attendance band you are in, and whether we should be bidding at all.

Not enough for a number

Everything below is missing, which is why there is not one:

  • Design stage
  • Surveys
  • Occupied or empty
  • What fixes the start
  • Who decides

You send a design

You get a range, with the assumptions beside it

Every assumption written where you can argue with it. Useful for a decision, not for a contract.

  • Drawings at a stated stage

    Concept or planning — tell us which, because it decides how wide the range is and we would rather say so than let you assume it is narrow.

  • Where the site is

    The band, and whether a firm nearer you would serve you better.

  • Whether the building is in use

    A different job rather than the same job more carefully. Without it we price as occupied, which is the expensive assumption.

  • What surveys exist, and which do not

    Named either way. We will not price around a missing one, and we will tell you which one is holding the range open.

  • What fixes your start

    A term, a lease, a season. It changes the sequence rather than the price, and we would rather design around it than discover it.

Still missing, and why it is a range

A specification and a set of quantities. Without those, any single figure would be a range with the honesty removed.

You send a tender package

You get a price

Only this one is a price. The other two are called prices by firms that want to be on your list.

  • Everything in the column to the left

    All of it, answered rather than assumed.

  • A specification and quantities

    The part that turns a range into a number. Where the quantities are ours to take off, we say so and price the taking-off as our risk.

  • Surveys in hand, not pending

    This is the one we hold hardest, and it came from getting it wrong once.

  • Who decides, and what they can decide

    Because most delay on a live job is a decision waiting on somebody.

  • A site walk before we submit

    We will not price a job we have not walked. This is not negotiable and it is the reason some tenders go elsewhere.

Your budget, which is the question everybody dodges

We do ask. A scope with no budget attached is a fishing trip for both of us.

What we will not do is simply price to your figure. If the scope costs less than you have allowed we will say so; if it costs more we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.

What is not on this page

  • No instant quote
  • No calculator
  • No figure
  • No response time
  • No win rate

A response time is a promise about a job nobody has looked at yet, and the usual way of meeting one is to send the first of the three numbers and call it the third.
