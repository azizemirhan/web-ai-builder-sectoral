Putting work to us

A price is a function of information. There is no calculator on this page.

Anything we could work out from a web form would be revised the moment somebody walked the site, and a number revised upward is worse than no number at all — it has already done its job of getting us onto your list.

  1. If you can give us a sentence

    You get no number

    A conversation and a site walk instead. That is the honest answer to a sentence, and it is the one most firms will not give you because it does not get them onto a list.

  2. If you can give us a design

    You get a range, with the assumptions beside it

    Every assumption written where you can argue with it rather than buried where you meet it later. Useful for a decision. Not useful for a contract.

  3. If you can give us a tender package

    You get a price

    Only this one is a price. The other two are called prices by people who want to be on your list, and both of them move.

What decides a price, and what it costs not to know

  • What is being built

    Why we askScope is the price. Everything else on this page only modifies it.

    If you cannot answerThe number becomes a range and we will say so rather than let you read it as a price.

  • Where the site is

    Why we askIt decides which of our attendance bands you are in, and therefore whether the site manager is there every morning — and whether we should be bidding at all.

    If you cannot answerWe cannot tell you which band you are in, and the band is the part that changes what you actually get.

  • Whether the building is in use

    Why we askIt decides phasing, hours and sequence, which is a different job rather than the same job more carefully.

    If you cannot answerWe price it as occupied, which is the expensive assumption. Tell us and it may come down.

  • What stage the design is at

    Why we askIt decides which of the three numbers above you get. A concept, a planning drawing and a tender package produce three different objects.

    If you cannot answerYou get the first one, which is not a number at all.

  • What surveys exist

    Why we askAsbestos, ground, structural, drainage. Missing surveys are the single largest cause of a price changing after it is accepted.

    If you cannot answerWe will not price around it. We have done that once and spent longer arguing about whose risk it was than the work took.

  • What fixes your start

    Why we askA date outside your control — a term, a lease, a season — changes the sequence rather than the price, and we would rather design the sequence around it than discover it.

    If you cannot answerWe assume nothing is fixed, which is the cheapest assumption and the one most often wrong.

  • Who decides

    Why we askIt decides how long an answer takes once we are on site, and most delay on a live job is a decision waiting on somebody rather than a trade waiting on us.

    If you cannot answerThe programme has a hole in it that nobody will notice until the first query.

  • Your budget

    Why we askA scope with no budget is a fishing trip for both of us. We do ask, and we say what we will not do with it.

    What we will not doSimply price to your figure. If the scope costs less, we say so. If it costs more, we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.

And what happens next

We come and walk it. That is not a formality and it is not a sales visit — a drawing tells you the building and the site tells you the job.

If what we see says a firm nearer you or a specialist would serve you better, you will get that instead of a price, and you will have lost a morning rather than a tender.

What we will not do

  • No instant quote
  • No calculator
  • No figure from a web page
  • No pricing around a missing survey

We will not price a job we have not walked, and we will not give you a number to get onto a list. Both are easy to do, both look like service, and both end with a conversation in month two that nobody enjoys.

There is no response time on this page either. A promise about how quickly a price arrives is a promise about a job nobody has looked at yet, and it is the kind of thing that gets met by sending you the first of the three numbers and calling it the third.
