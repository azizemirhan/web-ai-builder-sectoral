Putting work to us

Three things get called a price. One of them is one.

What comes back to you depends entirely on what you are able to send, because a price is a function of information, and a form that does not ask for it cannot produce one. So here is which of the three you would get, and what each is worth.

If what you have is a sentence

Guess

We will not give you one. It is the number most firms send, and the only one of the three that can be sent before anybody has looked at the job.

What it requires

Nothing at all.

Which is the whole problem with it. Nothing in the form determined the figure, so nothing in the job holds it, and it moves the moment somebody walks the site.

What you get instead

A conversation, and a walk round the site.

We will not price a job we have not walked. Say the idea in a sentence and we will come and look at it — that is the honest answer to a sentence, and the one most firms will not give, because it does not get them onto a list.

If what you have is a design

Range

With every assumption written out beside it in language you can argue with. Useful for making a decision. Not something to sign.

What it requires

Drawings, and an honest account of what is still open.

Where the design is undecided we say what we have assumed, and we assume the expensive version, so that the range moves down as it firms up rather than up.

What it is worth

Enough to choose between two schemes.

Not enough to build against. Anyone telling you otherwise is asking you to accept their assumptions without reading them, and those are exactly the assumptions that get revised later.

If what you have is a tender package

Price

Against a specification, after we have walked it, with the exclusions listed where you can see them. Only this one is a price.

What it requires

A specification, a drawing set, and the surveys.

We will not price around a missing survey. That rule came from a job where we did, and it has cost us tenders since — which is rather the point of having it.

What it is worth

A figure that stays where it was put.

What is inside it is fixed, what is outside it is written down where you can read it, and the way it changes afterwards is a written instruction rather than a phone call.

The questions

What we have to ask, and what it costs when nobody can answer

  • What is being built

    Scope is the price. Everything else here only modifies it.

    If you cannot answerThe number becomes a range, and we say so.

  • Where the site is

    It decides access, and whether we are the right firm for it at all.

    If you cannot answerWe cannot tell you which band you are in.

  • Whether the building is in use

    Phasing, hours and sequence. A different job, not the same job done more carefully.

    If you cannot answerWe price it as occupied — the expensive assumption.

  • What stage the design is at

    It decides which of the three above you receive.

    If you cannot answerYou get the first one.

  • What surveys exist

    The largest single cause of a price moving after it was accepted.

    If you cannot answerWe will not price around it.

  • Who decides

    A name, and what that person is allowed to sign without asking.

    If you cannot answerThe programme has a hole in it.

And the question everybody dodges

We do ask for a budget, and here is what we will not do with it. A scope with no budget is a fishing trip for both of us, so tell us if you have a figure in mind.

But we will not simply price to it. If the scope costs less than your figure we say so; if it costs more, we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.

There is no calculator on this page and no figure of any kind on it. Anything a web form could compute would be revised the moment somebody walked the site, and a number revised upward is worse than no number at all — by then it has already done its work of getting us onto your list. There is no promise here about how quickly you will hear back, either: that would be a promise about a job nobody has looked at yet, and the usual way of keeping one is to send a guess and call it a price.
