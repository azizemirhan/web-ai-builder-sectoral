Putting work to us

Every question here says why it is being asked.

Because these are the things a price is actually a function of — and a form that does not ask for them cannot produce a price, only a number that gets revised. Nothing on this page calculates anything.

What you send us, and what each answer decides

None of it is required. Answer what you can; the gaps decide which of the three answers comes back, and we will tell you which one you are getting in the first line of the reply.

Why we askScope is the price. Everything else on this form only modifies it, and in your own words is enough at this stage.

Why we askIt decides which of the three answers you get. A concept, a planning set and a tender package produce three different objects, and only one of them is a price.

Why we askIt decides phasing, hours and sequence, which is a different job rather than the same job done more carefully. Left blank we price it as occupied, which is the expensive assumption.

Why we askMissing surveys are the largest single cause of a price changing after it is accepted. We will not price around one — that rule came from getting it wrong on a job, and it has cost us tenders since.

Why we askSomething outside your control changes the sequence rather than the price, and we would rather design the sequence around it than discover it in month two.

Why we ask, and what we will not doA scope with no budget is a fishing trip for both of us. We will not simply price to your figure: if the scope costs less we say so, and if it costs more we tell you before we bid rather than after we have won.

This does not produce a number and nothing here calculates one. It tells us which of three answers we can honestly give you, and gets a date in for us to come and walk it.

And what comes back

  • No number

    If what you have is a sentence. A conversation and a site walk instead — the honest answer, and the one most firms will not give.

  • A range, with its assumptions

    If what you have is a design. Every assumption written where you can argue with it. Useful for a decision, not for a contract.

  • A price

    If what you have is a tender package, and after we have walked it. Only this one is a price.

There is no instant quote on this page, no calculator, and no figure of any kind. Anything computable from a web form would be revised the moment somebody walked the site, and a number revised upward is worse than no number at all — by then it has already done its work of getting us onto a list. There is no response-time promise either: that is a promise about a job nobody has looked at yet, and the usual way of meeting one is to send the first of the three answers and call it the third.
