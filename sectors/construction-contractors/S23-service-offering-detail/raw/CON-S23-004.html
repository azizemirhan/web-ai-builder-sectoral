The building envelope

Send us the junctions, not the elevations.

An elevation shows what a building will look like. A junction detail shows whether it will leak and whether anybody can price it — and it is the drawing that gets left until last on almost every job we are asked to look at.

Three drawings, and we can tell you something useful

  • The parapet, or whatever the roof does when it reaches the wall
  • A window head, with the tray drawn rather than noted
  • The base, with the finished ground level on it

Those three carry most of the risk in an envelope, and between them they tell us whether the drawings have been through a building or only through a printer.

Send the three details

We will mark them up and send them back with the questions on them. No price, and no visit needed for this part — it is an afternoon of somebody's time and it is the most useful afternoon in the job.

  • If the details do not exist yet

    Then do not draw them for us

    At concept stage a junction detail is a guess with a title block on it. What is useful instead is a conversation about the three we would want, so that when they are drawn they are drawn once.

    Ask for that conversation
  • If they exist and nothing is ordered

    This is the moment

    Everything is still a drawing, so everything is still cheap. Changes here cost a revision; the same change after the frame order is a variation.

    Send the three details
  • If the envelope is already going up

    Then send us the bay that is next

    We will not tell you the finished ones are fine, because we would not know. Anything found after the scaffold goes is a rope job or another scaffold, so what is worth doing is hosing the next bay before it is closed — a test at the bay tests the detail while it is still cheap to change.

    Ask about the bay test

And these are the joints we would be asking about

  • Wall meets windowOurs to the frame, theirs from it — drawn on a detail both firms sign before either orders.
  • Wall meets groundOurs, and put in writing the day the finished level moves, because it will.

The windows, the frame behind everything and everything below the damp-proof course are not in this work — they belong to the window contractor, the frame contractor and groundworks, and they are named on the drawing rather than left to be assumed. There is no turnkey version of this and no price on this page. A firm claiming there are no interfaces is claiming something no building has, and a number given before anybody has seen a junction detail is a guess — what to send in order to get a price is set out where you put work to us.
