The building envelope

Buildings do not fail in the middle of a package. They fail at the edges.

Roof, walls, windows, and the junctions between them. Water gets in where two trades meet and both thought the other had it — so the useful half of this page is not what is included, it is where the work stops and who owns the joint.

In this work

  • The roof covering, its upstands and its outlets
  • The wall build-up, including the cavity and what closes it
  • Openings: the reveals, the sills, the heads and the cavity trays
  • Every junction between those three, which is the part this page is about
  • Access, scaffolding and the sequence they impose

Not in this work, and who does it instead

  • The windows themselves — the window contractor
  • Anything that passes through the envelope — whoever put it through
  • The structure the envelope hangs on — the frame contractor
  • The finished internal face — the fit-out package
  • Below the damp-proof course — groundworks

  • Roof meets wall

    • Roof covering
    • Wall build-up

    The parapet, and the tray that stops water running down inside the cavity. Two build-ups, one detail, and it is only ever built once.

    Who owns the jointOurs, both sides. There is nobody to hand it to and we do not look for one.

  • Wall meets window

    • Wall build-up
    • Window contractor

    The reveal, the tray over the head, and the seal at the frame. Two firms, and the seal belongs to one of them and the tray to the other.

    Who owns the jointOurs to the frame, theirs from it. The line is drawn on a detail we both sign before either of us orders anything.

  • Wall meets ground

    • Wall build-up
    • Groundworks

    The damp-proof course, and the finished ground level somebody raised after it was set. The commonest late change on any job, and it is made by a landscaper.

    Who owns the jointOurs, and we will say so in writing when the level moves — because it will, and afterwards nobody remembers agreeing to it.

  • Anything that goes through it

    • Us
    • Whoever made the hole

    Vents, flues, brackets, cables. Each one is a hole in a thing whose entire job is to have no holes in it, and on most jobs this joint has no owner at all until somebody names one.

    Who owns the jointWe take it, and we say so at the start. The alternative is discovering in the second winter that nobody did.

Three things that go wrong on this kind of work, and what we do about each

  1. The detail nobody drew

    Standard details cover the typical bay. Every job has three junctions the drawings do not show, and they are always the ones with two trades in them.

    What we doDraw them and get them signed before we start, rather than at the point somebody wants the scaffold down.

  2. The sequence

    An envelope is built once and inspected once, from a scaffold that is already booked to come down. Anything found afterwards is a rope job or another scaffold.

    What we doEverything that has to be seen is seen while the scaffold is still standing, and the sign-off is a condition of striking it.

  3. The test

    A water test on a finished building tells you that a finished building leaks. It does not tell you which detail, and by then the answer is behind a wall somebody has decorated.

    What we doWe hose the first bay. A test at the first bay tests the detail while it is still cheap to change, and it is the cheapest thing on this page.
