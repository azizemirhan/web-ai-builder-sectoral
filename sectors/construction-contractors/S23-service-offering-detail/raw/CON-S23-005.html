The building envelope

The lines between the parts are the parts that leak.

So on this page the rules are the loud thing and the packages are the quiet one. The bands are what we build; the rule between each pair is the junction, with the name of whoever owns it written on it.

Roof meets wall

Ours, both sides. There is nobody to hand it to and we do not look for one. The parapet and the tray inside it, built once, from a scaffold already booked to come down. Anything found after that is a rope job or another scaffold.

The outer skin, the cavity and what closes it. Directly employed, which is why this is the band we can be held to entirely.

Ours to the frame, theirs from it. The reveal, the head tray and the seal. Two firms at one detail, and the line between them is drawn and signed before either orders anything rather than after one has built past it.

Ours, in writing the day the level moves

The rule between them

Wall meets ground

Ours, and put in writing the day the level moves. The damp-proof course was set on a level surveyed before there was any landscaping, and then the ground went up. The commonest late change on any job, and it is made by a landscaper.

Not our part

Everything below the damp-proof course

Groundworks. The band stops here and the page says so, which is the point of drawing the bands at all.

The rule that is usually broken

Anything that goes through it

We take it, and we say so at the start. Vents, flues, brackets, cables — each one a hole in a thing whose whole job is to have no holes in it. On most jobs this line has no owner at all, which is why it is the only broken rule on this page, and why the second winter is when it gets discussed.

And then a hose on the first bay

A water test on a finished building tells you a finished building leaks. It does not tell you which detail, and by then the answer is behind a wall somebody has decorated.

So the test is at the first bay, before the second is built. It tests the detail while the detail is still cheap to change, and it is the cheapest thing on this page.

There is no turnkey version of this

A firm claiming there are no interfaces is claiming something no building has — and the interfaces it is not claiming are exactly the ones that leak.

Every rule above has a name on it, including the ones we hand over. The drawing of a package with nothing between it and the next one is the drawing that leaks.
