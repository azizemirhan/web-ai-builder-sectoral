The paperwork

What a certificate is, and what it is not.

A certificate is not an achievement. It is a permission with an expiry date and somebody who can take it away — and that is the most useful way to read the column beside this one.

Most contractors put these on a page as a row of badges. The badge is the part you cannot check and the registration number is the part you can, so the badges are not here and the numbers are, with every one of them sitting on a public register kept by the body that issued it. We would rather you looked than took our word, and if one of them has lapsed by the time you read this, the register will say so before we do.

What they let us do

Between them they get us through the prequalification stage on most tenders, onto public buyer registers without submitting the same evidence to every authority separately, onto sites where waste routes are a contract condition, and through the gate at all — which is what insurance is for, and which no client should waive.

That is a real list and it is worth having. A firm without it spends its life filling in the same questionnaire, and a client dealing with one spends theirs reading the answers.

What they do not

The quality certificate audits the system rather than the building. The safety assessment is a prequalification exercise, not a description of how a site runs on a wet Friday when we are behind. The environmental one says nothing whatever about a carbon footprint, whatever the badge is shaped like. Trade body membership confirms that we exist, that we pay, and that nobody has struck us off.

And a competence card records a test that somebody passed, which is not the same as being good at the work, as anybody who has run a site already knows.

The one that matters is not on this page

None of it tells you whether the site is run well. It tells you that somebody checked the paperwork on a date.

Who can stop the work, what happens to them if they were wrong, and whether any of that changes when the job is behind — those are the questions, nobody certifies the answers, and they are worth asking standing on a site rather than reading anywhere.
