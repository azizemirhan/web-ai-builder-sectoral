The paperwork

The register, with the numbers where the badges usually go

A certificate is a permission with an expiry date and somebody who can take it away. Every number below is on a public register kept by whoever issued it.

What we do not hold

We are not on every public framework, and there are assessments in this industry we have never been through. If your tender requires one we do not hold, we would rather say so at the first email than after you have shortlisted us — and if it is one we could reasonably go and get, we will say that too, and let you decide whether it is worth waiting for.

Check every one of them

Each number above sits on a public register kept by the body that issued it, and we would rather you looked than took our word for it.

If one has lapsed by the time you are reading this, the register will say so before we do. That is the argument for printing numbers instead of badges.

What none of it tells you

Whether the site is run well. All of the above tells you that somebody checked the paperwork on a date — worth something, and not worth what a wall of badges implies.

Who can stop the work, and what happens to them if they were wrong, is on the safety page. Nobody certifies those answers, which is exactly why they are the ones to ask.
