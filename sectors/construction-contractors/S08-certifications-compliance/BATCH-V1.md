## The Governing Constraint — A Certificate Is Not An Achievement

Anti-pattern two is the certification wall, and it is forbidden for a better reason than the
invented logos: **a logo asserts nothing a client can use.** A reader looking at a row of them
learns that some fees were paid.

    A certificate is not an achievement. It is a permission with an expiry date
    and somebody who can take it away.

So every entry in this batch carries four things — the category of assurance, **what it lets us do**,
**what it does not cover**, and the registration itself as a reserved field. The second and third are
the content; the fourth is the design.

## The Inversion — The Number Goes Where The Logo Would Have Been

    A logo tells you somebody paid. A number tells you where to look.

On a certification wall the badge is large and the registration number is absent. That ordering is
the whole problem: the badge is the part you cannot check and the number is the part you can. So in
every study the **reserved registration is set at the scale a badge would have been**, with the
categories around it at ordinary text size.

**This is the one section in the catalog where the reserved field is not a compromise.** It is the
most prominent element on the page, and its prominence is the argument. A reviewer looking at any of
these studies knows immediately what the finished page asserts and where every claim will be
verifiable — which a wall of badges cannot tell them at any size.

Every study also carries the instruction that goes with it: each number sits on a public register
kept by the body that issued it, and *"if one has lapsed by the time you read this, the register will
say so before we do."*

## Scheme Names Are Reserved. Categories Are Real.

A real standard or scheme printed here would assert a membership the catalog cannot verify, so the
name, the number, the issuing body, the assessment and expiry dates and any cover level are **all
reserved**. What is real is the *category* — quality management, health and safety assessment,
environmental management, trade body registration, public sector supplier registration, insurance,
operative competence. Those describe kinds of certificate rather than memberships.

The checker tests for the common real schemes and bodies by name across all five studies. None
appears.

## The Honest Half — What It Does Not Cover

The part almost no competitor prints, and the only part that helps a client calibrate what they have
just been shown:

| Category | What it does not cover |
| --- | --- |
| Quality management | Whether any individual job was built well. It audits the system, not the building |
| Health and safety assessment | How the site runs on a wet Friday when we are behind |
| Environmental management | Our carbon footprint. No certificate covers that, whatever the badge is shaped like |
| Trade body registration | Competence. It confirms we exist, we pay, and nobody has struck us off |
| Public sector supplier registration | Any preference. Eligible is not chosen |
| Insurance | As much as you would assume. Cover has limits, and yours should read them |
| Operative competence | Whether somebody is good at the work. A card records a test that was passed |

## Why Dates Are Reserved Here And Were Omitted In S06

`S06` omitted every duration and reserved none: an empty box marked *weeks* asserts that a standard
stage length exists before anybody has walked the site.

An expiry date is the opposite case. **Every certificate has one as a matter of fact rather than of
promise**, so the empty field asserts nothing that is not already true of all of them — and a
certificate displayed without its expiry is the more misleading presentation of the two.

Same test, opposite answer, for the fourth consecutive section:

| Section | Figure | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `S05` | People on our books | Reserved — the empty field asserts nothing |
| `S06` | How long a stage takes | Omitted — the empty field asserts a system |
| `S07` | Anything countable | Omitted — every countable thing there is an incident figure |
| `S08` | Registration, body, expiry | **Reserved, and enlarged** — the empty field is the whole point |

### `004`, and the card that makes the other two work

A client with a prequalification list is about to spend an evening finding out whether this firm
qualifies. The study offers to answer first, in three outcomes: **we hold it · we could go and get
it · we cannot.**

The third card is the device. *"A page that can only say yes is a page whose yes means nothing."*
And the middle one refuses the obvious dishonesty: *"We will not tell you it is imminent to keep
ourselves on your list while you wait."*

### `005`, the empty field as the picture

A column of oversized ruled registration fields is an honest image of what a certifications section
actually is before anybody fills it in — and it is the only section where that is a virtue rather
than an embarrassment. Institutional rather than dramatic, because dressing up a compliance page is
the same instinct that produces the badge wall.

## The Handover Back To S07

Every study closes on the sentence that stops this section inflating itself:

> **None of this tells you whether the site is run well.** It tells you that somebody checked the
> paperwork on a date. Who can stop the work, and what happens to them if they were wrong, is on the
> safety page — and nobody certifies those answers.

`S07` sent its credentials here. `S08` sends the judgement back. A firm that lets its certificates
stand in for its practice has been assessed, not run.

## The Batch Carries No Media At All

Stated as a decision rather than an omission: **a certifications section has nothing to photograph
that is not a logo.** A picture of a certificate is a badge with paper around it, and a picture of an
audit is a stock meeting. Where `S04` carried almost no media because its argument had to survive
without it, `S08` carries none because there is nothing honest to put in the slot.

## Corrections

| Study | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `001`, `003`, `005` | The reserved dash floated in a large empty box and read as a redaction mark rather than as a field awaiting an entry — worst in `005`, where the box is at poster scale | A fill rule under the slot, so the box reads as a record with a line to complete |

That is a correction specific to this section and worth recording: **when a reserved field is
enlarged to carry the design, the placeholder has to be redrawn as well.** The discreet em-dash that
works at caption scale everywhere else in the catalog stops reading as *a value goes here* the moment
the field is the size of a headline.

## Checker Notes

Two rules failed on the first run for reasons worth keeping:

- **The badge check was written against prose.** Every study argues *about* badges at length — *a row
  of badges*, *whatever the badge is shaped like* — and the rule caught the argument. Rewritten to
  test **class names** instead: what must not exist is an element that is a badge, not a sentence
  about one. *Check the mechanism hard, the vocabulary soft*, in its structural form.
- **Two thresholds were set by what the first four studies happened to do.** `004`'s compact register
  legitimately carries six reserved fields rather than twenty, and states its exclusions tersely
  without the label. Both rules were re-keyed to the element rather than the count or the wording —
  the same lesson `S03` and `S04` each recorded, arriving for the third time.

## Notes

- Eighth authored batch in the `CON` sector. `S01`–`S08` are complete.
- **The reusable outcome is the inversion: put the verifiable identifier where the unverifiable
  symbol usually goes.** Any sector whose trust section is a logo wall — legal, medical, financial,
  education, food — has the same latent redesign available, and it converts a decorative row into
  something a reader can act on without changing a single fact.
- The second outcome is that **a reserved field can be the design rather than a hole in it.** This is
  the first section in the catalog where enlarging the placeholder made the page more honest instead
  of more obviously unfinished, and the reason generalises: it works wherever the missing value is
  the part the reader was going to check.
- The third is the `S07`/`S08` pair. Two adjacent sections, one that refuses every credential and one
  that is nothing but credentials, each ending by pointing at the other. Neither would be as strong
  alone.
- `S09 Company Stats` is next, and it is the section the sector direction's counter rule was written
  for: year founded, projects completed, people employed and largest project by value are reserved
  fields; happy clients, satisfaction and on-time percentages are omitted; safety records are
  forbidden.
