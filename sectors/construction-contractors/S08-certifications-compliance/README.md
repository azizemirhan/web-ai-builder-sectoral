# Section Role

The paperwork: what this firm is certified, registered, assessed and insured for, and what each of
those actually permits.

`S07` said how the site is run and deliberately kept every credential off the page. This is where
the credentials live — and the sector direction has already decided their treatment: **every
certification and registration number here is a reserved field.**

# The Governing Constraint — A Certificate Is Not An Achievement

Anti-pattern two is the certification wall: a row of standards logos, invented. It is forbidden, and
not only because the catalog cannot invent the logos. It is forbidden because **a logo asserts
nothing a client can use.** A reader looking at a row of them learns that some fees were paid.

    A certificate is not an achievement. It is a permission with an expiry date
    and somebody who can take it away.

So this section is built from what a certificate actually is, and every item carries four things:

| On every entry | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| What kind of assurance it is | **Real** — generic category vocabulary |
| **What it lets us do** | **Real, and the reason the entry exists** |
| **What it does not cover** | **Real, and the honest half** |
| Scheme name, registration number, issuing body, assessment and expiry | **Reserved fields** |

# The Inversion — The Number Goes Where The Logo Would Have Been

On a certification wall the logo is large and the registration number is absent. That ordering is
the whole problem, because the logo is the part you cannot check and the number is the part you can.

    A logo tells you somebody paid. A number tells you where to look.

So in every study the **reserved registration number is set at the scale a logo would have been**,
and the categories sit around it at ordinary text size. The reserved field is not an apology here;
it is the most prominent element on the page, and its prominence is the argument.

This is the `WELL-S11` test at its most useful. An empty registration field asserts nothing — every
certificate has a number, and the firm knows it. Reserving it and enlarging it tells a reviewer
exactly what the finished page will carry, and makes it impossible for the catalog to ship a
fabricated credential in its place.

**Every study also carries the instruction that goes with it:** every number on the page can be
checked on the issuing body's public register, and the reader is told to do it.

# Scheme Names Are Reserved, Categories Are Real

The direction forbids an invented certification, accreditation, scheme membership or registration
number. A real scheme name printed here would assert a membership this catalog cannot verify, so:

- **Reserved**: the scheme or standard name, the registration number, the issuing or assessing body,
  the assessment date, the expiry date, and any level or cover figure.
- **Real**: the *category* of assurance — quality management, health and safety assessment,
  environmental management, trade body registration, public sector supplier registration, insurance,
  operative competence. These describe kinds of certificate, not memberships.

**No study names a real standard, scheme, body or registration in visible copy**, and the batch
checker tests for the common ones by name.

# The Seven Entries And What They Do Not Cover

The honest half is the content most competitors omit entirely:

| Category | What it does not cover |
| --- | --- |
| Quality management | Whether any individual job was built well. It audits the system, not the building |
| Health and safety assessment | How the site runs on a wet Friday when we are behind |
| Environmental management | Our carbon footprint. No certificate covers that, whatever a logo suggests |
| Trade body registration | Competence. It confirms we exist, we pay, and nobody has struck us off |
| Public sector supplier registration | Any preference. It means eligible, not chosen |
| Insurance | As much as you would assume. Cover has limits and exclusions, and yours should read them |
| Operative competence | Whether somebody is good at the work. A card is a test that was passed |

# The Closing Refusal, And Its Handover Back To S07

Every study ends on the sentence that stops this section inflating itself:

> **None of this tells you whether the site is run well.** It tells you that somebody checked the
> paperwork on a date. Who can stop the work is on the safety page, and nobody certifies that.

`S07` sent the credentials here. `S08` sends the judgement back. The pair is deliberate: a firm that
lets its certificates stand in for its practice has been assessed, not run.

# Why Dates Are Reserved Here And Were Omitted In S06

`S06` omitted every duration and reserved none, because an empty box marked *weeks* asserts that a
standard stage length exists before anybody has walked the site.

An expiry date is the opposite case. **Every certificate has one, as a matter of fact rather than of
promise**, so the empty field asserts nothing that is not already true of all of them — and a
certificate shown without its expiry is the more misleading of the two presentations.

Same test, opposite answer, for the fourth consecutive section.

# Boundary With S05, S07 And S15

| Section | What it owns |
| --- | --- |
| `S05` Capabilities | **What we hold** — trades, plant, technical capacity |
| `S07` Safety Program | **How the site is run** — authority, briefing, near misses |
| `S08` Certifications & Compliance | **The paperwork**, and what each piece of it permits |
| `S15` Bid RFQ | **The formal submission** |

The test against `S07`: this section may display a **credential**; it may not describe **practice**.
The moment a study explains how the site is run it has written `S07` — and `S07` is the better page
for it, which is why the closing refusal says so out loud.
