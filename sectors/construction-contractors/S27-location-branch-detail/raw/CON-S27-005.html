One office, and the yard behind it

Nothing here is for you. Everything here is for your site.

A contractor's office is not a shop, and almost nobody who reads this page is coming here. So the address is small and complete, and the largest thing on the page is what leaves the yard before the sites start.

  • Plant To your site, the same day

    Off our own yard, loaded and gone the same day it is asked for. That is the only reason a yard is worth what it costs to keep.

  • The fitter In a van, not on a telephone

    A breakdown on a job this office runs is a drive rather than a call to a hire desk. It is the difference between an afternoon and a week.

  • The manager Before the gangs go out

    Out first thing, which is what makes the morning visit on your site possible at all. The yard is emptiest by the time the sites start.

What does not leave here

Anything for a site past the region above. That plant is hired locally, from somebody else's yard and on somebody else's schedule, and the fitter becomes a telephone call.

A job further out is run from another office, and we will name it rather than manage one from the wrong end of a motorway.

Who is based here

Contracts managers, a buyer, the plant fitter, the yard foreman, estimating and accounts.

The site managers are not. They are on sites, which is the whole point of them. No names are printed: we have not been told them, and a made-up one is worse than an empty field.

There is no such thing as national coverage from one yard

A firm claiming it is either very large or not telling the truth. Saying so costs less than being found out on the second job.

A delivery

The yard gate, not the office door. Somebody in the yard signs for it, and the middle of the morning is easiest once the gangs have gone.

A supplier or a subcontractor

Send what you do, where you work and your insurances. Turning up unannounced is not how anybody gets on a list.

Somebody looking for work

Early, before the gangs go out, and ask for the yard foreman. There is no form for it, because a form would be slower than the walk.

And not a client

That is the most useful line on this page. If you have a live job the person you want is on it, and the number you want is your site's rather than this one.

You cannot cross the yard in what you drove in. Boots and a hi-vis, or you will be met in the office instead — which is fine, and is what most people should do anyway.

Ring first, because the person you came to see is very often not here and the drive is longer than the call. Tell us before you come and we will say honestly what the route from the gate is like, including when the answer is that it is not passable that week.

There are no facility ticks anywhere on this page. A yard is not a level floor, it is not the same place two months running, and a tick would be a promise about somewhere nobody has measured.

Reserved — map, never an embedded service

It is this size on purpose.

What the map would be worth

A map tells you where this office is, which is the one fact on this page that will not help you. What matters is which sites it runs, and that is in the contact line at the top.

And what is not printed at all

No opening hours — an empty hours table asserts a weekly schedule. No directions, travel time or nearest station: nobody here has measured your journey, and a route written by somebody who has not driven it is worse than none.
