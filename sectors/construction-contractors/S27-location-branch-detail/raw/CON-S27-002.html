Clause one

Who this notice is for

  • A delivery. The wagon comes to the yard gate rather than the office door, and somebody in the yard signs for it.
  • A supplier or a subcontractor. Turning up unannounced is not how anybody gets on a list.
  • Somebody looking for work. Early, before the gangs go out, and ask for the yard foreman.

A client is not on that list. A contractor’s office is not a shop, and almost nobody who reads this page is coming here — the client least of all, because their meetings happen on their own site. If you have a live job, the number you want is your site’s rather than this one.

Clause two

Ring before you come

The person you are coming to see is very often not here. The drive is longer than the call, and the call is answered while the sites are working.

There are no opening hours printed on this notice. An empty hours table asserts a weekly schedule, and the honest version is a sentence: the yard is emptiest by the time the sites start, and the middle of the morning is when somebody can stop and talk to you.

Clause three

At the gate

You cannot cross the yard in what you drove in. Boots and a hi-vis, or you will be met in the office instead — which is fine, and is what most people should do anyway.

Nobody is going to be pleased about a visitor walking through a load-out in trainers, and being turned back at the gate is a longer conversation than being met at the door.

Clause four

Getting from the gate to the office

Tell us before you come and we will say honestly what the route is like, including when the answer is that it is not passable that week.

There is no row of tick icons on this notice. A yard is not a level floor, it is not the same place two months running, and a tick would be a promise about somewhere nobody has measured.

Clause five

What leaves here, and what does not

  • Plant off our own yard, loaded and gone the same day it is asked for.
  • The fitter, in a van, so a breakdown is a drive rather than a call to a hire desk.
  • The manager, before the gangs, which is what makes a morning visit on your site possible at all.

Jobs further out than the region this office runs are run from another office, and their plant is hired locally rather than loaded here. There is no such thing as national coverage from one yard. We will name the office rather than let a job be managed from the wrong end of a motorway.

Clause six

Why there is no map on this notice

A map tells you where this office is, which is the one fact on this page that will not help you. What matters is which sites it runs, and that is clause five.

There is no photograph either. A photograph of a gate is not a gate, and this notice is for people already standing at one.
