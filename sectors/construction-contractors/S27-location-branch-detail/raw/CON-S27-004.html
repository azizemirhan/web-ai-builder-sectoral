A contractor’s office is not a shop. Almost nobody who reads this page is coming here, and the client least of all — your meetings happen on your own site. So the largest block below sends people somewhere else, and the only ask on the page is that you ring before you come.

A delivery is the fourth arrival and the most common one: the wagon comes to the yard gate rather than the office door, and the middle of the morning is easiest once the gangs have gone. The line at the top of this page is the whole argument — an office that collects visits it cannot use is wasting somebody’s morning, and it is never its own.

Ring before you come

If you are coming anyway

You cannot cross the yard in what you drove in. Boots and a hi-vis, or you will be met in the office instead — which is fine, and is what most people should do anyway.

Ring first. The person you came to see is very often not here, and the drive is longer than the call.

Tell us before you come and we will say honestly what the route from the gate is like, including when the answer is that it is not passable that week. There is no row of tick icons on this page: a yard is not a level floor, and a tick would be a promise about a place nobody has measured.

What actually leaves here

  • Plant off our own yard, loaded and gone the same day it is asked for.
  • The fitter, in a van, so a breakdown is a drive rather than a call to a hire desk.
  • The manager, before the gangs, which is what makes a morning visit on your site possible.

Past the region above, none of that travels. Plant is hired locally and the fitter becomes a telephone call, which is slower and is worth knowing before you sign rather than after.

Reserved area — map, never an embedded service

A map tells you where this office is, which is the one fact on this page that will not help you. What matters is which sites it runs, and that is in the contact block above rather than under a pin.

It sits here, below the contact details, on purpose. Nothing decorative goes above a telephone number on a page somebody reads in a van.

What this page will not do to get you through the gate

  • No plan your visit button and no form. The ask is a telephone call before you drive, and it is the only one on the page.
  • No directions, travel time or nearest station. Nobody here has measured your journey, and a route written by somebody who has not driven it is worse than none.
  • No claim about how far we reach. There is no such thing as national coverage from one yard, and saying so costs less than being found out on the second job.
