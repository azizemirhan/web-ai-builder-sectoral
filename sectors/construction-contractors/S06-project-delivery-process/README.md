# Section Role

How a job runs from the first phone call to the end of the defects period.

`S02` said what you can hire us for, `S05` said what we hold. This section answers the question a
client asks last and worries about first: **what is actually going to happen to me?**

# The Governing Constraint — The Gates Are The Content, Not The Stages

Every contractor's process section is the same five arrows. *Consultation · Design · Build ·
Handover · Aftercare* is on every competitor's site, costs nothing to write, and tells a client
nothing they did not already assume. It is anti-pattern nine — the generic list that every rival
also claims and none can be checked — drawn as a diagram.

The stages are not where the information is. The information is at the joins:

    A process section that only lists what we do is a diagram.
    What a client needs to know is where the doors close behind them.

So every study in this batch draws the **gate** between one stage and the next, and each gate
carries the two things a client is actually trying to find out:

| At every gate | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **What is fixed after this** | *When am I locked in?* |
| **What we need from you before it** | *What is going to be my fault?* |

The second half is the one no marketing process diagram contains. Every such diagram is written
entirely about what the contractor does, while most delay on a live site is a decision waiting on
somebody on the client's side. A process section that says so is describing the job; one that does
not is describing an advertisement.

**Every study in this batch carries both halves at every gate.**

# The Schedule Rule, And Why This Section Omits Rather Than Reserves

The sector inherits `WELL-S08`: **a sequence is content, a schedule is a claim.** `S02` and `S03`
have already applied it. This is the section where it decides the design, so it is worth stating
exactly how far it goes.

**No duration, date, programme length or lead time appears anywhere in this batch — not as copy,
and not as a reserved field.**

That is a different treatment from the one directly next door in `S05`, and the difference is the
`WELL-S11` test doing its work:

| Section | Figure | Treatment | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `S05` | People on our books | **Reserved field** | The empty field asserts nothing. Every firm has a number and knows it |
| `S06` | How long a stage takes | **Omitted** | The empty field asserts a system — that a standard duration exists before anybody has seen the scope |

    Reserve the field when the empty field asserts nothing.
    Omit it when the empty field asserts a system.

A labelled empty box reading *Pre-start: — weeks* tells a reviewer that the finished page promises a
fixed programme per stage. No contractor can promise that before a site walk, and the catalog would
be shipping the shape of a claim even with the number left out. So the box is not drawn at all.

**What replaces it** is the gate. A client reading a process wants to know when change gets
expensive, and that is an ordering fact rather than a scheduling one — which the section can state
truthfully and completely.

# No Stage Numbers

The numbered arrow is this section's visual cliché, and it collides with the sector's signature
failure: the counter. Both put a digit where an argument should be.

So the stages in this batch are ordered by **position and by name** — *the first conversation, the
site walk, the price, pre-start, on site, handover, after* — and no study numbers them, in copy or
in generated content. The names are doing the ordinal work, and they carry meaning that a badge
reading `03` does not.

# What Is Real, And What May Not Be Invented

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Stage names | **Real** — generic vocabulary |
| What happens at a stage | **Real** — a description of work |
| What is fixed after a gate | **Real, and load-bearing** — an ordering fact |
| What the client owes before a gate | **Real, and kept** — the half nobody prints |
| Reporting and valuation rhythm | **Real** — the firm's own practice, not a promise about length |
| Duration, date, programme length, lead time | **Omitted** — not even as a reserved field |
| Percentage complete, on-time rate | **Omitted** — asserts a measurement system |
| Price, rate, provisional sum | **Omitted** — `S15` and the estimate own money |
| Certification, scheme, standard, named regulation | **Forbidden** — the sector direction |
| Client or project names, safety record | **Forbidden** |
| Site media per stage | **Reserved areas** |

**Not present in any study:** a digit in visible copy; a duration, date or lead time of any kind; a
percentage; a price or rate; a certification, accreditation or named regulation; a client or project
name; a safety record; an award, rating or review; an urgency device.

# The Sentence This Section Is Built Around

The sector's copy direction offers one line as its example of a sentence a competitor cannot copy:

> *"We will not price a job we have not walked."*

This is the section that earns it, and it appears at the site-walk stage in every study. It is a
position rather than a value, it is checkable, and a firm that prices from drawings cannot print it.

# Boundary With S02, S05, S07, S15 And S23

| Section | What it owns |
| --- | --- |
| `S02` Construction Services | **What you can hire us for** |
| `S05` Capabilities | **What we hold**, how deep, and what happens at the edge of it |
| `S06` Project Delivery Process | **What happens to you**, stage by stage, and where each door closes |
| `S07` Safety Program | **How the site is run** — who stops the job, briefing, near misses |
| `S15` Bid RFQ | **The formal submission** |
| `S23` Construction Service Detail | **One service in full**, on its own page |

    S05 is the resource. S06 is what the resource does to your month.

The test against `S07`: the *on site* stage may describe reporting, decisions and who answers the
phone, but the moment it describes stopping the job on safety grounds it has written `S07`. The test
against `S15`: a stage may be **described**; it may not be **submitted to**.
