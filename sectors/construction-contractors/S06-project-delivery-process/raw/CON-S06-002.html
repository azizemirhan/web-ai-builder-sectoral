How a job runs

The arrows are all the same. The doors are not.

What follows is the order a job actually runs in. Between the stages are the gates — the points where something stops being cheap to change, and where we needed something from you to get that far. Those are the parts worth your attention, so they interrupt the page.

01To begin with

The first conversation

You tell us what you want built and roughly where. We tell you whether it is the kind of work we do, and which parts of it would be our own people rather than somebody else’s.

If it is not for us, this is where we say so — a cheaper place to find that out than the third meeting.

Fixed after this

Nothing at all. This is the stage where changing your mind is free, and we would rather you spent it.

What we need from you

Roughly what, roughly where, and who will have the authority to say yes later on.

02Before any price

The site walk

We will not price a job we have not walked. A drawing tells you the building; the site tells you the job — the access, the neighbours, the ground, and the thing nobody mentions because they stopped noticing it years ago.

We bring the person who would run the job, not only somebody from the office who will hand it on.

Fixed after this

Still nothing — but what we saw now goes into the price, including an honest line for what we could not see.

What we need from you

Access, and half an hour of somebody who knows the building’s history.

03Then

The price

A price, and with it the list of what we could not price and why. Assumptions are written where you can argue with them rather than buried where you will meet them later.

The exclusions are the part worth your time. That is where the surprises live on every job, ours included.

Fixed after this

The scope you accepted. Anything added later is a variation, and it is better to argue about that now than in the middle of the work.

What we need from you

A decision on the exclusions, and any information you are holding that we have asked for.

04Before we start

Pre-start

Contract, sequence, temporary works, permissions, site set-up. The manager who will run the job is in this meeting, so the plan is made by the person who then has to live inside it.

Fixed after this

The sequence. Moving a stage now costs money, because the trades and the plant are booked around it.

What we need from you

The signed contract, the permissions that are yours to get, and final choices on anything with a long order.

05While it runs

On site

The work. A written report every week, a valuation every month, and one named person who answers the phone rather than a number that reaches an office.

Change gets more expensive every week, and the reason is physical rather than commercial. Moving a wall before the frame is a drawing. Moving it afterwards is a demolition.

Fixed after this

Everything you did not change while there was still time to change it cheaply.

What we need from you

Decisions when we ask for them rather than when they turn urgent. This is the largest single cause of delay on a live site.

06At the end, and after it

Handover, and the defects period

You get the building, the drawings, the manuals and the demonstrations on the day, rather than a box of paperwork posted later once everyone has forgotten what it was for.

Then the defects period, and the same site manager comes back. Sending somebody who was not there spends your morning explaining the job to a stranger before anything is fixed.

Fixed after this

The building. What remains is a defects list, which is a normal part of a job rather than an accusation.

What we need from you

Somebody with authority to accept it, time booked to walk it properly, and afterwards: tell us. A defect nobody reports is one nobody can fix.

No stage on this page has a length, and none has a blank where one should go.

A duration printed before anybody has walked your site is a guess in a uniform, and an empty box marked weeks makes the same promise more quietly. The order is something we can tell you truthfully. Where in that order each decision stops being cheap is something we can tell you as well.
