How a job runs

Every tape line is a door shutting.

The stages step further in as you go. Between them are the gates — what has just stopped being cheap to change, and what we needed from you to get that far.

01To begin with

The first conversation

You say what you want built and roughly where. We say whether it is the kind of work we do, and which of it would be our own people rather than somebody else’s.

If it is not for us, this is where we say so.

Fixed after this

Nothing at all. Changing your mind is still free, and we would rather you spent it here.

What we needed from you

Roughly what, roughly where, and who can say yes later on.

02Before any price

The site walk

We will not price a job we have not walked. A drawing tells you the building; the site tells you the job — the access, the neighbours, the ground, and the thing nobody mentions any more because they stopped noticing it years ago.

Site area · 3:2 · a walk, before the work

Fixed after this

Still nothing — but what we saw now goes into the price, including a line for what we could not see.

What we needed from you

Access, and half an hour of somebody who knows the building’s history.

03Then

The price

A price, and with it the list of what we could not price and why. The exclusions are the part worth your time — that is where the surprises live on every job, ours included.

Fixed after this

The scope you accepted. Anything added later is a variation.

What we needed from you

A decision on the exclusions, and anything you were holding that we asked for.

04Before we start

Pre-start

Contract, sequence, temporary works, permissions, site set-up. The manager who will run the job is in the meeting, so the plan is made by the person who has to live in it.

Fixed after this

The sequence. Moving a stage now costs money, because the trades and the plant are booked around it.

What we needed from you

The signed contract, your permissions, and final choices on anything with a long order.

05While it runs

On site

The work. A written report every week, a valuation every month, and one named person who answers the phone.

Change gets more expensive every week, and the reason is physical. Moving a wall before the frame is a drawing. Moving it afterwards is a demolition.

Site area · 3:2 · the work in progress

Fixed after this

Everything you did not change while there was still time to change it cheaply.

What we needed from you

Decisions when we asked, not when they turned urgent. The largest single cause of delay on a live site.

06At the end

Handover

The building, the drawings, the manuals and the demonstrations, on the day — rather than a box of paperwork posted later once everybody has forgotten what it was for.

Site area · 3:2 · handover, the building in use

Fixed after this

The building. What remains is a defects list, which is a normal part of a job rather than an accusation.

What we needed from you

Somebody with the authority to accept it, and the time booked to walk it properly.

07Afterwards

The defects period

The same site manager comes back. Sending somebody who was not there spends your morning explaining the job to a stranger before anything gets fixed.

Fixed after this

Nothing. This is the stage where you find out whether the rest of this page was true.

What we needed from you

Tell us. A defect nobody reports is one nobody can fix.

No stage on this page has a length, and none has an empty box where one should go. A duration printed before anybody has walked your site is a guess wearing a uniform, and a blank marked weeks makes the same promise more quietly. What we can tell you truthfully is the order, and where in that order each decision stops being cheap.
