How a job runs

The stages are the ordinary part. The gates are the ones to read.

Every contractor prints the same arrows. What matters is where each door closes behind you, and what we needed from you to get that far — because most of the delay on a live job is a decision waiting on somebody, not a trade waiting on us.

01

To begin with

The first conversation

You tell us what you want built and roughly where. We tell you whether it is the kind of work we do, and which of it would be our own people rather than somebody else’s.

If it is not for us, this is where we say so. It is a cheaper place to find out than the third meeting.

Gate

Fixed after this

Nothing at all. This is the stage where changing your mind is free, and we would rather you used it.

What we need from you

Roughly what, roughly where, and who will have the authority to say yes later on.

02

Before any price

The site walk

We will not price a job we have not walked. A drawing tells you the building; the site tells you the job — the access, the neighbours, the ground, and the thing nobody mentioned because they stopped noticing it.

We bring the person who would run it, not only somebody from the office.

Gate

Fixed after this

Still nothing — but what we saw now goes into the price, including an honest line for what we could not see.

What we need from you

Access, and half an hour of somebody who knows the building’s history.

03

Then

The price

A price, and with it a list of what we could not price and why. Assumptions are written down where you can argue with them rather than buried where you will meet them later.

The exclusions list is the part worth your time. That is where the surprises live on every job, ours included.

Gate

Fixed after this

The scope you accepted. Anything added later is a variation, and we would rather argue about that now than in the middle of the job.

What we need from you

A decision on the exclusions, and any information you are holding that we have asked for.

04

Before we start

Pre-start

Contract, sequence, temporary works, the permissions and the site set-up. The manager who will actually run the job is in this meeting, so the plan is made by the person who has to live in it.

Gate

Fixed after this

The sequence. Moving a stage now costs money, because the trades and the plant are booked around it.

What we need from you

The signed contract, the permissions that are yours to get, and final choices on anything with a long order.

05

While it runs

On site

The work. A written report every week, a valuation every month, and one named person who answers the phone rather than a number that reaches an office.

Change gets more expensive every week, and the reason is physical. Moving a wall before the frame is a drawing. Moving it after the frame is a demolition.

Gate

Fixed after this

Everything you did not change while there was still time to change it cheaply.

What we need from you

Decisions when we ask for them rather than when they become urgent. This is the single largest cause of delay on a live site.

06

At the end

Handover

You get the building, the drawings, the manuals and the demonstrations on the day — not a box of paperwork posted on some later date once everyone has forgotten what it was for.

Gate

Fixed after this

The building. What remains is a defects list, which is a normal part of a job rather than an accusation.

What we need from you

Somebody with the authority to accept it, and the time booked to walk it properly.

07

Afterwards

The defects period

The same site manager comes back. Sending somebody who was not there wastes your morning explaining the job to a stranger before anything gets fixed.

Gate

Fixed after this

Nothing. This is the stage where you find out whether the rest of this page was true.

What we need from you

Tell us. A defect nobody reports is a defect nobody can fix.

No durations on this page, and none left blank either. A stage length printed before anybody has walked your site is a guess wearing a uniform, and a blank box where one should go makes the same promise more quietly. What we can tell you honestly is the order, and where in that order each decision stops being cheap.
