How a job runs

Every stage after this one closes something.

A job runs through seven stages, and at each join something stops being cheap to change. The walk is the last one where changing your mind costs nothing at all — which is the only honest reason to make it the next thing you do.

Nothing is fixed at this stage

The site walk

We will not price a job we have not walked. A drawing tells you the building; the site tells you the job — the access, the neighbours, the ground, and the thing nobody mentions any more because they stopped noticing it years ago.

We bring the person who would actually run the work, not only somebody from the office who will hand it on afterwards.

What we need from you

Access at a time when the building is doing what it normally does, and half an hour of somebody who knows its history. Usually the most valuable half hour on the whole job.

Ask us to come and walk it

This commits you to nothing and it is not a quotation. If what we see says a specialist would serve you better, we will tell you that instead, and you will have lost a morning rather than a tender.

No stage above has a length, and none has an empty box where one should go. A duration printed before anybody has walked your site is a guess in a uniform. The order is something we can state truthfully — and so is where in that order each decision stops being cheap.
