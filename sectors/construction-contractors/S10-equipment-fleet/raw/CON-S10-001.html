The plant

The specification you need is your site’s, not the machine’s.

A fleet list is a car advert with the prices removed. What decides whether a machine is any use to you is your access, your ground and your hours — so this page is organised by those, and each one says what we bring to it.

Plant media — an excavator working, operator in it, on a live site

  • The site says

    You can only reach it through a domestic street

    A back garden, a side return, a terrace with a gate somebody’s bins live behind. Anything full-size arrives and turns straight round again.

    • Micro excavator
    • Tracked barrow
    • Hand-fed conveyor

    So we bringPlant that fits through a standard gate and tracks rather than rolls, because a wheeled machine on a lawn is a different conversation with the neighbours.

    Who is in itOur operator, ticketed, and the same one for the job where the programme allows. A machine is as good as the person in it, and that is not on any specification sheet.

  • The site says

    You cannot put a point load where you want to stand

    A basement below, a podium slab, a vault under the pavement, or ground nobody has proved yet. The machine is not the problem; where it stands is.

    • Spreader mats
    • Lighter tracked plant
    • Long reach from a safe stance

    So we bringMats and a lighter machine working further out, with the stance agreed by the temporary works engineer rather than by whoever is on site that morning.

    When it failsOur plant, our fitter, our cost. A breakdown on a mat is a bad morning; a breakdown you are waiting on a hire yard for is a bad week.

  • The site says

    The building is occupied and there are hours you may not make noise

    A ward below, a classroom next door, a trading floor above. The constraint is not the decibel; it is the clock.

    • Electric and battery plant
    • Hydraulic breaking
    • Hand demolition

    So we bringBattery plant where it exists and hydraulic rather than percussive breaking where it does not. Much of this fleet has no quiet equivalent yet, and the honest answer is to sequence the noisy work rather than pretend otherwise.

    Who is in itSomebody briefed on the hours before the machine starts, and a supervisor who owns the clock rather than the output.

  • The site says

    Something next door must not move

    A party wall, a listed façade, a railway boundary, a neighbour who has been told what will happen and is watching. Vibration is the risk, not the dig.

    • Pressed rather than driven
    • Low-vibration breaking
    • Monitoring targets

    So we bringMethods chosen for what they do to the neighbour rather than for speed, and monitoring in place before the first blow rather than after the first complaint.

    When we say noSome of this is not ours. Where the answer is piling rigs or a specialist technique, we say so and bring somebody who does it every week.

  • The site says

    Everything has to come out across a public highway

    Muck away past a school gate, a wheel-washing point that has to fit somewhere, and a road that will be inspected whether or not anybody warned you.

    • Dumpers
    • Wheel wash
    • Sheeted haulage

    So we bringA wash point designed into the compound at pre-start rather than added after the first complaint, and sheeted loads because an unsheeted one is somebody’s windscreen.

    Who is in itDrivers who know the route out, agreed with you before the first load rather than found on the day.

  • The site says

    You need height, and a crane would mean closing the road

    Materials to a third floor, a roof edge, a frame going up in a street that cannot be shut without a permit and a programme of its own.

    • Telehandlers
    • Powered access
    • Mast climbers

    So we bringReach from inside the site boundary wherever it can be done, because a road closure is a separate programme with its own permissions and its own way of going wrong.

    When we say noIf the job genuinely needs a crane and a closure, that is a specialist and we will tell you at tender rather than discover it at the frame.

A specification tells you what a machine can do somewhere. Whether it can get down your access, stand on your ground and work inside your hours is a different question, and it is the only one worth answering here.

A row of clean machinery photographed on a forecourt is an advertisement for plant. Every picture on this page is of a machine working, with somebody in it, because that is the part you are actually buying.
