The plant

The largest words here are your problem, not our machine.

A fleet page wants to be a catalogue of objects. This one sets the site condition at the size a machine name usually gets, and the machine at the size a caption usually gets.

The site says

You can only reach it through a domestic street.

So we bringPlant that fits through a standard gate and tracks rather than rolls, because a wheeled machine on a lawn is a different conversation with the neighbours.

Anything full-size arrives and turns straight round again — a wasted morning, and a phone call nobody wants to make.

The site says

You cannot stand where you want to stand.

So we bringMats and a lighter machine working further out, with the stance agreed by the temporary works engineer rather than by whoever is on site first that morning.

A basement below, a podium slab, a vault under the pavement. The machine is rarely the problem; where it is allowed to stand is.

The site says

There are hours in which you may not make noise.

So we bringBattery plant where it exists and hydraulic rather than percussive breaking where it does not. The constraint is the clock, not the decibel.

Much of this fleet has no quiet equivalent yet, so the honest answer is to sequence the noisy work rather than claim a silent version of it.

Everything has to leave across a public road.

So we bringA wash point designed into the compound at pre-start rather than added after the first complaint, and sheeted loads — because an unsheeted one is somebody’s windscreen.

Drivers who know the route out, agreed with you before the first load rather than found on the day.

And what we do not hold: anything needing a crane or a road closure. That is a specialist, a permit and a programme of its own, and it should be priced by somebody who does it every week. We say so at tender rather than at the frame.

What is not on this page

  • Makes and models
  • Weights and tonnages
  • Reach and capacity
  • Hours run
  • Fleet value

At this size a specification would read as a boast, which is the failure this page exists to avoid. A specification tells you what a machine can do somewhere; whether it fits your opening, stands on your ground and works in your hours is the question worth answering.

Every reserved area above is a machine working with somebody in it. A clean machine photographed on a forecourt is an advertisement for plant, and this is a page about work.
