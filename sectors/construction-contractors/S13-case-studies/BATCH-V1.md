## The Rule Came From WELL-S24

    A case study is an outcome claim by construction.

Every one in this industry ends on the same paragraph — *delivered on time, on budget, to a
delighted client* — which appears on the case studies of firms that failed, because it is written
afterwards by somebody who was not there. It is unwritable in this catalog and worthless in general.

`WELL-S24`'s answer applies unchanged: **document a method, not a result.** In construction the
method has a specific and unusually concrete shape.

## The Governing Constraint — A Job Is A Sequence Of Decisions

    What we found. What we chose. What it cost us.

Ask anybody who ran a project what happened and they will not describe an outcome. They will
describe three or four moments where something was found and somebody had to choose. That unit is
real, specific, survives the client's name being reserved, and is the only part of a job a reader
can learn anything from.

## The Requirement — One Of Them Is A Decision We Got Wrong

> **Every case study on this page contains a decision we got wrong**, what it cost, and what changed
> afterwards.

The checker requires it in every study and it is present in all five. It follows directly from
`S12`'s offer of a bad reference, and it is checkable the same way: a client can be asked about that
specific decision.

The admissions written for this batch are deliberately the kind that still cost something:

- *We let the asbestos survey stay a provisional item at tender.* **What changed:** we decline to
  price that work without it — **and it has cost us two tenders since**, both to firms happy to
  price around it.
- *We took the client's word that the long-order items were on order.* They had been signed off and
  never placed.
- *We built a sequence with no version in it where a permission arrived late.*

Naming a mistake that produced a rule the firm still pays for is a different act from naming one
that was costless in hindsight, and it is the difference this batch is built on.

## Cost Is Described, Never Counted

A decision costs something and saying so is the point, but *two weeks* is a duration and *a
five-figure sum* is a value, both of which this sector omits. So cost is stated **in kind**:

> *out of our float rather than yours* · *we absorbed it* · *the sequence rather than the price* ·
> *our own gang came off another job, which started later, and we told that client why*

Every one of those is checkable with the client and asserts no figure. **A figure is something you
would have to take on trust; "out of our float rather than yours" is something a client will confirm
or contradict.**

### `002`, the log

A case study written as an essay is written by somebody who has already decided what the job meant.
**A log is written in the order things happened**, which is the only order in which the decisions
make sense — and it leaves no room at the end for a paragraph about how well it went. Entries are
marked *found · chose · cost* in the margin, which is the batch's unit used as a navigation device.

Its first entry is the one worth noting: *we priced it as an occupied refurbishment and said why,
which made us more expensive than at least one other bidder.* A decision that loses work, printed as
a decision.

### `003`, filed by discovery

Case studies are filed by project because that is how they were written, and it hides the pattern:
**the same four things are found on every job in this industry** — the ground disagrees with the
survey, the building is not what the records said, a service runs where nothing was supposed to be,
and somebody had not been told. Grouping by discovery lets a reader ask *what do you do when the
ground disagrees* across every job where it has, which is a more answerable question than *tell me
about a project*.

Its wrong decisions are **collected into one band rather than distributed**, so they cannot be
skimmed past one at a time. It is the only part of that layout deliberately harder to avoid than the
rest.

### `004`, and a third arrival at the same idea

> **Ask for the one most like yours, not the best one.**

It is `S09-004`'s *the useful number is the one nearest yours* and `S12-004`'s *ask for the ones who
are not on this page* reaching the same place from a third direction. **Three sections in this sector
now convert by giving away the flattering version**, which was not planned and is the strongest
evidence that the sector direction's honesty rule produces conversion devices rather than costing
them.

### `005`, the inversion

Every case study in this industry sets the achievement large and the difficulty small, if the
difficulty appears at all. `005` sets **the wrong decision at the size the achievement usually gets**
and everything that went right beneath it at caption size — the same inversion `CON-S11-005` made
with seniority, applied to a much more uncomfortable fact.

## Corrections

| Study | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `002` | An emphasis written in markdown asterisks would have rendered literally in visible copy | Rewritten as `<em>` |
| `005` | **The identity list was being captured by the three-column grid rule**, so *Project*, *Client* and *Scope* landed under the *Found*, *Chose* and *Cost* columns and read as though they labelled them | Grid rule scoped to a `.parts` class; the identity list is a flex row again |

`005`'s is worth recording as a CSS discipline rather than a one-off: **a descendant selector on an
element type will capture every later element of that type in the same container.** `.right ul` was
written for one list and silently claimed a second one added afterwards, and the failure was not a
broken layout but a *plausible* one that asserted a relationship between fields and columns that
does not exist.

## Checker Note

The unit rule failed `003` on the first run because it looked for three labels inside each entry,
and `003` organises **by** the discovery — its *found* half is the group heading rather than a field.
Re-keyed to the content.

That is the **sixth** time in this sector a rule keyed to a label or a class has failed the study
that varies most from the first one written. It is now the single most common defect in this
catalog's checkers, and it always fails in the same direction: against the studies a diversity
catalog most needs to let through.
