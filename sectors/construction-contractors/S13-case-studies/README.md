# Section Role

Particular jobs, in enough detail to be worth reading — without naming them, and without the
paragraph every case study in this industry ends on.

# The Rule Was Set By WELL-S24

    A case study is an outcome claim by construction.

Every one in this industry closes the same way: *delivered on time, on budget, and to a delighted
client.* That sentence appears on the case studies of firms that failed, because it is written
afterwards by somebody who was not there. It is unwritable in this catalog and it is worthless in
general.

So `WELL-S24`'s answer applies unchanged: **document a method, not a result.** And in construction
the method has a specific shape.

# The Governing Constraint — A Job Is A Sequence Of Decisions

Ask anybody who ran a project what happened on it and they will not describe an outcome. They will
describe three or four moments where something was found and somebody had to choose.

    What we found. What we chose. What it cost us.

That is the unit of this section, and every case study in the batch is built from it. It is real,
it is specific, it survives the client's name being reserved, and it is the only part of a job a
reader can learn anything from.

# The Requirement — One Of Them Is A Decision We Got Wrong

The rule that makes the rest credible, and the one no competitor prints:

> **Every case study on this page contains a decision we got wrong**, what it cost, and what we
> changed afterwards.

It follows directly from `S12`'s offer of a bad reference. A firm willing to name its own worst
decision on a job is making a claim that can be checked by asking the client about that decision,
and a firm unwilling to is publishing an advertisement with a project photograph on it.

**The checker requires it in every study.**

# What Is Real, And What May Not Be Invented

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| What was found, chosen, and what it cost | **Real** — the section's content |
| The decision we got wrong, and what changed after | **Real, and required** |
| Sector and scope | **Real** — generic vocabulary |
| Project name, client, address | **Reserved fields** |
| Project media | **Reserved areas** |
| Contract value, duration, dates, programme length | **Omitted** — the `S06` and counter rules |
| Percentage saved, weeks recovered, tonnes diverted | **Omitted** — an invented result per line |
| *On time*, *on budget*, *to a delighted client* | **Forbidden** — the paragraph this section exists without |
| A client quotation | **Forbidden here** — `S12` owns quotations and reserves them |
| Awards, ratings, satisfaction figures | **Forbidden** |

**Not present in any study:** a digit in visible copy; a value, duration or date; a percentage; an
outcome claim of any kind; a client name; an invented quotation.

# Cost Is Described, Never Counted

A decision costs something, and saying so is the point. But *"it cost us two weeks"* is a duration
and *"it cost us a five-figure sum"* is a value, both of which this sector omits.

So cost is stated **in kind**: *it came out of our float, not yours* · *we absorbed it* · *it cost
the sequence rather than the price* · *we lost the trade to another job and waited for them*. Those
are checkable with the client and assert no figure.
