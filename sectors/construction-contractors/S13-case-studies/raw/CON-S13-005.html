Particular jobs

The biggest words on each job are the ones we got wrong.

Every case study in this industry sets the achievement large and the difficulty small, if the difficulty appears at all. This one is ordered the other way, because the mistake is the part a client can ring up and check.

Healthcare · refurbishment, building occupied

What we got wrong

We let the asbestos survey stay a provisional item at tender.

What it costWhen it came back it changed the sequence, and everybody spent longer arguing about whose risk it had been than the work itself took. The most avoidable kind of delay there is, and it was ours to avoid.

What changedWe decline to price this kind of refurbishment without the survey in hand. It has cost us two tenders since, both to firms happy to price around it.

Education · frame and envelope, term deadline

What we got wrong

We took the client’s word that the long-order items were on order.

What it costThey had been signed off and never placed. We found out at the point in the programme where finding out is most expensive, and the recovery came out of the sequence we had least room in.

What changedLong-order items are confirmed by order number at pre-start and checked again by the planner. That check is a line on our own programme rather than an assumption about somebody else’s.

Civic · enabling and civils, public alongside

What we got wrong

We built a sequence with no version in it where a permission arrived late.

What it costOne did, which is normal, and a normal delay became a stopped site because nothing behind it could move forward independently.

What changedEvery civic sequence we write now assumes at least one permission arrives late, and the work that does not depend on it is brought forward rather than queued behind it.

No job here ends with a paragraph about how well it went.

That sentence — on time, on budget, to a delighted client — appears on the case studies of firms that failed, because it is written afterwards by somebody who was not on the job.

What is here instead is checkable: ask the client about any decision on this page, including the large ones.

  • On time
  • On budget
  • A delighted client
  • Any percentage
  • Any value or duration
  • Any client quotation

Cost is described rather than counted. Out of our float rather than yours is something a client will confirm or contradict; a figure is something you would have to take on trust.

And there is no quotation from any client here. Those are on the testimonials page, reserved, with the phone number offered beside them.
