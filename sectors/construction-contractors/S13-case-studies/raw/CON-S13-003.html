Particular jobs

Filed by what was found, not by which job it was.

Case studies are filed by project because that is how they were written, and it hides the pattern. The same four things are found on every job in this industry.

Project area · 3:1 · the work in progress

Discovery one

The ground disagreed with the survey

A site investigation is a hypothesis, and the dig is the test. It is the most common discovery in this industry and the one with the fewest good options.

EducationProject Project name—

ChoseSelf-delivered the remedial groundworks rather than subletting them, because we would rather be the ones standing in the hole when it disagrees with the survey.

CostOur own gang moved off another job. That job started later and we told its client why.

IndustrialProject Project name—

ChoseStopped and re-tested rather than pressing on to the next gate, with the engineer on site before the excavator moved again.

CostA stopped dig for most of a week, out of our float rather than the client’s.

Discovery two

The building was not what the records said

Drawings of older buildings record what somebody intended. Openings get filled, slabs get thinner, and nobody writes it down.

HealthcareProject Project name—

ChoseRedesigned the fixings rather than re-pouring, with the temporary works engineer on site the same day rather than the following week.

CostThe float we held on the fit-out sequence. Ours, not the client’s.

CivicProject Project name—

ChoseMonitored the neighbouring structure from before the first blow rather than after the first complaint, and pressed rather than drove.

CostA slower method than the one we had priced. We did not ask for the difference.

Discovery three

A service ran where nothing was supposed to be

The one discovery where the cheap response and the correct response look identical for about an hour, and then stop.

HealthcareProject Project name—

ChoseStopped that trade and raised it as a design query the same morning, rather than cutting first and asking afterwards.

CostThe trade went to another job while we waited. We took them back when the answer came.

Discovery four

Somebody had not been told

Not a technical discovery and the most common of the four. The agreed hours, the access, the open evening — agreed with an office rather than with the people in the building.

HealthcareProject Project name—

ChoseMoved the noisy work to the afternoons rather than asking the ward below to change what it did.

CostTwo trades on site where one would have done. We absorbed it.

EducationProject Project name—

ChoseMoved the steel erection rather than the school’s open evening, and agreed gate times with the site staff rather than the estates office.

CostThe sequence rather than the price.

And the decisions we got wrong, collected rather than distributed

One from each job, put in a band of their own so they cannot be skimmed past one at a time. This is the only part of the layout deliberately harder to avoid than the rest, and it is the part a client should read first.

Healthcare

We let the asbestos survey stay provisional at tender

It changed the sequence when it came back, and everybody spent longer arguing about whose risk it was than the work took.

ChangedWe decline to price this work without it. Two lost tenders since.

Education

We took the client’s word that a long-order item was on order

It was signed off but never placed. We found out at the point where finding out was most expensive.

ChangedConfirmed by order number at pre-start, and re-checked by the planner.

Civic

We assumed one permission would arrive on the day it was promised

The sequence had no version in it where that did not happen, which made a normal delay into a stopped site.

ChangedEvery civic sequence now assumes at least one permission arrives late.

What is not on this page

  • On time
  • On budget
  • A delighted client
  • Any percentage
  • Any value or duration
  • Any client quotation

The closing paragraph every case study in this industry ends on appears on the case studies of firms that failed, because it is written afterwards by somebody who was not on the job.

Cost here is described rather than counted. Out of our float rather than yours is something a client will confirm or contradict; a figure is something you would have to take on trust.
