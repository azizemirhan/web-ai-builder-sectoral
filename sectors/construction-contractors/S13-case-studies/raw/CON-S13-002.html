One job, at length

A log, rather than an account written afterwards.

A case study written as an essay is written by somebody who has already decided what the job meant. A log is written in the order things happened, which is the only order in which the decisions make sense — and it leaves no room at the end for a paragraph about how well it all went.

Found Before anything started

The building was going to stay open, and nobody had said so in writing

It was in the room at the site walk and in nobody’s documents. The tender assumed a possession that was never going to exist, which is a common enough way for a job like this to begin badly.

We priced it as an occupied refurbishment and said why, which made us more expensive than at least one other bidder. That is a decision worth mentioning here because it is the kind of thing that loses work.

Chose At pre-start

We put the site manager in the estates meeting rather than reporting to it

The person who would be running the job sat in the room where the phasing was agreed, instead of being told afterwards what had been decided about their sequence.

It costs a morning every week of somebody expensive. It is the cheapest thing on this page, and every difficult decision that followed was easier because of it.

Found The first fortnight

The slab was thinner than the record drawings, in the one bay that mattered

Records of buildings of this age are a hypothesis. This one was wrong in the bay where the new plant had to sit, which is the way that particular luck usually runs.

Chose The same day

Redesigned the fixings rather than re-pouring

The temporary works engineer came out that afternoon rather than the following week, and the fixing detail changed instead of the structure.

The alternative was faster to decide and slower to build, and it would have put a wet trade above an occupied floor. We would rather spend a day choosing than a fortnight recovering.

Cost Absorbed

The float we were holding on the fit-out

Ours, not the client’s. It is the reason we hold float at all, and a firm that arrives with none has priced a job in which nothing is discovered.

Chose When the hours slipped

Moved the noisy work rather than asking the ward to move

The agreed hours did not survive contact with the floor below, whose day began earlier than the estates office had told us. Breaking went to the afternoons and the quiet trades took the mornings.

Two trades on site where one would have done, and we absorbed the difference rather than raising it as a variation.

The entry we got wrong

We let the asbestos survey stay a provisional item at tender

It should have been a condition of pricing. When it came back it changed the sequence, and everybody then spent longer arguing about whose risk it had been than the work itself took — which is the most avoidable kind of delay there is, and it was ours to avoid.

What changed: we now decline to price this kind of refurbishment without the survey in hand. It has cost us two tenders since, both to firms who were content to price around it, and we would make the same choice again.

There is no paragraph at the end of this saying it was delivered on time, on budget and to a delighted client. That sentence is on the case studies of firms that failed, because it is written afterwards by somebody who was not there.

There is no value, duration or percentage either. Cost is described rather than counted — out of our float rather than yours is something the client will confirm or contradict, and a figure is something you would have to take on trust.

And there is no quotation from the client. If you want one, they are on the testimonials page, reserved, with the phone number offered beside them.
