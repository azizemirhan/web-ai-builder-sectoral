Profile · contracts manager

The question is whether this person will be on your job. The honest half of the answer is what is left when they are not.

The risk you cannot read

In their head

This column is reserved on purpose, and nobody is going to write it later. It is the shape of everything a person carries about a job that has never been put on paper — who promised what on a walk, which detail was agreed and never drawn, what the client said in the car park.

It is drawn rather than written because that is exactly how much use it is to anybody else in the week this person is not here.

The answer to it

Written down

  1. The outstanding itemsWith who owes each one, and the day it was asked for. Not a list of jobs, a list of debts.
  2. The revision being built toWhich is not always the latest one issued, and the gap between those two is where most of the arguments live.
  3. The asks not in the contract yetThe things the client has asked for that nobody has priced. This line saves an argument in month four.

Three lines is the whole of it. Writing things down is not a large undertaking in this trade, only an unpopular one, and a firm that will not do it is asking you to insure a person rather than a job.

  • How often you will see them

    Two mornings in a normal week, walked before anything else in the day. Every morning in the fortnight either side of a date that cannot move, and inside the hour if the work has stopped.

  • What they settle, and what they ring about

    Settled on your site: the sequence inside the week, what is ordered for tomorrow, stopping the work. Rung about: anything with money on it, anything that moves the completion date, taking somebody off site for good.

    A page listing only what somebody can decide is describing an owner, not an employee.

  • Why they might not be here

    Holiday, which you knew about. Illness, which you did not. And the one nobody prints: they get moved to a job that is in trouble.

    You are told at the time. The alternative is letting the visits quietly thin out, and a client who works that out alone draws the worst conclusion from it.

At seven in the morning you want the site, not this person. They are driving, and the answer is standing on the slab.

Not on this page

No qualifications, memberships or training cards, and not as empty fields either. An empty field says we keep such a list and that this person’s is pending.

No count of anything, and no sentence in quotation marks. A quote attributed to a name that has not been filled in is a statement by a person who does not exist.

The portrait is the smallest thing here. Nothing above it needed a face to make sense, which is the test a profile page should be able to pass.
