Profile · how to use it

Most people reading this should be ringing somebody else, and this page says which.

A profile page usually ends in a form and a promise to ring back. This one ends in three things to have in front of you, and in an honest answer to the question the page is actually being asked: will this person be on my job? They are on your site two mornings in a normal week, and every morning in the fortnight either side of a date that cannot move.

Before you ring, have these three in front of you.

  • Where it is

    The address, or the drawing with a number on it. Without one of the two, the call ends in somebody promising to look at it later, and later is a week.

  • What stage it is at

    Drawn, priced, tendered, started, or stuck. The answer changes who should be on the phone, and half the time it is not this person.

  • The date that matters, and why

    Not the date you would like. The one with something behind it — a lease, a term, a tenant, a machine arriving.

That is the whole ask. There is no form on this page, because a form collects a name and gives you nothing, and the three things above are what turn a first call into an answer instead of a call-back. The question this page is answering is at the top, and the routing below is the honest half of it.

What this person does settle, standing on your site, is the sequence inside the week, what is ordered for tomorrow, and stopping the work. Those three happen in front of you and they are most of what a visit is for. Three of the four rows above send you somewhere else entirely. A profile that pretends otherwise is collecting calls it cannot answer, and the cost of that is paid by whoever is stood on site waiting. A page listing only what somebody can decide is describing an owner, not an employee — the middle column above is the useful one, because it tells you how long an answer takes.

The rest of the answer

What they will tell you, before you ring

  • That the date is not achievable, if it is not, and they will say it before you sign rather than in month three.
  • That the change you want costs a fortnight, not a day. The trade after it has to be re-booked, and that is the part that costs.
  • That the weather is not what put you behind. Usually it is the sequence, and the honest version of that conversation is uncomfortable.

Printing it here costs us calls and saves the ones worth having. Nobody has ever been talked into a programme they did not believe.

And the weeks they are not here

  • Booked holiday. Told in advance, with the cover named and walked round before the week starts.
  • Illness. Covered the same morning. The walk is slower, because somebody is reading the job rather than remembering it.
  • Moved to a site in trouble. You are told at the time, rather than left to notice the visits thinning out.

Absence costs anything at all because a person carries a job in their head, so three things are written down on every job: the outstanding items, with who owes each one and the day it was asked for; the drawing revision the work is actually being built to; and the things the client has asked for that are not in the contract yet.

What this page will not do to get the call

  • No qualifications, memberships or training cards, and not as empty fields either. An empty field says we keep such a list and that this person’s is pending, which asserts something nobody has told us.
  • No count of anything, and no sentence in quotation marks. A quote attributed to a name that has not been filled in is a statement by a person who does not exist, and it is the cheapest thing on a page like this to fake.
  • No call-back promise and no form. The only thing asked of you is the three things above, and the reason for asking is that a call with them in front of you ends in an answer.
