The people who run the work

The question is not who this person is. It is whether they will be on your job.

Every profile page in this industry makes that promise without stating it. This one answers how often, and then answers the harder half — what happens in the weeks they are not there, which on a long job is a certainty rather than a risk.

What the post covers

A contracts manager runs the job above the site, and is the person a client escalates to.

They hold the programme, the labour across more than one site, and the conversations that decide whether a change is a change or an argument. They are not on your site every day, and no page should suggest otherwise — the person who is there every day is the site manager, and that is a different post with different authority.

What is written here is the post, not a biography. Nothing on this page is a claim about how good anybody is, because a claim like that is worth nothing until somebody has stood on a site with them.

How often you will actually see them

  • Two mornings in a normal week

    On your site, walking it before anything else in the day, and back in the office by the middle of the morning.

  • Every morning in the fortnight either side of a date that cannot move

    A pour, a lift, watertight, handover. The pattern suspends itself when the job needs it to.

  • Inside the hour when the work has stopped

    Not a promise about response time. It is simply what happens, because a stopped site costs more than the drive.

Authority

What they settle standing on your site

  • The sequence inside the week. Which trade goes where, and in what order, without asking anybody.
  • What gets ordered for tomorrow, so the gang is not stood waiting on it.
  • Stopping the work. Anybody on the site can, and this person does not need the reason accepted first.

All three are decided in front of you, which is most of the reason the visit is worth anything.

And what they have to ring somebody about

  • Anything with money on it. A variation is priced by the commercial manager, not agreed on a walk.
  • Anything that moves the completion date. That is a change to the contract and it goes to a director.
  • Taking somebody off site for good. A day is theirs to call; permanently is not.

A page that lists only what somebody can decide is describing an owner, not an employee. This column is the useful one, because it tells you how long an answer takes.

The weeks they are not here

  • Holiday, booked

    You are told before it happens and you are told who is covering. The cover is named rather than implied, and they walk the site with this person before the week starts.

  • Illness, not booked

    Somebody covers the same morning. You will notice, because the second walk is slower — the person doing it is reading the job rather than remembering it.

  • They get moved to another job

    The reason nobody prints. It happens when another site is in trouble, and the honest thing is to say so at the time rather than let the visits quietly thin out.

Absence costs anything at all because a person carries a job in their head. So three things are written down on every job we run, and they are what makes a week of cover survivable:

  1. The outstanding items, with who owes each one and when it was asked for.
  2. The drawing revision the work is actually being built to, which is not always the latest one issued.
  3. The things the client has asked for that are not in the contract yet — the line that saves an argument in month four.

What is not on this page, and why

No qualifications, no memberships, no training cards, and not as empty fields either. An empty field says we list them and that this person’s are pending, which asserts something nobody has told us.

No count of anything, and no sentence in quotation marks. A quote attributed to a name that has not been filled in is a statement by a person who does not exist.
