Coordination, and what it is for

The same six problems. The same four rooms. Twice.

A coordinated model does not remove any of these; every one of them still happens. What it changes is which room somebody is standing in when it turns up — so here is the set laid out without coordination, and then the same set with it.

Without anybody coordinating

Each problem is real, each is found eventually, and most of them are found by the person who has to stop work in order to report them.

    An email

    • Nothing arrives here

  • At the drawing review

    A re-issue

    • Services against structure, sometimes, if somebody lays the sections side by side

  • On site, in the work

    A variation, and two trades standing

    • Services against structure
    • Ceiling void depth
    • Builder's work openings
    • Setting out against the survey

  • After handover

    Opening something that was signed off

    • Riser and plant access
    • Who owned which object, discovered when somebody needs the record

And with somebody coordinating

The same six. Five of them move left, which is the whole of what is being bought — and the sixth does not move on its own.

  • In the model

    An email

    • Services against structure
    • Ceiling void depth
    • Builder's work openings, ahead of the pour
    • Who owns which object, in a schedule
    • Setting out — only once a survey exists

  • At the drawing review

    A re-issue

    • Riser and plant access, because it needs somebody to ask a question the software does not ask

  • On site, in the work

    A variation, and two trades standing

    • Setting out, if the survey was never done. It stays here, and it takes the others back with it

  • After handover

    Opening something that was signed off

    • Nothing, which is the point of the exercise

The sixth item is drawn twice on purpose. A model of an existing building is only as true as the survey underneath it, and on a refurbishment the building is never where the drawing says it is. Without that survey the setting out is still discovered on site — and because everything else was coordinated against a wrong origin, it takes the other five back onto the site with it. That is why we will not start one without it.

Most of what goes wrong is not a clash the software missed

It is somebody modelling to a level of detail nobody agreed, an object two parties both believed the other owned, or a file issued after the decision it was meant to inform.

So the artefacts that matter are a schedule of who owns what, a level of definition against each line, and a date. They are held by a person rather than by a system, and the person is named.

A drawing you did not make is a drawing of nothing, so there is no plan, no overlay and nothing set at an angle — an angle is that same picture rebuilt out of rules. Which package we use is the least interesting fact here, and a compliance level is a scope of work rather than an achievement. A walkthrough sells a building; it does not coordinate one.
