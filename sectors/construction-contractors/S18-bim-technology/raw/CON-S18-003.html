The sixth item is drawn twice on purpose

A model of an existing building is only as true as the survey underneath it, and on a refurbishment the building is never where the drawing says it is. Without that survey the setting out is still discovered on site — and because everything else was coordinated against a wrong origin, it takes the other five back onto the site with it. That is why we will not start one without it.

Most of what goes wrong is not a clash the software missed

It is somebody modelling to a level of detail nobody agreed, an object two parties both believed the other owned, or a file issued after the decision it was meant to inform.

So the artefacts that matter are a schedule of who owns what, a level of definition against each line, and a date. They are held by a person rather than by a system, and the person is named.

Which package we use is the least interesting fact here, and a compliance level is a scope of work rather than an achievement. A walkthrough sells a building; it does not coordinate one.
