Coordination, and what it is for

Does your job need one? Sometimes the answer is no.

A coordinated model earns its cost where a lot of things have to share the same space and getting them wrong is expensive to undo. Where that is not true it is an overhead with a good reputation, and we would rather tell you that than sell you one.

  1. If it is a new build with services in it

    Yes, and the useful moment is before the frame order

    Worth it

    Plant, ductwork, drainage and structure all competing for the same voids. This is the case the whole exercise was invented for, and the value of it falls away the later it starts.

    • Services against structure, with penetrations designed in rather than cut later
    • Ceiling voids that hold what is actually going into them
    • Builder’s work openings agreed before the pour, because concrete does not have a revision
    • Riser and plant access, which needs somebody to ask a question the software will not ask
    Ask for the coordination scope

    A scope, a schedule of who owns which object, and a level of definition against each line of it.

  2. If it is a refurbishment

    Yes — after somebody has surveyed the building

    Yes, in that order

    A model of an existing building is only as true as the survey underneath it, and on a refurbishment the building is never where the drawing says it is.

    • Everything above, plus the parts of the existing structure nobody has looked behind
    • Setting out against a real origin, rather than against a drawing somebody scanned
    • Without it, the model is coordinated against the wrong position — and being confidently wrong is worse than having nothing
    Ask for the survey scope first

    We will not start one without it. That is the same rule we apply to a price.

  3. If it is a straightforward build

    No. It would cost you more than it finds

    Not worth it

    A simple frame, a shallow void, nothing much sharing a ceiling. The clashes it would find are ones two people can find with two drawings and an afternoon.

    • You would be paying for a coordination exercise to confirm what is already obvious
    • And for somebody to maintain it, which is the cost nobody quotes
    • What is worth doing instead: a coordination check on the two or three places where things do meet, and the builder’s work openings agreed properly
    Ask for the cheaper version

    It takes an afternoon and it is the part of the exercise that was doing the work.

  4. If your design team has already built one

    Then it is not a technology question

    A contract question

    The model exists. What has usually not been decided is who takes it on when construction starts, to what level, and who carries it when it turns out to be wrong.

    • Whether it was built to a level of definition that survives being built from
    • Which objects are ours from the day we start, and which stay with the designer
    • What happens to it at handover, and who updates it afterwards
    Ask for the responsibility review

    An afternoon with the schedule, before anybody signs anything about it.

And the one nobody asks about until later

An as-built model that nobody updates is a photograph of a building that has since changed. So the question of who maintains it after handover is settled before it is handed over, in writing, and if the answer is nobody we say that rather than describing it as a living record.
