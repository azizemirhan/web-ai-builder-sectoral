## Two Anti-Patterns Govern The Batch

> 4. **Stock plans, stock blueprints, stock CAD wireframe overlays.** A drawing you did not make is
>    a drawing of nothing.
> 5. **The clipboard-and-tablet montage.** Technology as a mood rather than as a capability.

Between them they remove every image this page is normally built from. **They also removed the
register:** no study in this batch is drawn at an angle. No wireframe, no isometric, no grid overlay,
no blueprint hatch, no rotation and no skew — because all of them are the stock drawing rebuilt in
CSS, and the compliance script rejects each of those properties outright.

## The Governing Constraint — It Moves Where You Find Out

    A model does not stop things going wrong. It moves the moment you find out — from
    the site to a room with the drawings still open.

That is the whole of the client-facing value, and it is expressible in the only currency this catalog
can print: **what fixing the same problem takes at each of four places.**

| Where it surfaces | What fixing it takes |
| --- | --- |
| In the model | **An email**, and an object moved |
| At the drawing review | **A re-issue**, and somebody reading it again |
| On site, in the work | **A variation, and two trades standing** while it is agreed |
| After handover | **Opening something that was signed off** |

No figures anywhere. The escalation is stated as the work it takes, which is more use to a reader
than a percentage would have been.

## The Six Things It Is Actually Done To

| Item | The point of it |
| --- | --- |
| Services against structure | A penetration designed into the beam rather than cut through it later |
| Ceiling void depth | The first thing trimmed when a budget moves, the last thing re-checked after |
| Riser and plant access | Room to lift the thing out again — **a clash the software does not flag**, so somebody has to ask |
| **Builder's work openings** | Every hole agreed before the pour, because **concrete does not have a revision** |
| **Setting out against the survey** | **A model of an existing building is only as true as the survey underneath it** |
| Who owns which object | A schedule of who models what, to what level, by when. **Not a technology question at all** |

The fifth is the caveat the rest depends on, and it is the same input `S15` refuses to price around.
This sector keeps arriving at the survey from different directions.

## Software Is Omitted, Not Reserved

The `S09` field-versus-figure test, applied again:

    Reserve the field when the empty field asserts nothing. Omit it when the empty field
    asserts a system.

A reserved slot labelled *Software* would assert that the package matters to the reader. It does not.
**Omitted in all five studies**, checked in two directions — no package named in visible copy, and no
reserved field for one either. Three studies say why: *which package we use is the least interesting
fact here, and a row of logos is a wall of badges in a different hat.*

### `002`, the narrative

The one register this catalog had not used, and the right one here: the subject is a sequence of
events, and the argument is that the same event costs four different amounts depending on where it
happens. Each account is set longer than the one before it, so the escalation is in the length of the
paragraphs as well as in what they say.

**No project is claimed.** The duct and the beam are told as a type, not as a job — `S13` owns named
cases, and blurring the two would have turned a general truth into an unverifiable anecdote.

### `003`, the relocation

The same six items, the same four rooms, drawn twice: once with nobody coordinating and once with
somebody. The reader watches five items move left, which is the whole of what is being bought.

**And the sixth does not move.** Setting out relocates only if a survey exists, so it is drawn in the
second picture in two columns at once — dashed in the model, dashed on site — with the note that
without the survey it takes the other five back onto the site with it. **An honest distribution has
to show what stays put**, and a version of this study without that exception would have been a nicer
picture and a false one.

The sector direction names this variant's trap as *the specification table.* A table would set the
six against four criteria and invite comparison between the items; these are not six comparable rows
but one set in two states, and the finding is the difference between the two pictures.

### `004`, and the answer that is no

> **Does your job need one? Sometimes the answer is no.**

Four situations: a services-heavy new build (yes, before the frame order), a refurbishment (yes,
*after* the survey), a straightforward build (**no — it would cost you more than it finds**, and here
is the afternoon's work that was doing the job anyway), and a client who already holds a model from
the design team (**not a technology question — a contract one**).

This is the eighth section in this sector to convert by handing over the less flattering answer, and
at eight it is no longer a novelty: **it is this sector's standing conversion form.** Recorded here
as that rather than as a fresh discovery.

### `005`, the poster

`S15-005`, `S16-005` and `S17-005` all set one phrase per register at display scale. A fourth would
have been a house style rather than an art direction, so this study spends the entire display budget
on **one sentence** and lets everything supporting it sit underneath in small type.

That proportion is also the argument. Anti-pattern five is *technology as a mood*; a page that
refuses spectacle completely, and then spends its one large gesture on a sentence about which room
you are standing in when a duct meets a beam, makes the refusal visible. The strip beneath says so
outright: **the evidence is deliberately dull, and underneath is the boring version, which is the
true one.**

## What Is Not On Any Of The Five Pages

- **No wireframe, plan or overlay, and nothing set at an angle.** A drawing you did not make is a
  drawing of nothing, and an angle is that same picture rebuilt out of rules.
- **No package named**, and no reserved field for one.
- **No compliance level as a badge.** It is a scope of work.
- **No fly-through.** A walkthrough sells a building; it does not coordinate one.
- **No living-record claim.** *An as-built model that nobody updates is a photograph of a building
  that has since changed*, so who maintains it is settled in writing before handover — and if the
  answer is nobody, the page says so.

## Checker Note

**No rule failed a study, and no study needed a correction after render** — the first batch in this
sector where both are true at once.

Two habits account for it, and both were learned the hard way in earlier batches:

1. **Rules written at the argument, with each study's own phrasing enumerated from the start.** The
   constraint check accepts six phrasings because `004` is a router and states the same claim as the
   condition under which the exercise earns its cost. Written to one label it would have failed the
   study that differs most, which is where fifteen batches say the failure always lands.
2. **Rules set at batch level where a study legitimately does not carry the device.** The four rooms
   and four costs are checked across the batch, not per study, because `004` is organised by
   situation rather than by room.

The new rule is the drawing check. It is the third structural rule in this sector aimed at a content
anti-pattern rather than a rendering property, after `S07`'s hi-vis hue check and `S17`'s green-hue
check. **A stock blueprint cannot be uploaded into this catalog — but it can be drawn in CSS**, and
the only place to stop that is in the stylesheet.

## Notes

- Eighteenth authored batch in the `CON` sector. `S01`–`S18` are complete.
- **The reusable outcome is that a technology page should be written in the currency of consequences,
  not capabilities.** Nobody buying a building cares which package produced a coordinated model; they
  care whether a problem arrives as an email or as a ceiling coming down. Every sector with a
  technology page can run this: name the thing that goes wrong, then name where it surfaces with and
  without the tool.
- The second outcome is `003`'s: **an honest distribution has to show what does not move.** Five of
  six items relocate; drawing the sixth in two places at once cost the study a cleaner picture and
  bought it the only claim on the page that a client could otherwise have caught us on.
- The third is `005`'s: **when three consecutive art-directed studies have used the same device, the
  device has become a house style**, and the way out is to change what the display budget is spent
  on rather than to change the words inside it.
