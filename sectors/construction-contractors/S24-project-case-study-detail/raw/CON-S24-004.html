The register of change

Ask every firm on your list for one of these.

Not for our work — for a document. Every contractor has a register of what changed between the drawing and the building on their last finished job, and asking for it sorts a shortlist faster than anything else you can ask a firm to produce.

The change register from a finished job, with who decided each entry

It is a real document, it already exists, and it takes somebody an hour to redact and send. Two answers are useful and neither requires you to trust anybody:

  • A firm that cannot produce one has not kept it, which tells you how the job was administered.
  • A firm that produces one with nothing in the fourth column has edited it, because every job has entries in that column.

And the phrase to be suspicious of

Delivered on time and on budget. It is not a claim about a project; it is a claim about a page. A job with no variations is a job nobody has looked at, and a firm that says it has never had one is telling you something about its record-keeping rather than about its buildings.

There is no price on this page, no programme figure and no pair of photographs taken in different weather. What is here is a list, and the list is checkable — which is the only property that separates a project page from an advertisement.
