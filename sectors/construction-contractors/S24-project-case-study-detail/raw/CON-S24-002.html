The register of change

This building differs from the drawings in four ways.

Set out below the way a publisher sets out a correction: what it was, what it became, and on whose authority. Without embarrassment and without boasting — which is the register a change register needs, and the one a project page almost never has.

  1. The first

    The building was not what the drawings said

    It rarely is. A survey records what somebody could see, and the parts nobody could see are the parts that move a programme. This one surfaced in the first fortnight, which is the best fortnight it could have surfaced in.

    For what the drawings showed, readWhat was found on opening upReserved
    On whose authority

    Us and the designer, inside a day.

    Paid by

    The client, and we said so before doing the work rather than after.

  2. The second

    The client changed their mind, which needs no defending

    A building is designed over a couple of years and lived in for forty. Somebody deciding late that a room should be used differently is not a failure of anything, and a contractor who treats it as one has forgotten what the building is for.

    The part worth recording is not that it happened. It is how quickly it was priced, and whether the answer arrived before the decision had to be made.

    On whose authority

    The client, at a price agreed before anybody moved.

    Paid by

    The client.

  3. The third

    A detail that worked on paper and not in the wall

    Most of these are caught in coordination, which is what coordination is for. This one was caught before anything had been ordered — which is the difference between a revision and a variation, and it is a difference of one week.

    For the detail as drawn, readThe detail that could not be built as drawnReserved
    On whose authority

    The designer, on our proposal, and the correspondence shows which of us said what.

    Paid by

    Nobody. Caught early enough that there was nothing to pay for.

  4. And the fourth

    We got it wrong

    This entry is here because a project page without one is a project page nobody has checked. We made a call, it was the wrong call, and it was ours to put right.

    What changed afterwards is the part worth reading: the way we sequence that operation is different now, and it is different on every job since rather than only on this one.

    On whose authority

    Ours, and the decision to put it right was ours as well.

    Paid by

    Us.

And what the client makes of the list

There is a space on this page for their own words, and it stays empty until they are theirs. What they are asked is not whether they were happy, which is a question with one answer, but whether the four entries above match what they remember.

The phrase this page does without is delivered on time and on budget. It is not a claim about a project; it is a claim about a page. A job with no variations is a job nobody has looked at, and every job has a list like this one — the only question is whether the firm will show it to you.
