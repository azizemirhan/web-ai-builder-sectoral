What we change, and by when

The ask is a date, not a service.

Everything a contractor can do about this has a point after which it cannot be done. So the useful question is not what we offer, it is where your project is standing right now — and what has already shut behind it.

  • If the drawings are not finished

    Bring us in before the frame is ordered

    This is the only stage at which a contractor affects the large numbers, and it is the stage at which we are usually not in the room. A buildability and substitution review, sat with your designer, costs a morning.

    Ask for the early review

    Still open to you

    • Substitutions in the specification, including build-ups we would otherwise price as drawn
    • Whether anything standing is kept rather than taken down
    • The demolition strategy, which decides reuse
    • Temporary services, logistics and the compound
    • Segregation, plant and fuel

    Already closed

    • Orientation, and what the building is for
    • Nothing else, which is why this stage is worth a morning
  • If the drawings are done and it is going out to tender

    Ask for the demolition walk before the machine is booked

    The frame is effectively fixed and so is most of the answer. What is left is real but smaller, and one item of it has a fortnight on the clock.

    Ask for the walk

    Still open to you

    • Reuse, but only until the machine arrives. After that you have rubble, and rubble is recycling at best
    • The temporary services application, which decides supply against generator
    • The logistics plan, if it is written with the programme
    • Segregation, decided at compound layout

    Already closed

    • The frame and the substructure
    • The glazing ratio
    • Most substitutions worth making — after the frame order they are variations, and a variation costs more than it saves
  • If you are already on site

    The list is short, and here it is in full

    We cannot retrofit decisions that are already built. Anybody telling you otherwise at this stage is selling you a report. What follows is everything still available, and it is worth having.

    Ask for the site review

    Still open to you

    • Segregation streams, if the compound can take another container
    • Fuel records against the plant, from now rather than backdated
    • Delivery consolidation for whatever is still to arrive
    • What is treated before it runs off the site

    Already closed

    • Everything in the two stages above
    • Reuse, once the structure is down
    • Supply against generator, once the application window has gone
  • If what you need is a number for a report

    We will send the notes, not a summary

    Including the loads that went to landfill, which is the part usually left out of the summary. A figure we assembled ourselves would be us marking our own work.

    Ask for the transfer notes

    What you get

    • Waste transfer notes, signed by the carrier and by the receiving site
    • Both registrations, so you can check them rather than take them
    • Fuel records against the plant list

    What you will not get

    • A percentage on a slide
    • A figure arrived at by purchase
    • A pledge dated past our order book

The reason the ask is a date

Most of a building's carbon is decided before a contractor is appointed, and the rest of it is decided at four or five moments that pass quietly. A page offering an environmental service without naming those moments is offering something that cannot be late, which is another way of saying it does not do anything.

We are not going to call ourselves green, eco or carbon neutral — an adjective is not a mechanism. We do not buy certificates in order to describe a job as neutral, because a figure arrived at by purchase is a purchase rather than a measurement. There is no percentage and no tonnage here; those are on the notes somebody else signed.
