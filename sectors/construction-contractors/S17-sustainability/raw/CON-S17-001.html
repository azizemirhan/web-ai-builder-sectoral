What we change, and by when

Most of it was decided before we were appointed.

The frame, the substructure, the orientation and the glazing were drawn by somebody else, and between them they hold most of a building’s carbon. What we control is what happens between the drawing and the handover — a shorter list than this kind of page usually claims, and here it is in full.

Already fixed by the time we price it

  • The frame
  • The substructure
  • The orientation
  • The glazing ratio
  • Whether anything is kept

Those are the large decisions, and we can propose against them but we cannot make them. Saying otherwise would be claiming credit for a design we were handed — and it would also hide the one thing worth telling you, which is that the window on all of them closes before a contractor is in the room.

    Segregation on site, not a mixed skip

    What it changes

    What leaves the gate as a material rather than as a load of mixed rubbish. Timber, metal, plasterboard and inert kept apart, each in its own container.

    The last point it can be decided

    At compound layout. Once the gate and the standing are set, another stream is a lorry movement problem rather than a bin, and a full skip is moved whether it was sorted or not.

    What we will not claim from it

    That it makes the building anything. It keeps plasterboard out of a mixed load, which is the expensive and the smelly problem, and it puts a name on the paperwork.

    Reuse before recycling, on the same site

    What it changes

    Whether a material stays where it is as a material — brick, steel, slate, joinery — or leaves as a lorry load and comes back as aggregate.

    The last point it can be decided

    Before the machine arrives. Once a structure is down you have rubble, and rubble is recycling at best. This is the shortest window on the page and the one most often missed.

    What we will not claim from it

    That crushing on site is reuse. It is recycling done nearer, and we will call it that. Reuse means the thing is still the thing it was.

    Substitutions we can propose in the specification

    What it changes

    A handful of build-ups where an alternative does the same job. The frame and the substructure are not among them, and they are where the weight sits.

    The last point it can be decided

    Between appointment and the order going in for the frame. Afterwards it is a variation, and a variation costs more than the substitution saves.

    What we will not claim from it

    That we designed the building. We proposed against a drawing and somebody else accepted or refused, and which of those happened is in the correspondence.

    What the site runs on

    What it changes

    Whether the compound draws from a supply or from a generator, and what the plant on site burns while it is standing as well as while it is working.

    The last point it can be decided

    The temporary services application, which goes in before mobilisation. Miss it and the site runs on a generator for the duration, and nothing said about it afterwards changes that.

    What we will not claim from it

    That a plant list is a carbon figure. It is a list of machines, and the fuel records against it are the only part anybody could check.

    Deliveries, dust and what leaves in the water

    What it changes

    The number of vehicle movements through somebody else’s street, when they happen, and whether what runs off the site is treated before it goes.

    The last point it can be decided

    The logistics plan, written with the programme rather than after it. Consolidation has to be designed into the sequence or it turns into vehicles waiting on a road.

    What we will not claim from it

    That a delivery plan is an environmental policy. It is a traffic decision that happens also to be an environmental one, and the people it affects most are the ones who live there.

    What gets measured, and who signs it

    What it changes

    Nothing on its own — which is worth saying, because measurement is the part of this subject most often presented as an achievement.

    The last point it can be decided

    Continuous, and that is the difficulty. A record kept from the first week is evidence; a record assembled at the end is a reconstruction, and the difference is visible in it.

    What we will not claim from it

    That our own figures are evidence. The waste transfer notes are, because somebody outside this firm signs them. Our spreadsheet is us marking our own work.

The only environmental record on a site that somebody outside the firm signs

Every other number on this subject is produced by the party it flatters. Waste transfer notes are not — a carrier signs them, a receiving site signs them, and both of those are registered with somebody who can be asked.

And five things we are not going to say

  • Not green
  • Not eco
  • Not carbon neutral
  • No offsetting
  • No target past our order book

An adjective is not a mechanism, so none of them is applied to this firm anywhere above. We do not buy certificates in order to call a job neutral — a figure arrived at by purchase is a purchase rather than a measurement, and it would tell you nothing about how the work was done. And a target dated further out than the work we currently hold is a press release: the windows on this page all close inside the job.
