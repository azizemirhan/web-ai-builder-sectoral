# Section Role

What this contractor actually changes about the environmental cost of a building, the point in the
programme after which each of those things can no longer be changed, and what will not be claimed
from any of it.

# The Rule Was Set In Advance

Anti-pattern twelve, from the sector direction:

> **The sustainability claim without a mechanism** — *green*, *eco*, *carbon neutral* as adjectives.
> `S17` states practices, never labels.

Together with the counter rule — every figure in this sector is a reserved field — that removes both
halves of the ordinary version of this page: the adjective and the percentage. What is left is the
only part that was ever load-bearing.

# The Governing Constraint — Most Of It Was Decided Before We Arrived

    Most of a building's carbon is decided before a contractor is appointed. The frame,
    the substructure, the orientation and the glazing were drawn by somebody else. What
    we control is what happens between the drawing and the handover, and that is a
    shorter list than these pages usually claim.

This is the single most useful true thing a contractor can put on a sustainability page, and it is
the opposite of what one usually says. It also generates all the content: **name what can still be
changed, and the point after which it cannot.**

# The Device — Practice, Window, And The Claim We Will Not Make

Every item carries three parts:

| What it actually changes | The last point it can be decided | What we will not claim from it |

The first is the mechanism the anti-pattern demands. The second is `S06`'s gate, arriving where it
bites hardest — **an environmental practice with no deadline attached is an intention.** The third is
the antibody: each item inoculates itself against its own overclaim, in its own words.

# The Six Practices

| Practice | The window | Will not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Segregation on site | At compound layout. Afterwards, another stream is a lorry movement problem | That it makes the building green. It keeps plasterboard out of a mixed load |
| **Reuse before recycling** | **Before the machine arrives.** Once a structure is down you have rubble | **That crushing on site is reuse.** It is recycling, and we call it that |
| Substitutions in the specification | Between appointment and the frame order. Afterwards it is a variation that costs more than it saves | That we designed the building |
| Plant, fuel and temporary power | The temporary services application, before mobilisation. Miss it and the site runs on a generator for the duration | That a plant list is a carbon figure |
| Deliveries, dust and water | The logistics plan, written with the programme | That a delivery plan is an environmental policy |
| **What is measured, and who signs it** | Continuous | **That our own figures are evidence.** The waste transfer notes are; our spreadsheet is not |

# The Verification Move, Carried From S08 And S16

    Waste transfer notes are the only environmental record on a construction site that
    somebody outside the firm signs. Everything else is us marking our own work.

Same shape as `S08`'s registration you can check and `S16`'s document somebody else dated. The
transfer note reference is a **reserved field**; the argument about who signs it is real.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No adjective for ourselves.** Not *green*, not *eco*, not *sustainable*, not *environmentally
  responsible*. Anti-pattern twelve.
- **No offsetting.** We do not buy certificates in order to describe a job as neutral. A figure
  arrived at by purchase is a purchase, not a measurement.
- **No percentage, and no figure of any kind.** The diverted-from-landfill number is on the transfer
  notes, which somebody else signed — `S16`'s rule about where figures belong.
- **No pledge dated past our order book.** A target for a year further out than the work we hold is
  a press release.
- **No photograph of a tree, a turbine or a solar panel we did not install.** The subject is waste,
  fuel and lorries.

# No Green Anywhere In The Batch

A deliberate art-direction rule, and a checkable one: **no study in this batch uses a green hue.**
The page is about skips, diesel and delivery slots; colouring it the colour of foliage would be the
adjective smuggled back in through the palette. The three device tones are a blue for *what it
changes*, an amber for *the window*, and a red for *what we will not claim*.
