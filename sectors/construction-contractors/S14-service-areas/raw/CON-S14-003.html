Where we work

A service area does not end at a line. It degrades.

So the further out each band sits, the less we can promise and the more space it takes to say so. The shape of this page is the argument.

  • Band one

    Around the yard

    • RegionsRegions in band one—
    • YardLocation of the yard—
    • The mornings

      Every morning, and the manager goes home at night.

    • The plant

      Off our own yard, on our own low-loader.

    • When it breaks

      Our fitter, the same day.

    What you lose

    Nothing. Travel is not a line on your price; this is where we work.

  • Band two

    The next ring out

    • RegionsRegions in band two—
    • The mornings

      Still every morning, and somebody’s day is longer for it.

    • The plant

      Ours where it travels sensibly, hired locally where it does not.

    • When it breaks

      Our fitter, but a drive rather than a visit.

    What you lose

    A little of the day, and some certainty about which machine turns up. Travel is priced here and shown as its own line rather than folded into a rate, so you can see what it costs you.

  • Band three

    Further than that

    • RegionsRegions in band three—
    • ConditionsConditions we would attach—
    • The mornings

      Most mornings, not every morning.

    • The plant

      Hired locally. A hire desk rather than our fitter, on their timescale.

    What you lose

    Daily attendance, which is the thing you were buying. We will travel this far for the right job and we are worse at it than a firm based there. If yours is only worth doing with somebody on it every day, say so and we will not bid — a better outcome for you than a competitive price and a manager who is somewhere else on Tuesday.

  • Beyond that

    We do not bid

    • Not coveredRegions we do not cover—
    • What we do instead

      Name a firm nearer to you, where we know one.

    And this is the biggest block on the page for a reason

    A contractor claiming to cover a whole country is either very large or not telling the truth, and the ones in the middle simply cost a client more in travel and less in attendance. Everything above degrades gradually; this is where it stops, and a page that does not draw the stopping point is asking you to assume there isn’t one.

Reserved area — a map, if the finished page carries one. No pins

Deliberately the smallest thing on this page

  • No pins
  • No radius
  • No mileage
  • No drive times
  • No nationwide claim

A row of pins says we work here at a glance and supports none of it — it does not say whether anybody from this firm has been to most of those places this year, which is exactly what the pins are being used to imply.

No distances either. A mile of motorway and a mile across a city are different jobs, and neither tells you whether somebody will be standing on your site at seven in the morning.
