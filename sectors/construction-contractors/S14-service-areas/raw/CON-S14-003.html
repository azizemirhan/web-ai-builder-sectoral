Where we work

A service area does not end at a line. It degrades.

So the further out each band sits, the less we can promise and the more space it takes to say so. The shape of this page is the argument.

And this is the biggest block on the page for a reason

A contractor claiming to cover a whole country is either very large or not telling the truth, and the ones in the middle simply cost a client more in travel and less in attendance. Everything above degrades gradually; this is where it stops, and a page that does not draw the stopping point is asking you to assume there isn’t one.

Reserved area — a map, if the finished page carries one. No pins

Deliberately the smallest thing on this page

  • No pins
  • No radius
  • No mileage
  • No drive times
  • No nationwide claim

A row of pins says we work here at a glance and supports none of it — it does not say whether anybody from this firm has been to most of those places this year, which is exactly what the pins are being used to imply.

No distances either. A mile of motorway and a mile across a city are different jobs, and neither tells you whether somebody will be standing on your site at seven in the morning.

Width is what it takes to say what you lose
