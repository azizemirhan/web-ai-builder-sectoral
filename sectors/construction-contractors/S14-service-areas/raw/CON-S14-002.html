Where we work

The map is the least useful thing we could show you.

It is reserved here at the size a finished page would give it, and the column beside it is the reason. A service area is not a shape on a map. It is the distance a site manager can cover and still be on your site every morning — which is a different question, and one an outline cannot answer.

What a map tells you, and what it does not

It tells you a shape. What it implies is that somebody from this firm has recently been everywhere inside it, and nothing on the page supports that — which is why a row of pins is the most common piece of unearned confidence in this industry.

There are no distances on this page either. A mile of motorway and a mile across a city are different jobs, and neither says whether anybody will be standing on your site at seven in the morning.

The honest version of this page is a list of things that get worse as you move away from us, which is what follows. There is no version of it in which the answer is nationwide.

Band one

Around the yard, where nothing is lost

The site manager is on your site every morning and goes home at night. The plant comes off our own yard on our own low-loader, and when a machine stops it is our fitter who attends, the same day.

Travel is not a line on your price here. It is where we work, and it is already in our overhead. If a contractor is charging you travel to reach its own back yard, that is worth asking about.

Band two

The next ring out, where the day starts earlier

Still every morning. What changes is whose day is longer, and the fact that plant is ours where it travels sensibly and hired locally where it does not.

When something breaks, our fitter still comes, but it is a drive rather than a visit, and you should allow for that rather than be surprised by it. Travel is priced here and shown as its own line rather than folded into a rate, so you can see what it costs you.

Band three

Further than that, where attendance stops being daily

Most mornings, not every morning. That is the whole of what you lose out here, and it is the only thing worth knowing. Plant is hired locally and we are not the ones choosing it; when it stops, a hire desk attends on its own timescale rather than ours.

We will travel this far for the right job, and we are worse at it than a firm based there. If yours is only worth doing with somebody on it daily, say so and we will not bid — which is a better outcome for you than a competitive price and a manager who is somewhere else on a Tuesday.

Beyond that

We do not bid, and we will say who might

A contractor claiming to cover a whole country is either very large or not telling the truth, and the ones in the middle simply cost a client more in travel and less in attendance.

We would rather lose the enquiry than be the second of those, so beyond the last band we decline and name a firm nearer you where we know one.

So the useful question is not do you cover us. It is which band are we in, and what does that cost me.

Send us where the site is and you will get the band, what changes at it, and whether we think a firm nearer you would serve you better. That last answer is the one worth having, and it is the one a map has never given anybody.
