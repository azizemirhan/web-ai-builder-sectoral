Where we work

The page gets darker as the attendance gets worse.

There is no map on this page and no pins. The only honest picture of a service area is a gradient, and the sentence about what you lose grows the further down you read.

  1. Band one

    One

    Around the yard

    You lose nothing.

    The site manager is on your site every morning and goes home at night. Plant comes off our own yard on our own low-loader, and when a machine stops it is our fitter who attends the same day.

    Travel is not a line on your price out here. It is where we work, and it is already in the overhead.

    The next ring out

    You lose some of the day, and some certainty about the plant.

    Still every morning — what changes is whose day is longer. Plant is ours where it travels sensibly and hired locally where it does not, so you are no longer guaranteed the machine we would have picked.

    Our fitter still comes, but it is a drive rather than a visit. Travel is priced here and shown as its own line rather than folded into a rate.

    Further than that

    You lose daily attendance, which is the thing you were buying.

    Most mornings, not every morning. Plant is hired locally and we are not the ones choosing it; when it stops, a hire desk attends on its timescale rather than ours.

    We will travel this far for the right job, and we are worse at it than a firm based there. If your job is only worth doing with somebody on it daily, say so and we will not bid.

  4. Beyond that

    We do not bid

    And here it stops, which is the part no coverage page draws.

    A contractor claiming to cover a whole country is either very large or not telling the truth, and the ones in the middle simply cost a client more in travel and less in attendance.

    We would rather lose the enquiry than be the second of those. Beyond this band we decline, and name a firm nearer you where we know one.

Why there is no map here at all

The other honest version of this page reserves one and argues with it. At this size even an empty map would become the subject, and the subject is attendance rather than territory.

A row of pins says we work here at a glance and supports none of it — it does not say whether anybody from this firm has been to most of those places this year.

  • No pins
  • No map
  • No radius
  • No mileage
  • No drive times
  • No nationwide claim

And no distances. A mile of motorway and a mile across a city are different jobs, and neither tells you whether somebody will be standing on your site at seven in the morning. The gradient above does.
