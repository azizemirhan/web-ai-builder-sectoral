# Section Role

The documents this firm will send you, what is in each one, who each is for, and the date on which
each stops being true.

# The Governing Constraint — The Figures Are In The Documents

    Every figure worth having is in one of these documents, and none of them is on this
    page. A number in a signed document has a date on it. A number on a web page does not.

This sector reserves every figure it prints. This section is the one place where that restriction
stops being a restriction and becomes the argument: **a cover limit, a payment term, a retention
period and a registration number all belong in a dated, signed, versioned document** — and a firm
that puts them on a web page instead has removed the only two things that made them checkable.

So the page holds no figures at all, and says why.

# The Device — Three Things Per Document

Parallel to `S13`'s *what we found / what we chose / what it cost us*:

| What is in it | Who it is for | When it stops being true |

The third column is the section's content. Every document library in this industry prints a title
and an icon; **the date of expiry is the thing that decides whether the document is worth
downloading, and it is the thing nobody prints.**

Note the distinction the studies hold to: **the rule that makes a document go stale is authorable;
the date is a reserved field.** *Reissued at the end of every quarter* is real. *Issued 14 March* is
not ours to invent.

# The Six Documents

| Document | Who it is for | Stops being true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability statement | Somebody deciding whether to have a conversation | When a project completes or somebody leaves |
| Prequalification pack | A procurement team filling in a matrix | When an accreditation is reassessed |
| Insurance summary | Anybody satisfying themselves before we come on site | **On the renewal date printed on the front** |
| Health, safety and environmental policy | Your compliance file | When the law changes, or when the person who signed it is no longer the person responsible |
| Method statement and risk assessment, the generic ones | Somebody who wants to see the shape before we write yours | **Immediately, for your job** |
| Payment terms for our supply chain | A client who wants to know whether a price is being held up by somebody who has not been paid | When we change them, which we have done once |

The last of the six is the one most contractors do not publish. It is included for that reason.

# The Gate, Named Rather Than Hidden

Two of the six are behind a request, and the page says which and why:

> **The ones you need in order to decide whether to talk to us are not gated.** We gate the two that
> go into somebody's tender file, because we have to know which version you are holding — an answer
> you quote back to us has to match the one we can evidence.

And the generic method statement is gated for a different reason:

> **A template sent without that sentence attached gets used as if it were the real one.**

Lead capture is not among the reasons, and the page says so.

# The Signature Rule

Carried from `S07`'s *who holds it*:

    An unsigned policy is a document nobody owns.

The health, safety and environmental policy stops being true when the person who signed it stops
being the person responsible — which is a stronger statement of currency than a date, and is the
reason the signature block matters more than the cover.

# What This Section Will Not Do

- **No brochure.** A brochure is a document made to be handed out rather than read.
- **No certificate images.** `S08` gives the registration numbers; a scan of a certificate proves
  less than a number a reader can check. Anti-pattern two arriving as a download list.
- **No gated capability statement.** Anyone deciding whether to talk to us should be able to find
  out what we do without giving us an email address.
- **No undated document.** If there is no issue date on the cover it does not go on this page.
- **No download count, no *most requested*, no lead magnet.**

# One Mechanism, In One Study

The sector's habit holds: **`004` carries the batch's only anchors**, and they are in-page routing
actions, never a link to a file. No study carries a form — `S15-004` holds this sector's submission
and `S05-004` its resource check.

**No study links to a document.** The file location is a reserved field in every study, because a
link to a document that does not exist is precisely the dishonesty this section is about.
