What we will send you

The title is the small print. The date it stops being true is the document.

Every library in this trade sets the name of the file largest and leaves the date off altogether. Here they are the other way round, because the rule by which a document goes out of date is the only thing on this page that tells you whether it is worth opening.

  1. Payment terms for our supply chain

    Open to anybody
    Stops being true

    When we change them, which we have done once.

    When our subcontractors are paid, what we hold back and for how long. The version before this one is underneath, in the same document, so you can see what moved rather than take our word for it.

    For a client wanting to know whether a price is propped up by somebody downstream who has not been paid. Most firms in this trade do not publish this one.

  2. Health, safety and environmental policy

    Open to anybody
    Stops being true

    When the person who signed it is no longer the person responsible.

    Which is a stronger test than a date. The signed policy carries a name against each responsibility rather than a department, because an unsigned policy is a document nobody owns.

    For your compliance file, and for whoever has to answer for what is in it.

  3. Capability statement

    Open to anybody
    Stops being true

    When a project completes, or when somebody leaves.

    What we build, at what size and in which sectors — and the four kinds of work we turn down, which is the part that saves you a meeting. Reissued at the end of every quarter.

    For somebody deciding whether to have a conversation with us at all. Not gated, because working out what a firm does should not cost an email address.

  4. Prequalification pack

    Sent on request
    Stops being true

    When an accreditation is reassessed, on a date the assessor sets.

    The standard answers to the standard questions, each carrying the registration you can check for yourself rather than a certificate you would have to take from us. Every answer also carries the date it was written.

    For a procurement team filling in a matrix who would rather not chase us line by line.

  5. Insurance summary

    Open to anybody
    Stops being true

    On the renewal date printed on its cover.

    After that it is a historical record and should come out of your file rather than sit in it. The cover, the limit against each and the renewal date are all in the document — which is where figures belong.

    For anybody satisfying themselves before we set foot on their site. Checking us is doing us a favour.

  6. Method statement and risk assessment, the generic ones

    Sent on request
    Stops being true

    Immediately, for your job.

    It is a sample of our handwriting, not a plan for your site, and the real one is written after somebody has stood on the ground. A template sent without that sentence attached gets used as though it were the real thing — which is the whole reason this one is on request.

    For somebody who wants to see the shape of one before we write yours. Sent with a covering note rather than on its own.

Why two of the six are sent rather than posted

Both of them end up in somebody’s tender file, and we have to know which version you are holding — an answer you quote back to us has to match the one we can evidence.

The other reason a firm gates a document is to collect an email address. A document gated for that is one nobody wanted to write and nobody wants to read, and it is not a reason we use.

Every figure worth having is inside one of these and none of them is on this page, because a number in a signed document has a date, a revision and a name against it, and a number on a web page has none of the three. There is no brochure here, which is a document made to be handed out rather than read, and there are no scanned certificates — a scan proves less than a registration you can check yourself. Nothing undated goes on this page at all, and there is no count of who else has opened what: that tells you what other people did, not whether the document answers your question.
