What we will send you

A document is a promise with a date on it.

Which is why every figure worth having is in one of these and none of them is on this page. Take the date away and you are left with a claim about a firm that may no longer exist in that form.

Somewhere on this site there is a page listing our documents as a row of identical icons, and it tells you nothing except that we own a printer.

This is that page rewritten. Each entry says what is in the document, who we wrote it for, and the rule by which it stops being true — because that last one is what decides whether it is worth the minute it takes you to open it.

The issue dates themselves are on the covers rather than here, and so are the limits, the terms and the periods. A number in a signed document has a date, a revision and a name against it. A number on a web page has none of the three, and can be quietly edited by whoever has the login.

Two of the six are sent on request. The reason is written below, and it is not to collect your email address.

Why two of them are on request

Because those two end up in somebody's tender file, and we have to know which version you are holding. An answer you quote back to us has to match the one we can evidence, and that is impossible if we do not know which printing you took.

The other reason firms gate a document is to collect an email address. A document gated for that is a document nobody wanted to write and nobody wants to read, and it is not a reason we use.

And what is not here

No brochure, which is a document made to be handed out rather than read. No scanned certificates — a scan proves less than a registration you can check yourself, which is why we list the registrations instead.

Nothing undated. If there is no issue date on the cover it does not go on this page. And no count of how many people have downloaded something: that tells you what other people did, not whether it answers your question.
