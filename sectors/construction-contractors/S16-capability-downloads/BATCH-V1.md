## The Governing Constraint — The Figures Are In The Documents

    Every figure worth having is in one of these documents, and none of them is on this
    page. A number in a signed document has a date on it, a revision, and somebody's
    name against it. A number on a web page has none of the three.

This sector reserves every figure it prints, and for fifteen sections that has been a restriction to
work around. Here it stopped being one. **A cover limit, a payment term, a retention period and a
registration number all belong in a dated, signed, versioned document** — and a firm that puts them
on a web page instead has removed the two things that made them checkable.

So the page holds no figures at all, and says why. That is the fifth or sixth time in this sector
that the catalog's own restriction has produced the stronger page rather than a compromised one.

## The Device — Three Things Per Document

Parallel to `S13`'s *what we found / what we chose / what it cost us*:

| What is in it | Who it is for | **When it stops being true** |

Every document library in this industry prints a title and an icon. **The date of expiry is what
decides whether a document is worth opening, and it is the thing nobody prints.**

The studies hold a distinction throughout: **the rule that makes a document go stale is authorable;
the date is a reserved field.** *Reissued at the end of every quarter* is real and is ours to write.
*Issued on the fourteenth* is not.

## The Six Documents, And What Kills Each One

| Document | Gate | Stops being true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability statement | Open | When a project completes or somebody leaves |
| Prequalification pack | On request | When an accreditation is reassessed |
| Insurance summary | Open | **On the renewal date printed on the front** |
| Health, safety and environmental policy | Open | **When the person who signed it is no longer the person responsible** |
| Method statement and risk assessment, the generic ones | On request | **Immediately, for your job** |
| Payment terms for our supply chain | Open | When we change them, which we have done once |

The last of the six is the one most contractors do not publish. It is in the batch for that reason,
and every study says so on its face.

## The Gate, Named Rather Than Hidden

> **The ones you need in order to decide whether to talk to us are not gated.** Two are sent on
> request, because they end up in somebody's tender file and **we have to know which version you are
> holding** — an answer you quote back to us has to match the one we can evidence.

And, in every study, the reason that is *not* used:

> Collecting an email address. **A document gated for that is one nobody wanted to write and nobody
> wants to read.**

Naming both halves is the section's honesty move. A page that gates silently is making the same
trade and hoping the reader does not notice.

### `002`, the colophon

Not the letter of `S15-002`. This one borrows the register a publisher uses for its editions: what
each contains, who it was set for, and which printing supersedes it. **A document library is a
shelf**, and a shelf is editorial furniture rather than a grid of tiles.

Its expiry rules are set as italic pull-quotes at the weight of the document titles — deliberately
short of `005`'s full inversion, because an editorial page keeps a title a title.

### `003`, sorted by what kills it

Every library in this industry is ordered by document type, which tells a reader nothing. This one
is ordered by **what makes each document stop being true**: a date somebody else set, a change we
make, or contact with your job. The bands hold two, three and one.

The sector direction names this variant's trap as *the specification table.* A table asserts that
six documents are six comparable rows; these are not — one expires on a printed date and one expires
the moment you open it — and the uneven bands say so before any of the copy does.

**No shelf-life bar and no expiry meter.** A bar of relative length would assert that one document
lasts twice as long as another, which is a quantity nobody here can support — the `S09` test,
applied to a chart rather than to a counter.

### `004`, and the two routes that end in nothing

> **Do not download all six. Tell us what you are trying to find out.**

A library that sends a reader away with six PDFs has converted nobody; it has delegated the work of
choosing. Seven routes name the one document that answers each question — and **two of them end in
*there is no document for that*:**

- *See how we would sequence the work on your site* → **ask for the site walk.** The generic method
  statement exists and will be sent, but read on its own it misleads.
- *Find out what we would charge* → **there is not going to be one.** No rate card, no schedule of
  prices. That is `S15`'s page, and a price is a conversation rather than a file.

That is **the sixth section in this sector to convert by handing over the less flattering answer**,
after `S09`, `S12`, `S13`, `S14` and `S15`. It is also the batch's only study with anchors, and they
route in-page: no study in this batch links to a file.

### `005`, the inversion

**The title is the small print. The date it stops being true is the document.** Every download page
in this trade sets the file name largest and omits the date; this study sets the rule largest and
the name smallest, which makes the section's argument typographic rather than stated.

The six are ordered longest-lived first and the type grows as the shelf life shortens, ending on
*Immediately, for your job* in coral at the largest size on the page. **That is an ordering, not a
scale** — nothing claims one document lasts twice as long as another.

Its reserved fields sit once, at ordinary scale, in the foot strip. Not enlarged: the lesson from
`S08-005`, `S09-005` and `S15-005` is that a display-scale em-dash reads as a redaction. The display
scale belongs to the rules, which are real.

## What Is Not On Any Of The Five Pages

- **No brochure.** A document made to be handed out rather than read.
- **No scanned certificates.** `S08` gives the registrations; a scan proves less than a number a
  reader can check. Anti-pattern two arriving as a download list.
- **No undated document.** If there is no issue date on the cover it does not go on the page.
- **No download count, no *most requested*, no free guide, no *enter your email*.**
- **No link to a file.** The file location is a reserved field in every study, because a link to a
  document that does not exist is precisely the dishonesty this section is about.

Each study declares those inside a `data-refusal` region, so the hard vocabulary scan can run on the
page with its own refusals removed.

The `002` correction is the more interesting one. **The defect was not that the type was the wrong
size; it was that the file's own comment claimed a hierarchy the CSS did not build.** A study whose
note and rendering disagree is worse than one that never claimed anything, because the note is what
a later reader trusts. Both were brought into line rather than one silently dropped.

## Checker Note

No rule failed a study for a defect in the rule this time — the first batch in this sector where
that is true. The reason is that two rules were written at batch level *from the start*, having
learned what happens when they are not:

- **The six expiry rules** are checked across the batch rather than per study, because `004` is a
  router and states the currency rule once, as a condition of appearing on the page at all.
- **The currency argument itself** is checked as an argument (five accepted phrasings) rather than
  as the label *stops being true*, which four studies use and the fifth does not.

Written per-study and keyed to the label, both would have failed `004` — the study that differs
most, which is where fifteen batches of this catalog say the failure always lands.

## Notes

- Sixteenth authored batch in the `CON` sector. `S01`–`S16` are complete.
- **The reusable outcome is that a download page is about currency, not about contents.** What is in
  a document is guessable from its title; whether it is still true is not, and it is the only thing
  that decides whether the reader should spend a minute on it. Every sector with a document library
  can run this: three lines per document, and the third is the one nobody writes.
- The second outcome is the constraint turning into the argument. **A catalog that may not print
  figures found the honest reason not to:** figures belong in dated, signed, versioned documents,
  and a web page strips all three properties off them. That reframing was available for fifteen
  sections and only became visible when the section's subject was documents.
- The third is `004`'s: **a library's job is to answer a question, not to have something for every
  question.** Two of its seven routes end in nothing to download, and both are the useful answer.
