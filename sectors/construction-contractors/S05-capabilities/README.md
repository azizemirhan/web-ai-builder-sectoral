# Section Role

What the firm actually holds: the trades on its own books, the plant in its own yard, and the
technical people who write the programme and price the job.

`S02` answered *can you do this one*. This section answers the question directly behind it:
**with what?**

# The Governing Constraint — The Depth Is Reserved. What Happens Beyond It Is Real.

Every contractor's capabilities page says *in-house*. The word is free, so it persuades nobody, and
the two things that would make it mean something are exactly the two this catalog cannot invent: how
many people, and how much plant.

The sector's counter rule already decides the first half — **people employed is a reserved field**,
a countable fact the firm knows and the catalog must not make up. What it does not decide is what
fills the space, and the answer is the part almost no competitor prints:

    Every contractor says "in-house". Almost none says how deep it goes,
    or what happens when the job is deeper than they are.

So each capability in this batch carries two things, and the second is the section's whole value:

1. **How it is held** — a four-value marker, the resource-level sibling of the `S02` delivery marker.
2. **Where it runs out** — the depth itself is a reserved field, and **the consequence of reaching
   it is real copy.**

| Marker | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `Directly employed` | On our books, on our insurance, under our supervision |
| `Owned outright` | In our yard, maintained by us |
| `Long-term hire` | Not ours, but the same units and the same operators each time |
| `Retained specialist` | Not ours at all, and the same firm every job rather than the cheapest that month |

**The depth is a reserved field. What happens beyond it is real.**

*How many gangs* is a countable fact and is therefore reserved. *"Beyond that we phase the sequence
rather than put a gang we have not worked with on your site"* is a policy — authorable, checkable,
and impossible for a firm that does not operate that way to copy. That is the sector's copy test
applied to a resource list.

# What Is Real, And What May Not Be Invented

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Trade, plant and technical function names | **Real** — generic vocabulary |
| How each is held | **Real** — the four-value marker |
| What happens at the limit | **Real** — a stated operating policy |
| What the firm does not hold at all | **Real, and kept** — the steer-away |
| Number of people, gangs, machines, offices | **Reserved fields** — the counter rule |
| Utilisation, availability or capacity percentage | **Omitted** — asserts a measurement system |
| Plant make, model, age, hours, fleet value | **Omitted** — an invented figure per line |
| Certification, scheme, operator card or registration | **Forbidden** — the sector direction |
| Client or project names | **Forbidden** |
| Safety record of any kind | **Forbidden** |
| Site, plant and yard media | **Reserved areas** |

**Not present in any study:** an invented figure of any kind; a percentage; a price, rate or day
rate; a certification, accreditation, scheme or operator card; a client or project name; a safety
record; an award, rating or review; an urgency device; a stock handshake or posed PPE group.

# The Reserved Depth Is A Field, Not A Counter

`S09 Company Stats` owns the counter. A capabilities section that sets its reserved numbers at
display scale has written a stats band and put a heading on it, and the two sections then collide.

So in every study here the depth field is **inline and subordinate** — captioned, at body scale,
sitting inside the capability it belongs to and never in a row of its own. The eye should land on
the holding marker and the consequence sentence; the reserved number is the smallest thing in the
block.

This is the `WELL-S11` field-versus-figure test carried one step further: the field is reserved
*and* demoted, because its prominence is what would turn it into a claim.

# The Three Families

The resource splits three ways, and every study groups by it rather than listing fifteen flat lines:

| Family | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Trades** | Groundworks and drainage, concrete frame and formwork, carpentry, brickwork, steel, M&E |
| **Plant** | Excavation and earthmoving, access and handling, formwork systems, survey instruments |
| **Technical** | Planning and programming, commercial and quantity surveying, site engineering, temporary works design, site management |

The families are also what keeps this section off `S10 Equipment Fleet`'s territory. Plant is one
of three families here and gets a line each; `S10` is the plant itself, at length.

# The Steer-Away Is Kept

Carried through from `S02` and `S04`, and this section is where it costs the most and therefore
proves the most. Named as not held, in plain words:

- **M&E** — retained, never claimed as ours
- **Specialist façade and curtain walling** — not held
- **Asbestos removal** — licensed work, not ours
- **Lift installation and commissioning** — not held

A capabilities page with no gaps in it is a claim of a firm that does not exist, and every reader
who has run a project knows it.

    S02 is the list. S05 is the resource behind it. S10 is one family of that resource in detail.

The test against `S02`: every line here must start from something the firm **owns or employs**, not
something a client wants **done**. A line that could move to `S02` unchanged has written a service.
The test against `S09`: remove the reserved numbers entirely and this section must still make its
whole argument. It does, because the argument is carried by the marker and the consequence.
