What clients said

Ask for the ones who are not on this page.

A testimonials page is a selected sample and everybody knows it. These are the clients who agreed to be quoted; the ones who did not are missing, and we are not going to pretend the difference is random. So the useful offer is the part that was selected out.

You get

A job that went badly

Not a difficult one that came right in the end. One where the client would tell you they were let down, and we will name it rather than leave you to guess which of the good ones is hiding something.

You get

The client’s number, unrehearsed

We do not ring them first. There is no version of this where we call ahead to explain what is coming, because a prepared reference is not a reference.

You choose when to call and what to ask.

You get

Our account of it, before you ring

What we think went wrong and what we changed afterwards, in writing, before you speak to them — so you can compare the two versions.

That comparison is the whole value of the exercise, and it is the part we cannot control.

It is one email, and it is the only reference worth taking

Every firm on your list will give you three happy clients. Ask all of them for an unhappy one instead, and see which of them can.

We would rather you tested us on this now than found out in month three that the page you were reading had been curated by somebody whose job was to curate it.

What we will not do

We will not send you somebody we have primed, and we will not pick the least bad of the bad ones and call it candour.

If we have not had a job go badly recently enough to be useful, we will say that too — and you should treat it with the suspicion it deserves.

There is no rating on this page, no stars, no review count and no client logos. A score asserts a survey nobody ran, and an average of a handful of quotations is a handful of quotations with arithmetic on top. What is here instead is the apparatus — who said it, whether you may ring them, what was cut, what they were given — and an offer that costs us something if we are wrong about ourselves.
