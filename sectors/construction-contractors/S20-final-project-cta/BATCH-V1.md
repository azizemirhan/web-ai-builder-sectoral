## The Governing Constraint — A Close Is A Commitment, Not A Button

    A closing section is not a button. It is the last thing we say before you decide,
    so it should be the thing we are prepared to be held to.

Every closing band in this trade asks the reader to do something. **These state what the firm will
do**, in terms specific enough to be broken visibly.

## The Five Undertakings

| We will | You would see it broken when |
| --- | --- |
| **Come and look at it before we say anything about money** | A quotation arrives from somebody who has never seen the site |
| **Send the person who would run it**, not the person who wins work | You meet somebody once, at the start, and never again |
| **Say at the walk if we are the wrong firm**, and name somebody better suited | Nobody ever says no, and you work it out from a price that reads strangely |
| **Say on the day if your fixed start cannot be met** | The programme becomes impossible in week six |
| **Not chase you.** One call after the visit | A sequence of emails from somebody who was not at the visit |

## The Device — Every Undertaking Carries The Question That Tests It

    A promise nobody can check is decoration.

So each is published with the question to put to whoever turns up:

> *Who is coming, and have they seen it?* · *Will you be on this job, and who else is?* · *Is this
> the sort of job you turn down?* · *What would have to be true for this start to hold?* · *What
> happens if I do not reply?*

All five questions appear in all five studies, and the compliance script counts them. It is `S18`'s
consequence device turned around: instead of naming what the firm will not claim, **this section
hands the reader the test.**

## Capacity, Stated Only Where It Can Be Bad News

Anti-pattern seven bans the countdown. The polite version of it — *our order book is filling* — is
banned here too, and named as such in every study's refusal. What replaces it is the same fact
pointed the other way:

> **If your fixed start cannot be met, you hear it on the day rather than in week six.**

Which is the `S06` gate arriving in a closing band, and the opposite of scarcity: it is capacity as a
thing that can disqualify the firm rather than pressure the reader.

### `002`, the declaration

Not the letter of `S15-002`, the colophon of `S16-002`, the marginalia of `S17-002`, the narrative of
`S18-002` or the people-list of `S19-002`. **A declaration** — the plain first-person-plural voice of
a firm putting its name to something, each undertaking numbered in words.

It is the only register that suits a page whose content is a set of promises, and it avoids legalese
by the same means the rest of the batch does: every clause is written so a reader could see it
broken.

### `003`, the unequal halves

Each row carries the sentence a closing band normally offers — *get your free, no-obligation quote
today*; *our expert team is ready to help*; *no job too large or too small* — set small, dashed and
grey, beside the undertaking that replaces it, set large and solid.

**None of the small ones could be broken, which is why they are all still in use.** The proportion
says that before any of the copy does. The five quoted lines are declared as `data-refusal` regions,
which is the correct use of that mechanism: the study is naming what it refuses, in the industry's
own words.

The sector direction names this variant's trap as *the specification table.* A table would set the
columns equal and invite comparison; these are not comparable, and the drawing says so.

### `004`, and the three reasons not to book it

> **There is one thing to ask for, and it is a morning.**

Not a quote, not a callback, not a download. And three dashed cards give the cases where a visit
would waste a morning on both sides — nothing drawn and nobody deciding; a fixed start too close to
be met; further out than our people can reach every morning — each with the cheaper thing to do
instead.

**A conversion section that cannot say *not yet* is a booking form with paragraphs around it.** That
is the ninth section in this sector to convert by handing over the less flattering answer.

### `005`, the litany

Five stanzas of exactly the same shape: the undertaking at display scale, and beneath it, in the same
position every time, how it would visibly break and the question that tests it.

`S15-005` set three words at display scale, `S16-005` inverted a hierarchy, `S17-005` ran a spine,
`S18-005` spent everything on one sentence and `S19-005` used a descending ramp. **This one uses
repetition** — by the second stanza the reader knows where to look, and by the fifth is reading
undertakings rather than claims. A page of promises should look like a page of promises.

## The Fewest Reserved Fields In The Sector

`S19` is almost entirely fields; this section is almost none. **Exactly two per study**, checked as an
equality rather than a floor: the person who would come, and where to ask for the walk.

    A commitment does not need a number attached to it.

The two sections were written consecutively and are the two ends of the same rule — `S19` needed a
density floor to stop the fields eating the page, and `S20` needed a cap to stop them arriving at
all.

## Checker Note

No rule failed a study, and no study needed a correction after render — the second batch running.

One adjustment was made while writing rather than after: `003` quotes five industry sentences as
content, and three of them contain vocabulary this catalog bans outright. **They were declared as
`data-refusal` regions**, which is exactly what that mechanism is for — a study naming what it
refuses, in the words it refuses. Without that the study would have failed the money check for
quoting the sentence it exists to replace.

The batch's own new rule is the **field cap**: reserved fields checked for equality at two rather
than for a floor. It is the mirror of `S19`'s density rule, written one section later, and between
them they bound the catalog's reserved-field discipline from both ends.
