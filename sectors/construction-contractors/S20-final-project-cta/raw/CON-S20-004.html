If you want to take this further

There is one thing to ask for, and it is a morning.

Not a quote, not a callback, not something to download. Somebody standing on your ground is the only next step that changes what either of us knows — so everything else on this page exists to make that morning worth spending, including the part telling you when not to book it.

Ask us to come and walk it

The person who comes is the person who would run the job, not somebody whose job is winning work. If they are on a site and cannot come this week, we will tell you that rather than send a substitute with the same title.

At the end of it you will be told which of three answers your scope can produce, whether we are the right size and distance for it, and whether a fixed start is achievable. All three on the day.

And what we are undertaking, if you do

  • The person who comes is the person who would run it.Ask us: will you be on this job, and who else is?
  • We will tell you at the walk if we are the wrong firm for it, and name somebody better suited.Ask us: is this the sort of job you turn down?
  • We will say on the day if your fixed start is not achievable, rather than in week six.Ask us: what would have to be true for this start to hold?
  • We will not chase you. One call after the visit, from the person who came to it.Ask us: what happens if I do not reply?

Nobody buys a building on urgency, so there is no deadline on this page, no count of places remaining and no line about our order book filling — that last is the polite version of the same device and it is still a pressure trick. There is no free quote, because a number given before the walk is a guess that gets revised upward, and by then it has already done its work of getting us onto a list. There is no promise about how quickly we reply, because that would be a promise about a job nobody has looked at yet. And there is no sequence of reminders: one call after the visit, and then it is yours.
