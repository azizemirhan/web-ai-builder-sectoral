If you want to take this further

Five things we will do, and the question that checks each one.

A closing section is usually a button. This one is a list of undertakings, written specifically enough that you would be able to see us break them — and each comes with the question to put to whoever turns up.

  1. One

    We will

    Come and look at it before we say anything about money

    Whatever you are holding — a sentence, a drawing set or a full package — the next thing that happens is somebody standing on your ground, not a figure arriving in an inbox.

    If we broke it

    A quotation would arrive from somebody who has never seen the site, and it would move the first time anybody did.

    Ask us

    Who is coming, and have they seen it?

  2. Two

    We will

    Send the person who would run it, not the person who wins work

    If that person is on a job and cannot come this week, we will tell you that rather than send somebody else and give them the same title.

    If we broke it

    You would meet somebody once, at the start, and never see them again after the contract was signed.

    Ask us

    Will you be on this job, and who else is?

  3. Three

    We will

    Tell you at the walk if we are the wrong firm for it

    Too small for us, too far for our people to be on it every morning, or a kind of work we are worse at. And we will name somebody nearer or better suited, which is the useful half of saying no.

    If we broke it

    Nobody would ever say no, and you would find out from a price that read strangely without ever being told why.

    Ask us

    Is this the sort of job you turn down?

  4. Four

    We will

    Say at the walk if your fixed start is not achievable

    A term, a lease or a season you cannot move. If we cannot meet it you will hear that on the day rather than in week six, when it has become everybody’s problem and nobody’s fault.

    If we broke it

    The programme would only become impossible after the contract was signed, which is the point at which it stops being a conversation.

    Ask us

    What would have to be true for this start to hold?

  5. Five

    We will

    Not chase you

    One call after the visit, from the person who came to it. After that it is your move, and deciding not to go ahead needs no explanation and no reply.

    If we broke it

    You would get a sequence of emails written by somebody who was not at the visit and does not know what was said at it.

    Ask us

    What happens if I do not reply?

What to have out on the table, so the morning is worth it

  • Whatever drawings exist, even if they are out of date. Out of date is information; nothing is not, and we would rather see the version somebody has been arguing about.
  • Who decides, and what that person can sign without asking somebody else. It is the question that most often turns out to have no answer.
  • What fixes your start — a term, a lease, a season. If nothing does, that is also useful and changes what we would propose.
  • Anything another firm has already told you, so we can disagree with it in front of you rather than behind them.

And what is not being done to you here

  • No countdown
  • No limited slots
  • No free quote
  • No follow-up sequence

Nobody buys a building on urgency, so there is no deadline on this page, no count of places left and no line about our order book filling up — that last one is the polite version of the same device and it is still a pressure trick. There is no free quote either, because a number given before anybody has walked the site is a guess that gets revised upward. And there is no sequence of reminders: one call after the visit, and then it is yours.
