If you want to take this further

Five sentences, and the same two lines under each of them.

How you would see it broken, and what to ask whoever turns up. A promise nobody can check is decoration, so the shape repeats without variation — by the second one you know where to look, and by the fifth you are reading undertakings rather than claims.

  1. The first

    We will come and look at it before we say anything about money.

    You would see it broken

    A quotation arrives from somebody who has never seen the site, and it moves the first time anybody does.

    Ask us

    Who is coming, and have they seen it?

  2. The second

    The person who comes is the person who would run it.

    You would see it broken

    You meet somebody once, at the start, and never see them again after the contract is signed.

    Ask us

    Will you be on this job, and who else is?

  3. The third

    We will tell you at the walk if we are the wrong firm for it.

    You would see it broken

    Nobody ever says no, and you work it out from a price that reads strangely without ever being told why.

    Ask us

    Is this the sort of job you turn down?

  4. The fourth

    If your fixed start cannot be met, you hear it on the day.

    You would see it broken

    The programme becomes impossible in week six, which is after the point where it stops being a conversation.

    Ask us

    What would have to be true for this start to hold?

  5. And the fifth

    We will not chase you.

    You would see it broken

    A sequence of emails arrives, written by somebody who was not at the visit and does not know what was said at it.

    Ask us

    What happens if I do not reply?

What to have out on the table, so the morning is worth it

  • Whatever drawings exist, even if they are out of date. Out of date is information; nothing is not.
  • Who decides, and what that person can sign without asking somebody else.
  • What fixes your start — a term, a lease, a season. If nothing does, that is useful as well.
  • Anything another firm has already told you, so we can disagree with it in front of you rather than behind them.

And who would come

Who would come
Name of the person who would run the job—
Ask for a walk
Contact for booking the site visit—

Nobody buys a building on urgency, so there is no deadline on this page, no count of places remaining and no line about our order book filling — the last of those is the polite version of the same device and it is still a pressure trick. There is no free quote, because a number given before anybody has walked the site is a guess that gets revised upward, and by then it has already done its work. There is no promise about how quickly we reply: that would be a promise about a job nobody has looked at. And there is no sequence of reminders — one call after the visit, from the person who came to it, and then it is yours.
