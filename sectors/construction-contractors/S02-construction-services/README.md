# Section Role

The set of things a client can hire this firm to do. A named service, a line saying what it covers,
and enough for a visitor to find the one that matches the job in their head.

It answers the first of the sector's four questions: **can you actually do this one?**

# The Governing Constraint — Every Service Says Who Actually Does It

A services list is the easiest section in this sector to write and the least useful, because every
contractor's list is the same list. *Design and build · groundworks · frame · fit-out · civils ·
refurbishment* appears on every competitor's site, and none of it distinguishes anybody.

**The thing that does distinguish a contractor, and that almost none of them print, is which of
those they do with their own people and which they buy in.**

    Self-delivered · Managed, with a specialist subcontractor · Joint

That single marker per service is:

- **Real** — every firm knows the answer for every line of its own list.
- **Checkable** — a client can ask on site and find out.
- **Uncopyable** — a firm that subcontracts everything cannot print it without saying so.
- **An answer to the actual question** — *can you do this one* means *whose hands, and who carries
  it when it goes wrong*.

It is the sector's version of the position `WELL` kept arriving at: the honest, slightly awkward
operating fact that no competitor can lift.

**Every study in this batch carries the marker on every service.**

# What Is Real, And What May Not Be Invented

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Service names | **Real** — generic trade vocabulary, the `WELL-S02` rule |
| What a service covers | **Real** — a description of work, not an outcome claim |
| Who delivers it | **Real** — self-delivered, managed, or joint |
| Project count or value per service | **Omitted** — an invented figure per line, five times over |
| Duration, programme, lead time | **Omitted** — the `WELL-S08` rule: a sequence is content, a schedule is a claim |
| Price, rate, *from £X* | **Omitted** |
| Certification or scheme per service | **Forbidden** — the sector direction |
| Client or project names | **Forbidden** |
| Per-service media | **Reserved areas** |

**Not present in any study:** an invented figure of any kind; a price, rate or lead time; a
certification, accreditation or scheme; a client or project name; a safety record; an award, rating
or review; an urgency device; a stock handshake or posed PPE group.

# The Second Piece Of Real Content — What The Firm Will Not Take On

On the `WELL-S23` steer-away pattern. A services section that cannot say what it turns down is a
menu, not a position:

- Single-trade packages, where a specialist will be better and cheaper
- Work the firm would have to buy in entirely, and would only be marking up
- A job it cannot resource in the window the client needs

`004` makes this its conversion device, and `001`, `003` and `005` each carry a version of it.

# Boundary With S04, S05 And S23

| Section | What it owns |
| --- | --- |
| `S02` Construction Services | **What you can hire us for** — the set, with a line each |
| `S04` Sectors Served | **Who we do it for** — healthcare, education, industrial, retail |
| `S05` Capabilities | **What we hold in-house** — trades, plant, technical capacity |
| `S23` Construction Service Detail | **One service in full**, on its own page |

    S02 is the list. S05 is the resource behind it. S23 is one of them at length.

A study whose lines could be moved to `S05` unchanged has written capability, not services. The test
is that every line here starts from something a client wants **done**, not something the firm
**owns**.
