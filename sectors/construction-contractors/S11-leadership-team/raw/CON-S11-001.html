The people

A team page shows you the people who win the work.

You want the people who run it. So this one is ordered by how often you will actually deal with somebody, which puts the site manager at the top and the director at the bottom — and says what each of them can decide without asking anybody.

Every day, on your site

The two people whose decisions you will feel. If you remember one line from this page, make it this group.

  • Site manager

    Decides without askingStops the work. Orders what the job needs that day. Moves the sequence within the week without a phone call to anybody.

    One site, not three. Where we cannot resource that, we do not take the job.

    Answers to the contracts manager, and to you every morning.

  • Foreman

    Decides without askingRuns the gang. Stops the work. Calls the site manager rather than working round a problem.

    The person who briefs your job every morning before anybody picks anything up.

    Answers to the site manager, on site, in person.

Every week

The person you escalate to, and the one who can actually change something.

  • Contracts manager

    Decides without askingAgrees a variation. Moves resource between sites. This is the escalation, and the number you are given at pre-start is theirs rather than a switchboard.

    If something has gone wrong and the site manager cannot fix it inside the week, this is the person whose problem it becomes. Not the director.

    Answers to the managing director, and carries your job on the same list as a small number of others.

At fixed points

You will meet them, at moments that are on the programme rather than at random.

  • Commercial manager

    Decides without askingAgrees the valuation. Prices a change.

    The one who priced your job stays on it. Handing it to somebody who did not price it is where most variation arguments start.

    Answers to the managing director, and to the contract.

  • Planner

    Decides without askingRewrites the programme. Nobody else does, which is why it stays a programme rather than a wish.

    Walks the site before writing it, and again when it changes.

    Answers to the contracts manager, and to the sequence.

At tender, and at handover

And nowhere in between. This is the row most team pages leave out.

  • Managing director

    Decides without askingChanges the contract. Turns work down. And nothing on your site day to day.

    A firm where the managing director is making site decisions is a firm with a problem — either the site manager has no authority or the director cannot leave it alone. Neither is good news for the job you are paying for.

    You will see them at the tender meeting and at handover. If you want them at the monthly progress meeting instead, say so at pre-start and it can be arranged — but it is not what makes your job go well.

What is not on this page

No qualifications, no memberships, no years in the trade, no career histories. They are true of everybody here and they distinguish nobody — every contractor’s team page carries the same list, and none of it tells you who will be standing on your site.

What does tell you that is above: who you will see, how often, and what they can decide without asking. All of it is checkable by asking the person it is written under.
