Who we build for

The sector name is the small print. The constraint is the headline.

Every contractor prints the same six categories. Only the sentences underneath them are worth reading, so they are set the other way round.

  • Healthcare

    The building does not close while you work in it.

    So: phasing is priced before anything else, and our site manager sits in the estates meeting rather than hearing about it afterwards.

  • Education

    Term starts whether you are finished or not.

    So: the programme is written backwards from the first day of teaching, and gate sequencing is agreed with the school rather than worked around it.

  • Industrial

    The ground decides before anybody draws an elevation.

    So: we self-deliver the groundworks, because we would rather be the ones standing in the hole when it disagrees with the survey.

  • Retail

    The floor is trading while the work happens.

    So: the job is written in shifts, and the landlord and the client get the same programme rather than two versions of it.

  • Civic

    Half the permissions belong to somebody who is not in the room.

    So: hoardings, routes and closures are designed at tender, and the sequence assumes at least one permission arrives late.

  • Residential

    The neighbours did not choose to live next to a site.

    So: somebody on site is named and reachable, and a complaint gets an answer rather than a form.

Where we are the wrong firm: anything with a live process running through it — clean areas, data halls, food production. The constraint there is contamination and continuity of process, and firms who work in it every week will beat us. Better said here than discovered together on your site.
