Four questions, and one of them stops most drafts.

Every firm has a reason for publishing and almost none of them publish the rule they publish by. These are the four this piece had to answer before it went up, in order, with the one that stops most drafts marked.

  • The first gate

    Did somebody ask it?

    Three different people, inside a month. The third time it seemed better to write the answer down than to say it again, and that is the only origin a piece here is allowed to have.

    What does not count as somebody asking: a keyword, a content calendar, or a quiet week. A topic chosen because it ranks is a topic chosen by somebody who is not the reader.

  • The second gate

    Does the writer run something?

    A contracts manager who runs the kind of site this is about, and who has had the conversation it describes with an actual client. Name of the writerReserved

    If the person writing it does not run something, it does not go up. A piece about site work by somebody who has never stood on one is worth nothing however well it is written, and the byline is the only part of it a reader can check.

  • The third gate, and the fatal one

    Can a reader use it without hiring us?

    • Put a hose on the first bay before the second is built — an afternoon, and it tests the detail rather than the finished building.
    • Ask whoever is building it who owns the parapet joint, and get the answer in writing rather than in a meeting.
    • Have three junction details drawn before you tender: the parapet, a window head, and the base with the finished ground level on it.

    This is the gate that stops most drafts. A piece that cannot answer it is an advertisement with paragraphs in it, and the honest thing to do with one of those is not publish it.

  • The fourth gate

    Is what we get out of it stated?

    Some people who read this ring us. Not many and not soon, but often enough to pay for the afternoon it took to write.

    Others ring somebody else instead, with better questions than they had before. That is also fine, and saying so is more honest than calling this a public service — which is the claim a page like this usually makes by saying nothing at all.

And the piece itself

Reserved, and drawn at the measures the finished text will occupy. Filler would be worse than a blank, because a reviewer reads it and forms a view of writing nobody has done. What this piece does not cover is anybody else's building: it is about one kind of junction, it is not a specification, and nobody could advise on yours without standing on it.
