From the site journal

The largest thing on this page is the part you can use without us.

It sits where a newsletter box normally sits, which is the whole argument. A journal page converts when a reader does the thing, finds it worked, and remembers where they read it — not when it collects an address in exchange for a paragraph.

Three things you can do tomorrow without ringing anybody

  1. Put a hose on the first bay before the second one is built. It costs an afternoon, and it tests the detail rather than the finished building.
  2. Ask whoever is building it who owns the parapet joint, and get the answer in writing rather than in a meeting. If nobody can answer, that is the answer.
  3. Have three junction details drawn before you tender — the parapet, a window head, and the base with the finished ground level on it.

All three can be done on your building by somebody who is not us, and if you do them you will not need this page again. That is the test of whether it was worth writing: a piece that cannot be used without hiring us is an advertisement with paragraphs in it.

What we get out of publishing this

Some people who read it ring us. Not many, and not soon, but often enough to pay for the afternoon it took to write. That is the whole of our interest in it and we would rather say so than leave it to be assumed.

Others ring somebody else instead, with better questions than they had before. That is also fine. A page that pretends to have no interest in the reader is making a claim it cannot support, and the silence where this paragraph usually goes is that claim.

The piece itself

Reserved, and drawn at the measures the finished text will occupy rather than filled with something to look at — filler would be worse than a blank, because a reviewer reads it and forms a view of writing nobody has done. It was written because three different people asked the question inside a month, by a contracts manager who runs the kind of site it is about — if the writer does not run something, it does not go up.

It covers one kind of junction on one kind of building. It is not a specification and it is not advice on yours, which nobody could give without standing on it. There is no guide to everything here and no list of tips: a piece with that title was written for a search engine.

And one small ask, afterwards

If you do the first thing and it goes wrong — if the bay leaks — send us the photograph. We would rather see it than not, and there is no charge for looking at a photograph.

An ask that only makes sense once you have already acted, which is the only kind a page like this has earned.
