The test of a piece like this is whether somebody could act on it tomorrow and never call. So there is a block below headed what you can do with this without us, and everything in it is specific enough to be done on your own building.

Why this one was written

Because three different people asked it inside a month, and the third time we wrote the answer down instead of saying it again.

Not because it ranks. A topic chosen by a search engine is a topic chosen by somebody who is not the reader.

Who wrote it, and what they run

A contracts manager who runs the kind of site this is about, and who has had the conversation it describes with an actual client.

If the person writing it does not run something, it does not go up. An article about site work by somebody who has not been on one is worth exactly nothing.

What you can do with this without us

  • Put a hose on the first bay before the second one is built. It costs an afternoon and it tests the detail rather than the finished building.
  • Ask whoever is building it who owns the parapet joint, and get the answer in writing rather than in a meeting.
  • Get three junction details drawn before you tender — the parapet, a window head and the base with the finished ground level on it.

And what we get out of publishing it

People who read this ring us. Not most of them, and not soon, but often enough to pay for the afternoon it took to write.

Some of them ring somebody else instead, with better questions than they had before. That is also fine, and it is a more honest description of what this page is for than calling it a public service.

What this piece does not cover

It is about one kind of junction on one kind of building. It is not a specification and it is not advice on yours, which nobody could give without standing on it.

There is no guide to everything here and no list of tips. A piece with that title was written for a search engine, and a search engine is not the reader.
