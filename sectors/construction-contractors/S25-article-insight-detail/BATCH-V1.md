## The Governing Constraint — Give Something Away Or It Is An Advertisement

    A piece that cannot be used without hiring us is an advertisement with paragraphs in
    it. The test of an article is whether somebody could act on it tomorrow and never
    call.

So every study carries a block headed **what you can do with this without us**, and the three things
in it are real, specific and doable by a reader on their own building:

> **Hose the first bay** before the second is built · **Ask who owns the parapet joint**, in writing ·
> **Have three junction details drawn** before you tender

All three come from `S23`, which is the first time in this sector a journal page has given away the
content of a service page rather than teasing it.

## The Three Rules Behind The Piece

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **It was written because three different people asked it** inside a month | Not because it ranks. A topic chosen by a search engine is a topic chosen by somebody who is not the reader |
| **The person who wrote it runs something** | **If the writer does not run something, it does not go up.** A piece about site work by somebody who has never stood on one is worth nothing however well written |
| **What we get out of publishing it is stated** | *Some people who read it ring us. Not many, not soon.* Others ring somebody else with better questions, which is also fine — and saying so is more honest than the silence usually standing there |

## The Text Itself Is Reserved

The catalog's standing resolution, and the form `WELL-S25` invented for it, used here in the sector
that most needed it:

    Reserve the prose. Author the apparatus.

    A blank says nothing. Filler says something false. Measures say how much.

An article body cannot be invented, so it is drawn as ruled measures at the width the finished text
would occupy. Filler would be worse than a blank, **because a reviewer reads it and forms a view of
writing nobody has done.**

### `002`, the head-note

The editor's paragraph placed before a text to say where it came from and what it is, closed by a
note on what it leaves out. It is a real scholarly form and it is **exactly the shape of *reserve the
prose, author the apparatus*** — the head-note is the form invented for precisely that position, so
the reserved body is framed rather than apologised for.

Distinct from the colophon of `S16-002`, the errata of `S24-002` and the standfirst of `S21-002`.

### `003`, the four gates

Every firm has a reason for publishing and almost none publish the rule they publish by. Four gates,
in order, with the third marked as fatal: **can a reader use it without hiring us?**

> A draft that cannot answer it is an advertisement with paragraphs in it, and the honest thing to do
> with one of those is not publish it.

The sector direction names this variant's trap as *the specification table*; four equal criteria would
have invited a score. **These are gates in sequence, and one of them is fatal.**

### `004`, the giveaway as the primary action

The three things a reader can do tomorrow sit in the largest block on the page — **in the position a
newsletter box usually occupies**, which is the whole argument. Directly beneath it, unprompted, is
the disclosure of what the firm gets out of publishing.

The one ask is small, last and conditional: *if you do the first thing and the bay leaks, send us the
photograph.* **An ask that only makes sense once the reader has already acted**, which is the only
kind a page like this has earned.

### `005`, the unwritten text at measure

The largest object on an article page is the article, and here it has not been written. `S21-005`
faced the same problem with a title and answered it by drawing the field as measures; this is that
answer at the scale of a text — **paragraphs of ruled measures with the shape a real piece has: an
opening, a long middle, a short close.**

Everything the catalog can honestly write about an unwritten piece is apparatus, so all of it is set
small in one margin beside it.

## Checker Note

Two rules were narrowed too far and broadened — *three different people asked* (`003` states it as
*three different people, inside a month*) and the governing sentence, which `001` carried in a
shortened form. Three failures were genuine content gaps and were fixed in the studies.

The batch's own new check is the **reserved-prose check**: the body must be a `lines` or `para` block
carrying `role="img"` and an `aria-label` beginning *Reserved area*, and **every study must also say
in visible copy why it is not filler.** It is the first check in this catalog that requires a study to
explain its own placeholder — which is right for a section where the placeholder occupies most of the
page.

## Notes

- Twenty-fifth authored batch in the `CON` sector. `S01`–`S25` are complete.
- **The reusable outcome is that an article should be testable by what it gives away.** A firm can ask
  one question of any draft — *could a reader act on this tomorrow and never call?* — and the answer
  sorts publishable pieces from advertisements without needing an opinion about the writing.
- The second outcome is the disclosure. **Stating what the firm gets out of publishing is more
  trustworthy than the silence that usually stands there**, and it costs nothing, because a reader
  already assumes it.
- The third is the byline rule: **if the writer does not run something, it does not go up.** It is a
  publishing constraint rather than a design one, but it is the only part of an article a reader can
  check before reading it.
