How the site is run

A record is an outcome. A programme is a decision.

Outcomes can be under-reported. Decisions can be checked on site. So what follows is the set of standing decisions this firm makes, who holds each one, and what it costs us when somebody uses it.

Anybody on this site can stop the work.

Including somebody who started this morning, an agency operative, and a driver who has come through the gate once. They do not need to find a manager first.

If they stop it and they were wrong, nothing happens to them. That is the whole of it — a stop that costs the person something is a stop that will not happen the second time, and the second time is the one that matters.

  • A named role holds it

    Who restarts it

    Not the person who stopped it, and not whoever is under the most pressure that morning. The supervisor and the person who raised it, together, and not before.

    What it costs usSometimes the rest of the day. The delay is recorded against the programme, never against the person who called it.

  • Anybody holds it

    The near miss

    Whoever saw it says so, and it is written down and briefed to everybody the same week — including when nothing happened, which is the only useful time to learn anything from it.

    What it costs usRecording near misses honestly makes our own figures look worse than a firm that does not. That is exactly what those figures are worth.

    The morning brief

    Run by the supervisor for that work, not read off a form: what changed since yesterday, what is going on above and below you, and who is working next to you today.

    What it costs usThe start of every morning, from everybody on site, before anybody is productive. It is the cheapest thing on this page.

  • A named role holds it

    Who a subcontractor answers to

    On safety, every firm on site answers to our supervisor — regardless of who they invoice, who introduced them, or how long they have been doing it their own way.

    What it costs usSubcontractors. Some will not work that way, and we would rather find that out at tender than at first fix.

  • A named role holds it

    Temporary works

    Designed by the engineer who carries it, and never altered by the person who wants the prop moved. A prop that is in the way is in the way for a reason.

    What it costs usWaiting for a decision from somebody who is not on site, on a morning when moving it ourselves would take a minute.

  • Anybody holds it

    When the job is behind

    Every serious incident in this industry happens on a job that is behind. The stop authority does not change when we are behind — which is the only time it is worth anything at all.

    What it costs usWe have handed back time on jobs rather than compress a sequence. It is the most expensive line on this page and the one we would keep.

What is not on this page, and why

  • An incident rate
  • A days-without board
  • Zero harm
  • A perfect record
  • A wall of scheme logos

An incident rate rewards under-reporting — the cheapest way to improve one is to write less down. A days-without board punishes whoever breaks the streak, and the person who breaks it is the person who reported something.

Both make a page look better and a site more dangerous, so a firm that prints them is telling you what it optimises. We would rather tell you who can stop the work, because you can check that by asking anybody standing on it.

The certificates and registrations are real and they are not here either — they belong on the compliance page, where a number can be checked against a register rather than admired on a wall.
