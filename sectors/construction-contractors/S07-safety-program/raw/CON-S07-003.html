How the site is run

Sorted by when it applies, not by what it is called.

A record is an outcome and outcomes can be under-reported. These are decisions, and a decision can be checked by asking somebody standing on the site.

Site area · 4:1 · work happening, real PPE, not posed

Phase one

Before work starts

Most of what keeps a site safe is decided before anybody picks anything up.

A named role

The morning brief

Run by the supervisor for that work, not read off a form: what changed since yesterday, what is happening above and below you, who is working next to you.

Costs usThe start of every morning from everybody, before anybody is productive.

A named role

Induction

Everybody is told on their first morning that they can stop the job, by the person who would be stopped. Told by anybody else it is a policy; told by them it is true.

Costs usA supervisor’s time on every arrival, including a driver here once.

A named role

Temporary works

Designed by the engineer who carries it and never altered by the person who wants the prop moved. A prop in the way is in the way for a reason.

Costs usWaiting on somebody not on site, on a morning when moving it would take a minute.

A named role

The chain on site

On safety every firm answers to our supervisor — regardless of who they invoice or how long they have done it another way.

Costs usSubcontractors. Some will not work that way, and we would rather know at tender.

Phase two

The moment something is wrong

The shortest part of this page, and the only part that has to be true.

Anybody on site

Anybody stops the work

Somebody who started this morning, an agency operative, a driver through the gate once. No permission, no manager to find first, and no need to be right about the trade being stopped.

Costs usTime we do not get back, on the morning we can least afford it. That is the price of it being real.

Anybody on site

And if they were wrong

Nothing happens to them. No conversation afterwards, no note, no quiet reputation for being difficult. A stop that costs the person something will not happen the second time.

Costs usSome stops will be unnecessary and we pay for those in full. A cheaper problem than the alternative.

Phase three

Afterwards

What happens next is what decides whether it happens again.

A named role

Who restarts it

The supervisor and the person who raised it, together — not the person who stopped it alone, and not whoever is under the most pressure that morning.

Costs usSometimes the rest of the day. Recorded against the programme, never against the person who called it.

Anybody on site

The near miss

Whoever saw it says so; it is written down and briefed to everybody the same week rather than filed. It is the only time anybody learns something at no cost.

Costs usHonest recording makes our own figures look worse than a firm that does not bother. That is what those figures are worth.

Anybody on site

When the job is behind

Every serious incident in this industry happens on a job that is behind. None of the above changes when we are behind — the only circumstance in which any of it is worth anything.

Costs usWe have handed time back rather than compress a sequence. The most expensive line here, and the one we would keep.

What is not on this page, and why

  • An incident rate
  • A days-without board
  • Zero harm
  • A perfect record
  • A wall of scheme logos

An incident rate rewards under-reporting — the cheapest way to improve one is to write less down. A days-without board punishes whoever breaks the streak, and the person who breaks it is the person who reported something.

Both make a page look better and a site more dangerous. A firm that prints them is telling you what it optimises, so we would rather tell you who can stop the work — which you can check by asking anybody standing on it.

The certificates and registrations are real, and they are not here either. They belong on the compliance page, where a number can be checked against a register rather than admired on a wall.
