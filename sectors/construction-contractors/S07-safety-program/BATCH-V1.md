## Authoring Direction

## The Governing Constraint — A Record Is An Outcome. A Programme Is A Decision.

The sector direction forbids outright, in this section above all: an incident rate, an
accident-frequency or lost-time figure, a *days without an incident* board, *zero accidents*, *zero
harm*, *100% safe*, an invented certification or registration number, a logo wall of standards
bodies, and any named regulation asserted as complied with.

**That prohibition is the section's argument, not an obstacle to it.**

    A safety record is an outcome, and outcomes can be under-reported.
    A safety programme is a set of standing decisions, and decisions can be checked on site.

And the reason is stated in the client's interest rather than the catalog's:

- **An incident rate rewards under-reporting.** The cheapest way to improve one is to record less.
- **A days-without board punishes whoever breaks the streak** — and the person who breaks it is the
  person who reported something.

Both make a page look better and a site more dangerous. A firm that prints them has told you what it
optimises. Every study says so.

## The Device — Who Holds It, And What It Costs Us

Each standing decision carries two halves:

| On every decision | Why it is the honest half |
| --- | --- |
| **Who holds the authority** | A policy nobody can use is decoration. The question is whose hands it is in |
| **What it costs us when they use it** | Anyone can grant an authority that is free to exercise. The cost is the proof |

This is the sector's third instance of one method: the `S05` limit, the `S06` gate, and now the
`S07` cost. Each is the sentence a competitor cannot copy, attached to something they can.

## The Sentence, And The Sentence That Makes It True

> **Anybody on this site can stop the work** — including somebody who started this morning — and
> they do not need to find a manager first.
>
> **If they stop it and they were wrong, nothing happens to them.**

The second sentence is the load-bearing one, and the batch treats it that way: `005` sets it in the
page's only accent colour at poster scale. A stop that costs the person something is a stop that
will not happen the second time, and the second time is the one that matters. Both sentences appear
in all five studies, and the checker requires both.

## This Section Reserves Nothing — For Its Own Reason

`S06` reserved nothing because an empty duration asserts a system. `S07` reserves nothing because:

    Every countable thing in a safety section is an incident figure or a proxy for one.

A labelled empty box on a safety page draws the shape of the board the direction forbids, and tells
a reviewer that the finished page will carry a number where a decision should be. **No study in this
batch contains a reserved field of any kind**, and the checker enforces it.

Three consecutive sections — reserve in `S05`, omit in `S06`, omit again in `S07` for a different
reason — is the `WELL-S11` field-versus-figure test being applied rather than quoted.

## The Programme-Pressure Admission

The item that costs most to print and makes the rest credible, carried in all five:

> Every serious incident in this industry happens on a job that is behind. **The stop authority does
> not change when we are behind** — which is the only time it is worth anything.

## No Hi-Vis, Tested By Hue

Anti-pattern six. Orange is this sector's default way of signalling *safety* without saying
anything, and on a safety page it is at its most tempting and least honest.

**No study uses a saturated orange or amber anywhere in its palette**, and the batch checker proves
it rather than trusting the author's eye: every hex in every stylesheet is converted to HSL and
flagged if its hue falls between fifteen and fifty-five degrees at saturation above forty-five per
cent and mid lightness. Warm papers and near-black browns pass; hi-vis does not. All five palettes
came back clean.

Where colour carries meaning it carries exactly one: **green for an authority anybody holds, blue
for one a named role holds.** `003` adds a deep red for the phase called *the moment something is
wrong*, and `005` uses a single red on the word the page turns on.

### `002`, where the refusal became the strongest passage

The study is built as the questions a client asks on a first visit, in the order they usually
arrive — and the last one is **"Where are your numbers?"**. Putting the prohibition in the answer
position turns the sector's hardest constraint into the page's best writing, because the honest
answer is more persuasive than any rate would have been. Its cost line is the batch's most exposed:
*"A page with no figure on it, next to competitors whose pages have several. We think you can tell
the difference, and if you cannot, one visit to either site will."*

### `003`, grouped by when

A safety page listed by topic is a policy index. Grouped by **the moment each decision applies in** —
before work starts, the moment something is wrong, afterwards — it becomes usable: a reader finds
the band they need and reads only that. The middle band is deliberately the shortest and the most
important, which the layout shows before the copy says it.

Distinct from `CON-S05-003`, which ran vertical colour columns keyed to its marker. Here the bands
run horizontally and are keyed to **time**, leaving the authority marker to appear as a chip inside
each card — different axis, different thing carrying the colour.

### `004`, converting by arming the buyer

Five questions, our answers beside them, and an instruction to put the same five to every other firm
on the tender list. Only a firm confident in its own answers can print that, which is exactly what
makes it work as a conversion device.

It then undercuts its own medium on purpose: **"Do not take these answers from a web page."** Ask
them standing on a job, of somebody in a hard hat who does not know you are a client — *"Come and do
it on one of ours. We will not choose which site, and we will not walk you round the part we would
have picked."* A safety page that pretends a web page settles the question is doing the same thing a
days-without board does.

### `005`, restraint as the art direction

Every other art-directed study in this sector reached for a dark ground, a stagger or a colour
field. This one refuses all three: white paper, one rule weight, one red, no cards and no fills.
Safety documentation that has been dressed up is the exact thing a reader should distrust, so the
art direction is the discipline rather than the decoration.

## The Checker Problem This Section Forced

The hard vocabulary scan failed four of the five studies on the first run — on *zero harm*, *perfect
record* and *incident rate*. Every hit was inside a **refusal block**, where the page names the
forbidden things precisely in order to reject them.

This is the fourth time in this catalog that a rule written for a claim has caught a refusal, and
the first time softening it would have been wrong: this section's core content **requires** naming
what it will not print. So the fix is structural rather than a tier change:

- Each study now declares its refusal region with a `data-refusal` attribute.
- The checker strips that region — matching `<div>` depth — before running the hard scan.
- **Outside the declared refusal the vocabulary may not appear at all; inside it, it must**, which a
  separate check confirms by requiring *rewards under-reporting* and *breaks the streak*.

    A prohibition a section exists to explain cannot be enforced by a rule that only knows how
    to find the words.

That pattern is reusable anywhere a section's job is to refuse something by name.

## Notes

- Seventh authored batch in the `CON` sector. `S01`–`S07` are complete.
- **The reusable outcome is that a prohibition can be the argument.** Where a sector forbids the
  usual proof — safety figures here, but equally outcome data in health, results in law, returns in
  finance — the strongest available content is to name the missing proof and explain why printing it
  would make the thing worse. That passage cannot be copied by a firm that would rather have the
  number.
- The second outcome is the authority test: **who holds it, and what it costs you when they use
  it.** Any claim about culture — safety, quality, service, escalation — becomes checkable the
  moment it names the holder and the price of exercise, and stays decorative until it does.
- The third is the `data-refusal` region. A section whose job includes refusing something by name
  needs its checker to know where the refusal is; marking it in the markup is cheaper and more
  honest than weakening the rule.
- `S08 Certifications & Compliance` is next, and it inherits directly from this batch's closing
  line: the certificates are real, they are not on the safety page, and **every number and
