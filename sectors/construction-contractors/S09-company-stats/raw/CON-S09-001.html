The firm, in figures

Four numbers, and four different kinds of number.

Most stats bands put a date, a running total, a snapshot and a single extreme into four identical boxes. They are not the same kind of thing, and drawing them as one is the part that misleads. So each is drawn as what it is, and each says what it is counted against.

  • A fixed point

    Year founded

    Year the firm was founded—

    A date is not a score. It does not grow because we are any good, and a firm that has traded longer under a different name has a shorter answer to the second line than the first.

  • A running total

    Projects completed

    A total with no start date can be any number you like. The definition matters as much as the count: a firm that counts every call-out will always beat one that counts contracts.

    Largest project by value

    The largest project we have done is not the size of project we usually do. It is one job, drawn here as the outlier it is, and it is the figure most likely to make you think your own is too small to bother us with. It is not.

The numbers that are not here

  • Happy clients
  • Satisfaction rating
  • On-time completion
  • Awards won
  • Any safety figure

Happy clients is the client list with a compliment attached, and nobody ran that survey. A satisfaction or on-time percentage asserts a measurement system, and the definition of on time is set by whoever is reporting it. An award is a body we chose making a judgement we paid to enter.

Safety figures are absent for a harder reason, and it is on the safety page: an incident rate rewards under-reporting, so a firm that prints one has told you what it optimises.

If a figure is countable and it is not one of the four above, it is a claim rather than a count. That is the whole of the rule, and it is why this page is short.
