## The content decision

This role is normally a wall of trade-body marks. The contract forbids an invented scheme, standard, certification or approval, and forbids the badge and shield shapes outright:

> **A logo is not a *check*.** Anything on this page that claims a standard has a certificate behind it, or it is not on this page.

**For each one we hold** — four reserved fields: *what it is*, *who issues it*, *what it covers, and what it does not*, and the one a logo wall never carries: **where to check it — a public register you can look at yourself.**

> **Why there is no wall of marks.** A logo on a website is an image file. The only thing worth showing is the register somebody else keeps, and where you can look at it.

And the part that is not reserved, because it is true today:

> **What is checked here anyway.** Every job gets a sheet. Every sheet gets a name on it. That is the check that runs every day, whatever is framed on the wall.

**No trade body, scheme, standard or accreditation is named anywhere in the five studies.**

## What was designed

The only media allowed on this page is **the document the page says is the real check**: the job sheet on the desk, the job sheet being written, the inspection sheet on the clipboard. Nothing framed, nothing on a wall, nothing with a mark on it.

The four reserved fields use the sector's bare labelled register, and the fourth field — *where to check it* — is deliberately the longest label, because it is the one a badge cannot have.
