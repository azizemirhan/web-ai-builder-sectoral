# Design Direction

**Architecture and interior design web design is primarily visual.**

The full sector direction — visual language, anti-patterns, copy direction, media direction, the
Architecture section-shell rule and the Architecture reading of the five study directions — is in
`./ARCHITECTURE-DESIGN-DIRECTION.md`. It applies to all `ARC` authoring and re-authoring, and is
the sector application of `../../standards/01-AUTHORING-STANDARD.md` rather than a competing rule
set.

In summary:

- Compose with architecture, interior, spatial and material imagery, light, proportion, scale,
  negative space, editorial typography and whitespace.
- The register to aim at is a premium architecture portfolio, a contemporary design publication or
  an art-directed studio site — not a drawing package, tender document, specification sheet or
  professional register.
- Technical and professional document metaphors — sheets, issue and revision blocks, dossiers,
  registers, ledgers, schedules, specification tables, curriculum sheets, technical matrices,
  atlases, title blocks — are not the sector's default styling device, and are used only on the
  narrow grounds the global standard allows.
- Visible copy is concise, confident and editorial, and detail pages manage depth through visual
  modules and progressive disclosure rather than uninterrupted prose.
- Reserved media areas are intended output. Studies must not become text-only because real media
  cannot be fabricated, and must not invent projects, people, clients, awards or addresses.
- A section study contains its own role only: no site-wide header, primary navigation or global
  footer unless that component is the role being authored. S01 is the hero alone.
