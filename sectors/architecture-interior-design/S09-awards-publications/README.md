# Section Name

Awards & Publications

# Sector

Architecture & Interior Design

# Prefix

ARC

# Purpose

Show where the work has been recognised and published, as visual evidence rather than as a
bibliography.

# Visitor Intent

The visitor is checking external validation: who has recognised this practice, and where the work
has appeared.

# Content Responsibility

Recognition: award or publication name, the project it relates to, brief context, and the visual
artefact where one exists.

# In Scope

- Publication covers, tear sheets and editorial spreads
- Project imagery associated with the recognition
- Award title and publication name
- Concise context per entry
- A press quotation where a real one exists
- A year, only where real data exists

# Out Of Scope

- A global site header, primary navigation or footer
- **Fabricated awards, shortlistings, publications, citations, dates or quotations** — this is the
  section that most invites them
- CV-style credit lists, bibliographies and citation registers as the section's primary device
- Press articles in full, which belong to S16 and S25

These are authoring and research directions, not production enums, and the study IDs do not change
with them. The five studies must differ structurally. They must not become five colour schemes,
five font themes, five cosmetic variants, or five copies of one grid with the content swapped.

In this section:

- **003** holds a longer recognition list through visual grouping — covers, years, grouped
  entries — rather than through a table or citation block.
- **005** is an art-directed recognition composition. Not a CV, register or bibliography.

**Recorded corrections.** `ARC-S09-001`, `ARC-S09-003` and `ARC-S09-005` were reviewed as too
technical or too text-heavy, and three of five studies carried no media at all. Future direction is
more visual and more editorial.

# Architecture Direction

Sector direction: `../ARCHITECTURE-DESIGN-DIRECTION.md`.

`ARC-S09-002` — a tear sheet beside a short record list — proves the section can be visual. Covers,
spreads and project imagery with short recognition lines are the direction; table-heavy and
list-only presentation is not.

# Section Shell

Section only. No global header, navigation or footer.

# Media Relationship

Expected. Reserved areas for covers, tear sheets and project imagery. A section with no media at
all has not answered this role.

# Interaction Notes

Optional. Grouping by year or type may use disclosure; entries must remain reachable without
script.

# Responsive Considerations

Cover and entry must stay associated through reflow. A dense record list must become readable
stacked entries, not a horizontally scrolling table.

# Authoring Questions

- **Primary visual element:** publication covers, tear sheets and the project imagery behind the
  recognition.
- **Immediate understanding:** that the work has been recognised, and by whom.
