What the service is

Placeholder description. Renovation is not one long task but a sequence of decisions about an existing fabric, and most of those decisions need something from the client as well as from the studio: access, a decision, a document, a choice between two options that are both reasonable.

So this page describes the service as a ledger. Each stage is set out with the studio's obligations on one side and the client's on the other, and the thing that has to exist before the stage can close is named at its head. It is the same division a written appointment would carry.

Division of responsibility, stage by stage

Read across each stage. Nothing on either side is optional, and a stage closes only when the item named at its head exists.

The studio does

You provide

  1. Stage 01

    Survey and condition record

    Closes with: an agreed record of what is there

    The studio does

    • Measures the building and draws it as found
    • Records condition, room by room, in writing
    • Lists what could not be seen or reached

    You provide

    • Access to the whole building, including closed rooms
    • Any drawings, deeds or reports already held
    • Confirmation of who else may enter during the survey
  2. Stage 02

    Brief and constraints

    Closes with: a written brief, signed

    The studio does

    • Writes the brief against the record, not against a wish
    • Sets out the constraints found on the record
    • Separates what is fixed from what is still open

    You provide

    • What the building has to do when it is finished
    • Who decides, when more than one person is involved
    • Anything already promised to a neighbour or an authority
  3. Stage 03

    What stays and what goes

    Closes with: a marked-up retention plan

    The studio does

    • Proposes what to keep, repair, replace or remove
    • Gives the reason for each, in one line each
    • Marks the decisions that cannot be reversed later

    You provide

    • A decision on each item, or a reason to defer it
    • Any attachment to a part of the building the studio should know about
    • Instructions where a specialist opinion is wanted first
  4. Stage 04

    Design of the new work

    Closes with: a coordinated design set

    The studio does

    • Designs the new work against the retained fabric
    • Details the junctions where old meets new
    • Coordinates the consultants' information into the set

    You provide

    • Appointment of the consultants the design depends on
    • Comments returned in one collated response
    • Approval to proceed, in writing
  5. Stage 05

    On site

    Closes with: the works complete and recorded

    The studio does

    • Answers queries against the issued information
    • Inspects periodically and reports what was seen
    • Records changes as they are agreed, not afterwards

    You provide

    • Instructions to the contractor through the agreed route
    • Decisions where an opening-up finds something unexpected
    • Acceptance of the completed work

Record strip · optional

Outside this appointment

These sit with others. The studio coordinates with whoever holds them, and says so at the point the ledger depends on their work.

  • Structural design and any specialist survey
  • Mechanical, electrical and drainage design
  • Statutory applications and their fees
  • Cost planning and contract administration
  • Removal of hazardous material

Who this suits

Placeholder description. A client who owns or is taking on an existing building and wants the decisions about it recorded as they are made, rather than settled quietly on site.

The ledger is demanding on both sides. Where a client cannot commit to the decisions in the right-hand column, a different route is more honest: an appointment where the studio holds more of the work is listed below.
