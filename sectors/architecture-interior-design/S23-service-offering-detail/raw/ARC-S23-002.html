Concept design

The stage where the building is still an argument — before it becomes a set of drawings that only record decisions already made.

The offering

Concept design is placeholder description for the first design stage: the point at which the site, the brief and the constraints are put together and a proposition is made about what the building could be.

It produces a proposition, not a specification. Plans, sections and models are made in order to test an idea, and they are made loosely enough that the idea can still change. What leaves the stage is a scheme that can be argued for, together with the reasoning that got there.

The stage is deliberately short. Its value is that it ends with a decision, and the work that follows is more expensive to reverse.

The studio takes this stage as a separate appointment, so a client can commission it alone and stop there.

What it contains

Included

  • Site and context appraisal
  • Brief written with the client and agreed in writing
  • Two or three alternative schemes, drawn to the same standard
  • Plans, sections and a study model of the preferred scheme
  • A written design report setting out the reasoning

Not included

  • Planning or permitting submissions
  • Technical drawings for construction
  • Structural, mechanical or electrical design
  • Cost advice

A concept is worth having only if it can be given up. The stage is built so that changing your mind is still cheap.

How it is delivered

  1. Appraisal

    The site is visited and read, and the constraints that will shape the scheme are written down before anything is drawn.

  2. Brief

    The brief is drafted with the client, and the parts that are fixed are separated from the parts that are open.

  3. Alternatives

    Schemes are drawn in parallel and to the same standard, so the comparison between them is fair.

  4. Resolution

    One scheme is chosen and developed far enough to be tested, and the reasoning is written up as a report.

Who it suits

Placeholder description. The stage suits a client who has a site and an intention but not yet a scheme, and who would rather test the intention than commit to it.

It is not the right starting point where a scheme already exists and needs technical development, or where a submission deadline leaves no room for alternatives to be drawn. Those routes are listed in the colophon below.
