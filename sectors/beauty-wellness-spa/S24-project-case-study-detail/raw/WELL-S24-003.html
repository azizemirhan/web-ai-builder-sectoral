The stages, and what happened at each

  • Agreed. A conversation, then a plan written on paper that the guest keeps. Nothing bought up front, nothing booked past the first visit.
  • First visit. The plan is tested rather than executed. What was used is written down so it can be repeated or avoided.
  • Revised. The plan was rewritten in the room, on the same sheet, with the previous version crossed out rather than replaced quietly.
  • Reviewed. Built into the plan rather than requested. Stopping is one of the conclusions a review is allowed to reach.
  • Ended. By agreement, at a review. Ending costs nothing and closes nothing off.

What is recorded, and what is not

Kept on file

  • What was used, and in what order
  • Anything the guest said not to use
  • What was changed in the plan, and why
  • Reactions, however minor

Deliberately not kept

  • Photographs of anybody’s face
  • Measurements or scores of anybody’s skin
  • A rating of how the programme went
  • Anything we would not read out to the guest

The confidentiality position

Why there is no photograph here+

Results imagery is regulated in this sector, and we would not publish somebody’s face in any case. A picture of a stranger’s skin tells you nothing about your own.

Why the guest is a reference rather than a name+

Because consent to be treated is not consent to be published, and the two get confused often enough that we keep them separate by default rather than by request.

What we would need before publishing anything+

Written permission, for that specific use, which can be withdrawn afterwards without explanation. Anything published under those terms is taken down when it is withdrawn.

Whether a programme like this would suit you+

That is not answerable from a page. It is a conversation, and if what you are describing is a change on your skin rather than a preference about it, it is a conversation with a doctor first.

This record documents a method, not a result. There is no outcome printed on this page, no comparison of two states, and no account of what the programme achieved — those are regulated in this sector, and we would rather leave them out than invent them.
