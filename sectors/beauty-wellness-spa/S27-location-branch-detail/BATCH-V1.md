## The Governing Constraint — The Page Cannot Describe The Place

The scaffold's Out Of Scope list removes almost everything a location page usually contains:
fabricated addresses, coordinates, numbers and hours; fabricated maps, embedded map services and
**invented directions**; reviews, ratings or claims attributed to the place. `S16` had already
settled the sector's answer for the set of studios, and `S27` inherits it for one studio in full.

    The page cannot describe the place. So it describes the arrival.

Where the building is, what it looks like and how long it takes to reach are reserved or unknowable.
**What happens when you walk in is entirely authorable**, because it is the studio's own practice —
and it is the thing a visitor is actually anxious about before a first visit. The full table of
treatments is in `./README.md`; the short form is that address, telephone and map are reserved,
directions and hours are omitted rather than left blank, access is answered as a conversation
rather than as a row of symbols, and what is done here and who is based here use the `S03` and
`S04` vocabularies.

## The Scaffold's Responsive Rule, Made Structural

> Address and contact details are the content most likely to be needed on a phone and must be
> first-class at small widths, not pushed below decorative media.

As in `S26`, this is made impossible to break rather than promised: **in every study the contact
block precedes any reserved area in source order**, so there is no width at which it comes second.
The batch check reads the position of the first reserved field against the first reserved area in
each file. Three studies carry areas; three pass. Two carry none.

### WELL-S27-003 — Dense / Information-heavy

- **Layout model:** A record band — studio name on its own line, then address, telephone and email as
  three equal fields — over two balanced columns: the reserved map and the access questions on the
  left, what is done here and who is based here on the right.
- **Revision** in the earlier session: the three fields had sat on different baselines and the page
  was jammed against the top edge; the record is now its own band and the columns finish together.
- **Access as questions, not symbols.** *Is there a step at the door — Ask. Can I be treated without
  going upstairs — Ask.* A tick beside a step is a promise about a building nobody has measured.
- **Palette:** black and white only.

### WELL-S27-004 — Conversion-led

- **Layout model:** Contact first, then one panel: ring before you travel, what that call gets you,
  and the honest line about when the other studio is the better one.
- **Conversion device:** the conversion is a telephone call before a journey, not a booking — `S20`
  owns the closing ask and `S06` the flow. What this page can usefully convert is somebody about to
  travel to the wrong building.
- **The steer away:** *"Sometimes the answer is that the other studio is the better one, and you will
  be told that before you set off rather than after."*
- **Media:** one reserved map area, after the contact block. Dark green ground.

### WELL-S27-005 — Sector-native / Distinctive

- **Layout model:** The record held in one quiet line at the head, then the arrival written as five
  thresholds — each a single large serif line on a warm sand field with a hairline between — and the
  studio's facts as a small sans foot beneath.
- **Why this is sector-native:** in most sectors a location page answers *where is it and how long
  will it take*. Here the visitor's real anxiety before a first visit is *what happens when I walk
  in* — whether they will be looked at, made to wait at a desk, called out by name. So the page
  answers that, in order, from the pavement to the point where it goes quiet: *you come in and sit
  down; if you are early the chair is ready; somebody comes out for you; the room is warm before you
  are in it; then it goes quiet.* Every line is a statement of practice, the only thing about this
  place that can be written without inventing it.
- **Media: none, deliberately, and differently from `002`.** `002` argues that a call beats a
  photograph. `005` is **paced like the arrival it describes** — big unhurried lines and a great deal
  of ground, the sector's own register of calm and space, where a map or a picture would have hurried
  it.
- **What it refuses:** any description of the building — the door, the stairs, the street, what is
  underfoot. All of it would be invented, and `003` and `004` already answer it the honest way.
- **Palette:** warm sand and near-black, the sector's own material register.
