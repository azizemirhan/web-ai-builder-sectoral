Why it is like this

We gave things up on purpose, and this is which ones.

Almost every difference between one spa and another is a decision about what to stop doing. Most of them are never explained, so here are ours, with what each one costs.

Reserved area — the studio, photographed as it is

Three decisions, and none of them is free. That is how you can tell they were decisions rather than descriptions.

The first one is the room. A treatment room with someone being shown in and out of it is not a quiet room, whatever is written on the door. So there is one guest in the building at a time, and the practical effect is that we earn less in a day than a studio running two rooms. We think that is the correct trade, and we would rather say so than pretend it costs nothing.

The second is the conversation at the start. You are not receiving something that was decided before you arrived — what gets used, and how hard, is worked out with you in the room and changed as it goes. That makes the diary harder to run, because no two appointments take exactly the same shape.

The third is the one people notice least and mind most. Nobody is sold anything while they are lying on a couch with their eyes closed. If a product would suit you, we say so afterwards, standing up, when you can say no easily. This costs us a great deal of retail and we have never regretted it.

What the three cost us

  • One guest at a timeLess taken in a day than a studio running two rooms at once.
  • Decided in the roomNo two appointments take the same shape, which makes the diary harder to run.
  • Nothing sold lying downA fraction of the retail a spa this size would normally take.

None of that makes the treatments better than anyone else's. It makes the hour behave the way we would want it to behave if we were the one lying down, which is the only test we have ever been able to apply honestly.
