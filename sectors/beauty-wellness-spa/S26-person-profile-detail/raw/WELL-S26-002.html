One of the people here

One practitioner, written rather than photographed

Name — reserved

Written rather than photographed. What you are choosing is how somebody works, and that is entirely describable without a face or a biography.

What happens before anything is decided

A long set of questions, most of which are not about your skin. What you use at home, what you have stopped using, what happened the last time somebody did something to your face, and what you are actually hoping for — which is usually not what people say first.

Nothing is chosen until that conversation is finished, and the answer to it is sometimes that today is not the day.

What happens when the plan is wrong

It gets said out loud, during, rather than written in a note afterwards. If something is not doing what was expected, you will hear about it while you are still on the bed, and what happens next is decided with you rather than for you.

What gets turned down

A first appointment the day before something important, because there is nowhere to put it if it goes differently. Working over skin that is sore, broken or changing, which is a question for a doctor first. And a course of anything, when coming back twice a year would do the same thing for less of your money.

What they work on

Works mostly on faces, and has done for long enough to have opinions about it.

  • Facials
  • Brows & lashes

How to get them

Ask for them by name when you book. If they are away or full you will be told, rather than quietly given somebody else.

Why there is no photograph

A face and a name are facts about a person. This study carries neither, and it is here to prove that a profile reads completely without them — qualifications and memberships are left out entirely rather than shown as empty fields.
