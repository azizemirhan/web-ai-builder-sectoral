# Section Role

What guests say. The section presents quotations from people who have been treated here, framed so
a visitor can tell how they were gathered.

It answers: *what do people who have actually been here say about it?*

# The Governing Constraint — Why This Section Has No Reframing

Every other constrained section in this sector had a reframing available. `S07` could talk about
operating commitments instead of treatment outcomes; `S09` could talk about function and policy
instead of brands. **This one has none.** A testimonials section is made of attributed quotations,
and `../WELLNESS-DESIGN-DIRECTION.md` forbids an invented client, testimonial attribution, review
score or rating outright. There is no version of a fabricated quote that is acceptable, because the
whole value of a quote is that a real person said it.

So the rule is the plainest one in the sector:

> **The quotation structure is authored. The quotation is reserved.**

This is the same device as the portrait slots in `S04` and the partner-mark slots in `S09`, applied
to text: a **reserved quotation area** is a sized, labelled region where a real quote will go. Its
size communicates the expected length, so the typography, measure and rhythm of the section are all
demonstrable without a single invented word being put in a guest's mouth.

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| The quotation itself | **Reserved area**, sized short / medium / long |
| The attribution | **Reserved token** — `Guest 01`, following the `Practitioner NN` convention from `S04` |
| A guest portrait | **Reserved media slot**, optional per study |
| A review-platform logo | **Reserved mark slot**, following the pattern from `S09` |
| A star rating or score | **Omitted entirely** — see below |
| How quotes are gathered | **Real content.** This is the one thing the studio can honestly say here. |

**Why ratings are omitted rather than reserved.** A reserved area says "a quote goes here", which
is true of any business that has testimonials. An empty star row says something stronger: that this
studio operates a rating system and has scores in it. That is the same failure recorded in `S04`,
where an empty credentials field would still have asserted the studio holds credentials. A figure
cannot be meaningfully reserved, so no study in this batch contains a star row, a score, an average,
a review count, or a "500+ happy guests" figure.

**What is real here.** The studio's own policy on how it gathers and publishes quotes — permission,
verbatim, unedited, no incentive — is a fact about the studio, exactly like the `S07` commitments.
It is the editorial frame of this section, and it is the only text in the batch that is not
reserved.

**Not present in any study:** an invented quotation, guest name, initial, age, location, photograph,
treatment attribution or occupation; a star rating, score, average or review count; a review
platform name or logo; an award, certification or press quote; an efficacy claim in any form.

# Boundary With S05

| Section | What it owns |
| --- | --- |
| `S05` Results Before After | **Visual evidence** — paired imagery, bound by the consent and claims rule |
| `S10` Testimonials | **Spoken evidence** — quotations, bound by the same rule |

Both sections are evidence roles and both reserve their evidence. `S05` reserves images; `S10`
reserves words. Neither may supply the evidence it exists to present.

# Out Of Scope

- A global site header, primary navigation or footer
- Before-and-after imagery, which belongs to S05
- Practitioners, which belong to S04
- Press coverage and awards
- Prices, which belong to S11

# Authoring Questions

**1. What is the primary visual element of this role?**

The measure. A testimonials section is typography and rhythm — how long a quote runs, how it is
set, how much air sits around it. Because the words are reserved, the compositions have to make
those decisions visible through the size and placement of the reserved areas themselves.

**2. What must a visitor understand immediately?**

That these came from real people, and how the studio got them.
