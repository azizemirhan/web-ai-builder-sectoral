## V1 Revision — The Hero Was Not Behaving Like A Hero

The batch as first authored carried **one contained image in three studies and none at all in two.**
The reasoning in each study was sound in isolation, and the aggregate was wrong: **a hero is the one
section in this sector where photography is not decoration.** It is the first answer to *what is this
place like*, which is the question a nervous visitor is actually asking, and four of the five studies
were answering it in type.

So every study now opens on a reserved area, and the five differ by **what the picture does** rather
than by whether there is one:

| | The picture is | And it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `001` | the room, at more width than the sentence | the larger half of the frame |
| `002` | the room, at 21:9 | the one element allowed past the type measure |
| `003` | the waiting room, the surgery, the dentist | **a slider you push** |
| `004` | the room | the ground the three refusals ride on |
| `005` | the ceiling | framed from the chair, which is the only view you get |

Nothing was softened to make room for the pictures. The three commitments, the action and its
qualifier are still in all five, verified by parity diff, and all five are still inside the `40–90`
hero band.

## The Section Role

The opening proposition of a clinic, answered in the time somebody decides whether to scroll.

## The Governing Constraint — Nobody Is Browsing

The contract sets it for the whole sector and this batch is where it was found:

> **Nobody arrives at a dentist's website in a good mood.**

The sector designs for a visitor who is comparing. There is no such visitor. They are **bracing**, and
the anxiety has three named sources — it will hurt, it will cost more than they said, and **they will
be judged for leaving it.** The third keeps people away for years and **no competitor addresses it at
all.**

So it is the proposition in four of the five studies:

> **We are not going to ask why you left it.**

## The Three Commitments

Shared by all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy. Each is something
a visitor could check by asking — which is the only kind of claim this sector should make.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **No lecture** | What we found, not what you should have done |
| **The whole cost, in writing** | Before anything starts, including what you can leave |
| **Nobody works on commission** | Nothing is recommended because it pays better |

The third answers the second-largest dental anxiety — being sold treatment you did not need — and
almost no clinic states it. The second is how a study states cost **without a price**: the contract
requires a stated price to be complete, so what is given instead is the policy, which is a commitment
rather than a figure.

The single action is the same in all five, and so is its qualifier: **an examination and a
conversation. Nothing is done unless you ask.**

## What Is Not Claimed

The contract's claims limits, applied and verified by scan across the batch:

- **No comfort claim.** No *pain-free*, *painless* or *gentle*. `005` instead states what is **done** —
  the work is narrated, and it stops on a signal.
- **No outcome guarantee**, no superiority claim, no award, no rating, no testimonial.
- **No price and no percentage** anywhere.
- **No credential, registration number or specialist title.** These are verifiable facts about real
  people and the workspace reserves rather than invents them.
- **No gendered pronoun** for any placeholder person.

## The Media Rule, Applied

The contract names the sector's three default photographs and rules all three out: the stock smile
model, the open mouth, and the instrument near a face. What is honest is **the room, the people and
the named equipment.**

| Study | Reserved areas | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `001` | One of the surgeries, in use | The room, with people in it. An empty surgery shot wide is a picture of a room that could be anywhere |
| `002` | The same room, at 21:9 edge to edge | Placed **under** the type, never behind it — type over a photograph forces a scrim, which forces the clinical-blue wash the contract bans |
| `003` | The waiting room · the surgery · the dentist who would see you | Two rooms and a person, **the visit in sequence.** The person is **labelled by role, never by name**, so no study presents a placeholder individual as a real one |
| `004` | A surgery, in use | The room, carrying the refusals band on its lower edge. **No type is set over it** and nothing is dimmed to make text legible |
| `005` | The ceiling above the chair | The room the visitor will sit in, **framed from where they will be lying** — the only view a patient actually gets |

No study reserves an image of a mouth, teeth, or an instrument, and no slot label names a person.

**No equipment panel anywhere in the batch.** The contract licenses equipment as a subject only when
the copy names it, and no hero here names a scanner or a camera. `003` had a fourth rail panel
available and did not take it.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe opening, and the plainest statement of the revision: **the room gets more of
  the frame than the sentence does.** The action moves up into the type column so that column carries
  its own weight instead of becoming a caption beside a picture. The commitments run as a row on a
  rule beneath, with no boxes.
- **`002`** — the statement is the largest type in the batch and the only thing on the first screen,
  because **a reader who is nervous can take in one sentence, and a hero that gives them four has
  been designed for somebody calm.** Everything else is held to a strict measure; **the photograph is
  the one element allowed past it**, running to both edges of the frame at 21:9. That is how an
  editorial hero gives an image the first screen without a scrim.
- **`003`** — **the slider, and it only moves when the visitor moves it.** A hero carousel normally
  advances on a timer, which takes the page away from a reader who has not finished with it.
  `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory` does the paging, the panels are sized so the next one is already
  peeking at the right edge, and the scrollbar is styled into an accent track so the affordance is
  shown rather than implied. **No JavaScript, no autoplay, no dependency.** The three panels are not
  the same room at three angles — they are **the visit in sequence**, so pushing the rail walks the
  visitor through the arrival they are nervous about. The commitments below are still **joined modules
  on one `2px` seam**, with the action inside the third.
- **`004`** — the conversion reflex here is a smiling model, *book now* and a discount. That converts
  the visitor who was already going to book and does nothing for the one who has been putting it off
  for years, which is most of them. So the three refusals still come **before the headline** — but now
  they arrive as a filled amber band **pulled up over the lower edge of the room.** The picture is the
  largest element on the page and the refusals are still the first thing read. **The photograph shows
  the room; it is not asked to make the argument.**
- **`005`** — **dentistry is the only profession where the customer cannot see what is being done to
  them.** They are lying down, mouth open, somebody working out of sight, and that asymmetry is the
  root of the distrust — not the pain, which is brief, and not the price, which is written down
  somewhere. The hero names it, answers it with the four things said out loud — what, why, how long,
  and **raise your hand and we stop** — and then shows **the ceiling above the chair.** Every other
  hero in this sector photographs the room from the doorway, at its most flattering, from an angle no
  patient will ever occupy. This one is framed from the chair, so it is a picture of a ceiling: *here
  is what you will be looking at instead.* It is the only photograph in the batch a competitor would
  refuse to take.

`005` carries its own proposition rather than the shared one, which the `S01` role permits: a hero
batch varies the headline while the commitments and the action stay constant.

## The Page Rhythm, After The Revision

`S01` no longer supplies a shape `C` at any variant. Rule 2 of the contract — **every assembled page
must contain at least one shape `C`** — is still met where it was already met: variant `004` takes its
`C` from `S02`, and variant `005` from `S02` and `S03`.

**Variants `001`, `002` and `003` have no `C` in the first five sections and now owe one.** They owed
one before this revision too; the revision does not change the debt, and it is recorded here so a
later section pays it deliberately rather than by accident.

Rule 1 — never more than two consecutive `A` at one variant — is unaffected: this batch is `B` across
the board.

## What The Rest Of The Sector Inherits

This batch fixes, for `DN-S02` onward: the register (commitments, not adjectives), the three
anti-pattern photographs, the claims limits, the five themes, and the tone of the one action. The
contract is the file to read first; this is the batch that produced it.

And from the revision:

- **A hero is entitled to media.** Contract rule 3 says a section is not entitled to media just
  because it could have some. That rule is about restraint in the middle of a page, and it was
  over-applied at the top of one. **The section whose job is the first impression is the one place the
  photograph is the argument rather than a decoration.**
- **A slider is legitimate if it does not move by itself.** Scroll-snap plus a visible track gives a
  real one with no script, no dependency and no autoplay, and it degrades to a plain scroller.
- **Reserved-area labels cost words.** In the `40–90` hero band, three panel labels are worth more
  than a decorative eyebrow, and `003` spends them that way.
