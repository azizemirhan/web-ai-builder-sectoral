## The Section Role

What equipment the clinic has, and whether the patient can tell the difference from the chair.

## The Trap

A grid of acronyms and beige boxes — *CBCT*, *CAD/CAM*, *intraoral scanner* — under a heading about
state-of-the-art technology. None of it is checkable and the visitor cannot evaluate any of it, so the
page reads as a list of the clinic's purchases rather than an account of their visit.

## The Governing Idea

> **Every machine here replaced something. That is the only reason it is listed.**

## The Four, And The Field Nobody Prints

Shared across all five studies, so the batch varies by composition rather than by copy. The last
column is the one no clinic publishes.

| Machine | Replaced | What you notice | **What it does not do** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The scanner | The putty impression tray | Nothing sets in your mouth; about four minutes | **Cannot see under the gum** |
| The 3D X-ray | A referral to a hospital scanner | One appointment instead of two | **Uses more radiation than a flat X-ray, so one is taken only when a flat picture cannot answer the question** |
| The milling unit | The temporary crown and the second visit | Nothing to come loose over the weekend | **Does not suit every crown** |
| The screen at the chair | Our description of what we found | You see it while we are looking at it | **A picture is not a diagnosis. Someone still has to read it** |

The radiation line is the fact every practice holds, has a written rule about, and never publishes.
It is the only sentence in this section that costs the clinic something to print, which is why every
study carries it in full — including the rule, not just the admission.

## The Closing Demotion

> **None of it makes a filling last longer than the hands that place it.**

In all five. A technology page that will not say this is selling the equipment rather than the care,
and the sector is full of them.

## No Acronyms, No Brands

*CBCT* and *CAD/CAM* are the words a supplier sells with. **The 3D X-ray** and **the milling unit** are
what they are. A scan confirms neither acronym, nor any model or manufacturer name, appears in any
study.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe reading: the two machines a visitor could point at, paired at the top, then the
  four swaps in two runs with each limit on an accent rule. The lead tells the reader how to read the
  page — *what each one cannot do is printed underneath it* — which is the plainest available
  statement of the section's method.
- **`002`** — **no photograph, in the one section where the contract licenses one.** A picture of a
  scanner is a beige box on a stand; it cannot show what the machine replaced, how long the scan
  takes, or what it fails to see, and those are the only three things on the page that matter.
  **Permitted is not the same as useful.** What takes the photograph's place is a colour field: the
  four swaps sit together on one tinted ground at display size, so the block that would have been an
  image is instead the sentence *the putty tray is gone.*
- **`003`** — **the tier boundary is the argument.** Three of the four items are hardware and get a
  reserved area each. The fourth is not: the screen at the chair replaced *our description of what we
  found*, and what changed there was **a decision about who is allowed to look, not a purchase.** So
  it runs full width beneath the grid, on the accent, with no area — because there is nothing to
  photograph. A four-cell grid would have flattened that into a fourth tile, which is what every
  technology page in this sector does and why they read as inventories.
- **`004`** — **the disclosure is not a footnote.** The list runs first, second, third, fourth, and the
  second item is a filled amber band at display size that interrupts the row it belongs in, carrying
  its own limit at the size the machine name is. The reserved area — the unit itself — then sits
  directly beneath the admission rather than beside the headline as reassurance. A disclosure at the
  top of a page is a disclaimer and readers skip those; a disclosure **in the row it belongs to**
  cannot be read past. **The item most likely to impress is the item given the page's only warning.**
- **`005`** — **the page is written as a list of absences.** Not *we have a scanner* but **the putty
  tray is gone**; not *we have a milling unit* but **the temporary crown is gone.** The machine that
  took each place is set underneath in small accent type, subordinate to the thing it removed.

`005` is the sector-native reading. **A patient never experiences a machine — they experience its
absence:** the tray that is not in their mouth, the second appointment they are not booking, the crown
that does not come loose on a Saturday. A technology page that leads with the hardware describes the
clinic's purchase; one that leads with the removal describes the visit. Its reserved area is the
batch's only whole-room shot, singling out no machine, because the argument is that no single machine
is the point.

## Page Rhythm — Two Contract Rules Decided This Batch

**Rule 1 fixed the shapes.** `S04` and `S05` are both shape `A` at variants `001`, `003` and `005`.
Never more than two consecutive `A` at one variant, so `S06` could not be `A` at any of the three. It
is `B` or `C` across the board.

**Rule 2 was a debt, and this batch pays part of it.** Every assembled page must contain at least one
shape `C`. Variant `002` had none in `S01`–`S05` — five anchored sections in a row — and the `S01`
batch document recorded the debt when the hero revision removed that section's `C` studies. **`002` is
the `C` here**, and the reasoning is native to the section rather than retrofitted: the one place a
photograph is licensed is the sharpest place to argue that a photograph is useless.

Variants `001` and `003` still owe a `C`. `004` and `005` take theirs from `S02` and `S03`.

### Found only by rendering — the same defect twice

- **`003`'s three card seams did not align.** The swap paragraph runs to three lines in one card and
  two in the others, so the rule above each limit landed at three different heights — in the study
  whose entire claim is that it is structured. A floor of three lines on the swap paragraph puts the
  seams on one line.
- **`005`'s four columns had the same fault**, from the other direction: *the temporary crown* wraps to
  two lines where the other three absences fit on one, dropping that column's accent label and seam
  below its neighbours. A floor of two lines on the heading fixes it.

The general form, worth carrying forward: **a grid whose items carry a shared horizontal rule needs a
floor on the element above that rule.** Equal card heights are not enough — the seam is what the eye
reads as alignment, and the seam moves with the text above it, not with the bottom of the card.

### Also found by rendering

- **`002`'s closing line broke to three with *it.* alone.** The measure was `26ch` at display size.
  Widened to `36ch`; it sets on two.

### Found by the parity diff

Nothing missing on the first pass — but the diff is what confirmed the **radiation rule** survived
into all five studies in full. The admission alone (*it uses more radiation*) is easy to keep; the
rule that follows it (*so we take one only when a flat picture cannot answer the question*) is the
part that would have been cut for space in `004`, where it sits at display size in the band.

### Found by the word count

`001` came in at **167**, three under the band floor. Rather than pad it, the lead took the sentence
that tells the reader how the page works — *what each one cannot do is printed underneath it* — which
is the right line for the Universal / Safe variant to carry anyway.

## What This Batch Fixes For The Rest Of The Sector

- **Equipment is named in plain language, never as an acronym or a brand.**
- **Every machine is stated as a swap**, and the swap is what the patient can check.
- **The limit is printed at the same weight as the capability**, including the radiation rule.
- **Permitted is not the same as useful** — a licensed media subject is still not an entitlement, and
  `002` is the study that says so.
- **A shared seam across a grid needs a floor above it**, or the structure the study claims is not the
  structure it renders.
