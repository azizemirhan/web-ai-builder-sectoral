## The Section Role

What the clinic treats, and — the part that decides whether the section works — **whether the patient
can find themselves in it.**

## The Trap

The default is six procedure names: *implants, veneers, endodontics, orthodontics, hygiene,
whitening*. Every element of that fails the same way as the sector's stock photography: it is the
clinic's vocabulary, not the patient's.

> **It asks the patient to translate their problem into clinical language before they are allowed to
> proceed.** They cannot do it reliably, so they guess or they leave.

## The Governing Idea

> **Nobody looks up composite bonding. They look up the thing that is wrong.**

So **the symptom is the title and the procedure name is the small label** — the same correction `S01`
made to the sector's register: say the thing the visitor already knows, not the thing the clinic
already knows.

## The Third Field, Which Nobody Prints

Each entry carries **how soon it needs looking at**, because an anxious person's first question is not
what it is called but whether they have left it too late.

**Two of the six are *when you are ready*, and they are the two that cost the most.** A treatments
section that marks its own expensive work as non-urgent is doing the only honest triage available on
a website. It is not a diagnosis and it is not personalised — it is how the clinic sorts its own
appointment book, and the contract's claims limits are respected throughout: no price, no guarantee,
no comfort claim.

## The Media Finding

This batch produced the sector's first real application of the contract's first test — **ask what the
photograph is of** — and the answer changed the shape of the section.

The obvious structured answer is a six-up card grid with an image in every card, and the contract's
own device list offers it. **It is the wrong answer here.** Six symptoms do not have six honest
photographs: three of them have none at all, and the pictures a clinic reaches for to fill three empty
cells are precisely the three the contract bans — the stock smile, the mouth, the instrument.

> **A six-up grid in this section would have forced a banned image into three cells.**

So no study carries six. `001` anchors on **one** honest area — reception, where all six of these
actually begin. `002` places **one** band at the hinge between the urgent three and the unhurried
three. `003` builds a **bento of eight cells** — six symptom modules and two image cells, sharing one
seam — so the media is *in* the grid rather than beside it, and no cell was filled with a photograph
of nothing to make the pattern come out even. `004` and `005` carry none and each says why.

Where entries are not cards, they are **type on the ground**: a card without media is a table cell
with rounded corners.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe reading: the six three across, each with the triage pill, the symptom, the
  procedure name small, and what the first visit is.
- **`002`** — the order is the argument. A treatments section is conventionally sorted by what the
  clinic wants to sell; here it is sorted by how soon, so the toothache is first and the two most
  expensive treatments are last. The band sits at the hinge, and the line under it reads *below this
  line, nothing is urgent.*
- **`003`** — eight cells on one `2px` seam, the urgent one held in the accent. Triage as the
  numbered-card-set device rather than decoration.
- **`004`** — **sorting against yourself.** The emergency sits alone in a filled band; the two most
  expensive treatments come last, smallest, under a heading that says neither is urgent. **A clinic
  that marks its own most profitable work as non-urgent is the only kind whose urgency you can
  believe** when it does say *now* — and it gives up the top of the section, where every competitor
  puts the implants.
- **`005`** — **the six are ordered by how long people wait before coming in.** Two days for a
  toothache; twelve years for crooked teeth. The gap between those two numbers is the subject of this
  entire sector and no clinic has printed it, because it looks like an accusation. It is the opposite:
  the long numbers are the things people are embarrassed about, and the section says so. It is the
  strongest form of `S01`'s promise — **we are not going to ask why you left it, and here is the proof
  that we already know.**

`005`'s waits are the clinic's own observation of its own appointment book, marked
`data-placeholder="true"`. They are explicitly **not** clinical averages, population statistics, or a
claim about what anybody should do, and the study says so in its header.
