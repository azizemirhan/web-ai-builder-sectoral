## The Section Role

Who works here, and which of them is allowed to call themselves what.

## The Trap

A grid of headshots, a string of letters nobody can parse, and a line about being passionate about
creating beautiful smiles. None of it answers either question a patient actually has: **who will see
me**, and **is this person allowed to do this?**

## The Governing Idea

> **Specialist is a protected title, and most clinics use it loosely.**

Some of these people are on a specialist register and some are not, and the page says which —
**including the principal, who is not.** A team page that marks its own most senior name as *not a
specialist* is the only kind whose specialists mean anything.

*Not a dentist* on the hygienist is the third status and the one clinics blur hardest. It is a fact,
it is checkable, and it costs nothing to state.

## Credentials Are Reserved, Never Invented

The contract is explicit: registration numbers, specialist titles, qualifications and memberships are
verifiable facts about real people. **No study prints letters after a name.** What every study prints
instead is where to check:

> **The letters after our names are on the public register, not on this page. Look us up before you
> book.**

That cannot be fabricated, and it is more use than the letters would have been. It is the same move
`CONS-S26` made about credentials, arrived at independently here because the constraint is the same.

## Names, Portraits And Pronouns

- Placeholder names in initial-and-surname form, marked `data-placeholder="true"`.
- **Every portrait label names the role** — *the principal dentist, mid-sentence*; *the periodontist,
  at work* — never the placeholder person. A scan confirms no label carries a name.
- **No gendered pronoun anywhere.** Inventing a gender for a placeholder person is the same class of
  error as inventing a qualification.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe reading: five cards, portrait above the status pill, media in every item.
- **`002`** — a team page gives everyone an identical headshot at an identical size, which claims they
  are equally likely to treat you. **They are not, and the clinic knows the ratio exactly.** So the
  one portrait goes to the person the reader will actually meet, and the two registered specialists —
  the ones a competitor would lead with — are four lines of type further down.
- **`003`** — the **status rides on the portrait**, so the one checkable fact about each person is the
  first thing the eye lands on and cannot be separated from them. The offset drop is not styling: an
  aligned grid of five faces states that they are interchangeable, and the section's whole argument is
  that they are not.
- **`004`** — **handing over the check.** Three of them: the public register for the title, the
  appointment book for who you will see, and the referral note for what leaves the practice. **A team
  page that tells you how to catch it lying is the only kind worth reading** — and it gives up the
  impression, which is the entire conventional asset here.
- **`005`** — **everybody is sized by how often you will actually see them.** Most patients see the
  hygienist three times a year, their own dentist twice, and the oral surgeon never. So the hygienist —
  the least senior person here, and the one no clinic puts first — is the largest thing on the page,
  and the two registered specialists are the smallest. The page is laid out at the true frequency
  rather than the flattering one.

`005` is the sector-native reading because every section so far has replaced an impression with
something checkable — a symptom instead of a procedure name, a wait instead of a promise, a
reversibility line instead of an outcome. **Size is the version of that available to a team page**,
and it costs the clinic the one thing a team page is usually for.
