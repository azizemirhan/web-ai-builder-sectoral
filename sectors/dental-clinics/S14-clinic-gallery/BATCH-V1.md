## The Section Where The Ban Bites

Thirteen sections into this sector, media has been scarce — five batches carried a `C` because the
honest subject count was one or none, and no shape `A` has appeared since `S05`. **This is the section
where six honest subjects genuinely exist**, so media in every item is correct here rather than
forced.

It is also the section where the contract's hardest anti-pattern applies directly. **The empty white
surgery is banned outright, and a clinic gallery is largely photographs of empty rooms.**

The ban resolves cleanly once it is read properly. It is not a ban on photographing a surgery — it is
a ban on the estate-agent shot: scrubbed, unoccupied, over-lit, offered as evidence of a quality a
photograph cannot carry. Three things keep this batch clear of it, and all three are on the page
rather than in a comment:

- **Taken on an ordinary working day, in this order, and nothing was moved.**
- **Every caption is a fact about what happens**, never an adjective about how it looks. The scan
  rejects *modern*, *stunning*, *calming*, *relaxing*, *luxury* and *spa*.
- **`005` fixes it at the cause.** A gallery is shot from where a photographer stands — the corner
  with the widest angle, lens low, room emptied to read as space. **That vantage belongs to nobody who
  will ever be in the building.** Every frame there is shot from where the visitor will be, and a room
  photographed from the seat a patient sits in cannot be an estate-agent shot, because the estate
  agent never sits down.

## The Governing Idea

> **A room is not what it looks like. It is what happens in it, so that is the caption.**

## What Is Not Photographed

> **Nobody's face, no treatment in progress, nothing from a supplier's catalogue.**

`S04` reserves portraits and `S05` reserves the mouth. **The third refusal is this section's own** —
the stock interior is the specific temptation of a gallery, and no other section had to name it.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the canonical use of shape `A`: six frames in a three-across grid, in route order, each
  captioned. The straight gallery, done honestly.
- **`002`** — **one room, because six is a catalogue.** A gallery of six thumbnails delivers no room in
  particular, which is why every clinic's is interchangeable with every other's. The one it shows is
  the ceiling, because that is the honest answer to what a gallery is being asked: nobody chooses a
  dentist on the waiting-room sofa.
- **`003`** — **the gap is the closing line, drawn.** Four studies *say* that you will be in five of
  these six; this one builds it. The five you enter are one joined object; the sterilisation room sits
  across a real gap on the paper ground. **A reader who never reaches the caption has already been
  told which one is different, and the thing telling them is the space.**
- **`004`** — the conversion-led reading gives up the gallery. Six photographs of rooms is the
  highest-cost, lowest-yield section on a clinic site, and **what converts is the invitation** — *the
  sixth you can ask to see* is an offer to be inspected. So that takes the band, and the page shows
  two rooms: the door, which settles whether somebody can get in, and the ceiling, which settles what
  the appointment is like.
- **`005`** — **every frame is taken from where you will be, and the label says from where.** From the
  pavement. From the chair by the window. From the visitor's side of the desk. From the chair.

`005` is the sector-native reading, and it is the only study in the sector so far whose device is a
*camera position* rather than a layout. It also produces the batch's cleanest parity justification:
**the sterilisation room has no frame there, because there is no vantage** — you are never in it. Its
absence from the grid is the study's rule holding rather than a field being dropped, and its caption
appears in type like everywhere else.

### Found only by rendering

- **`003`'s detached room was the largest image on the page.** The five in the run sit in a five-column
  plate at roughly 155px wide; the sixth had the same `3/4` ratio in a column nearly twice as wide, so
  it rendered about 380px tall against their 205px. **The study argues that the sixth is the one
  outside the route, and the layout was making it the most important thing on the page.** Ratio
  changed to `4/3` so the two match in height rather than in proportion.

  The general form: **matching ratios across columns of different widths does not match sizes.** Where
  two groups must read as equal, match the rendered dimension, not the aspect ratio.

## What This Batch Fixes For The Rest Of The Sector

- **A gallery is a route, not a set.** Order the rooms the way a visitor meets them and caption each
  with what happens there.
- **The empty-surgery ban is about vantage and staging, not about the subject.** A room in use,
  photographed from where the visitor sits, captioned with a fact, is the opposite of the banned shot.
- **The stock interior is a reserved subject**, alongside faces, mouths and credentials.
- **Match rendered size, not aspect ratio**, when two groups of images must read as equal.
