## The Governing Idea

> **Everything that works is free, already public, and fits in one paragraph.**

The trap here is the clinic blog. *Five tips for a whiter smile*, a stock photograph, a category tag, a
date, an author who works at an agency. It exists to be found rather than read, and **the reason it can
be published monthly is that it is not saying anything.**

There are about six things that actually work. None of them is ours and **not one has changed in twenty
years.** So the honest section is the whole of the advice, plus an account of what a clinic would be
adding by writing it out again.

## The Six, And The Three That Cannot Be Bought

Shared across all five studies.

| What works | The part people get wrong |
| --- | --- |
| **Twice a day, with fluoride** | The one last thing at night is the one that matters |
| **Spit, do not rinse** | Rinsing washes off the fluoride you have just put on |
| **Clean between the teeth** | A brush reaches three of the five surfaces |
| **Sugar: how often, not how much** | Six times in a day does more than the same sugar once |
| **Do not smoke** | The largest item on the list, and the least about teeth |
| **Bleeding gums are information** | Not normal, and the earliest thing you can see yourself |

**Three of the six cannot be bought** — sugar frequency, smoking and bleeding gums. `003` makes that
its structure and `005` makes it a heading: *the part that does not photograph.* It is the reason this
section is not a shop, and the reason a clinic blog has so little to sell here.

## What We Will Not Publish

Whitening, because it sells and is not oral health. Charcoal and oil pulling, because **each has a
product attached to it.** And anything monthly, because nothing above changed.

The substitute is the strongest line in the batch, and it is `004`'s closing statement:

> **The most useful document about your mouth is your own record, not an article.**

Nothing to download, no email to leave — ask instead and you get what we told you, in writing, on the
day.

### Why `003` takes an `A` when the article grid is the thing being refused

The card grid a clinic publishes here is **six photographs of nothing** — a stock image per article,
chosen for mood. This grid is six photographs of **six objects that exist**, and every frame answers
something the text cannot: what size, what shape, how worn. Contract rule 3 is satisfied item by item
rather than in aggregate. **The form is the same and the content is its opposite**, which is the
sharpest refusal available.

### Why `002` is the shortest study in the sector

Every other `DN` study is authored at structured density, `170–230`. `002` is authored at **standard
density and comes in at 140.** A section whose claim is *there is less here than you were led to
expect* cannot be the longest thing on the page. **The word count is part of the argument.**

### The S17 fault, now automated

`S17` ended with four instances of one mistake — a `border` and a `max-width` on the same rule, so a
divider stops at the text measure instead of the section edge. **The batch script now checks for it**,
and this batch was authored with the `padding-right` pattern from the start, so it reported clean.

Writing the check exposed a distinction worth keeping: **it fires on `solid` borders only.** A dashed
border in this workspace is an image frame, and a frame legitimately owns its own width — `004`'s
summary slot is `max-width: 600px` and correct. A solid border is a divider, and a divider cut short is
the fault. **The rule is about rules, not about frames.**

### Two faults the script could not catch

- **`005`'s bench read wrong in both directions.** `align-items: end` bottom-aligned the captions as
  well as the frames, so a three-line caption started higher than its neighbours instead of level with
  them — fixed with `align-self: start` on the captions alone. And the `0.72fr` pea column rendered
  **taller than the brush row**, directly contradicting the study's own argument that the pea being
  small is the fact being communicated. The column went to `0.55fr`, and the pea is now the smallest
  thing on the bench, as it is on a real one.
- **`004`'s summary was a 21:9 letterbox** — the wrong shape for a sheet of paper handed across a desk,
  and at full width it dominated a page whose subject is that there is nothing to collect. Now `3 / 2`
  at `600px`.

## What This Batch Fixes For The Rest Of The Sector

- **Density is an argument, not only a constraint.** A section claiming brevity should be short, and
  `002` is authored a full band below its neighbours on purpose.
- **A refused form can be reused against itself.** `003` keeps the six-card grid and puts real objects
  in it; `004` keeps the lead magnet's position and puts the deliverable in it, free.
- **When a study argues about scale, the layout has to obey.** Equal cells would have destroyed `005`.
- **`align-items: end` applies to every item in the grid**, captions included. Bottom-aligning a
  caption is almost never what is wanted.
- **A frame is not a rule.** Both carry borders; only one of them must span the section.
