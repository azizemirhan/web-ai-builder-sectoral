## The Governing Idea

> **Nobody asks the questions in a normal FAQ. These are the six we are asked, in the order we are
> asked them, and the first one is money.**

The sector's version is a list the clinic wishes it were asked, answered with reassurance. **The check
here is the source**, and the ordering is a claim anybody who works at a desk can test: *how much is
it going to be* is the most-asked question in dentistry, and it appears fourth or not at all on every
competitor's page.

## The Section's Hardest Line

**`Will it hurt?` is the question a dental FAQ exists to answer, and the contract bans every way the
sector answers it** — no *pain-free*, no *gentle*, no comfort claim of any kind.

What replaces it is a procedure rather than a reassurance: *we do not know, and anyone who promises is
guessing. You are told before each part, and if you raise your hand we stop.* The first clause is the
honest admission, the second is `S01-005`'s commitment, and the whole thing can be checked on the day.
**A reassurance cannot be checked at all**, which is why the sector's version is worthless even when
it happens to be true.

**An FAQ that answers with new material is an FAQ making things up.** Fifteen sections in, the section
whose job is to answer questions should have nothing left to invent — and it does not. That is the
check on this batch, and it is why the section could be written at all.

## Three We Cannot Answer

> How long a filling will last. Whether you will need more work later. Whether the pain comes back.
>
> **We can tell you what usually happens. Nobody can tell you what will happen to you.**

The three things every patient actually wants to know and no dentist can say. `004` is built on
putting them first.

## No Accordion In Any Study

The collapsed FAQ exists to make a long list look short and to hide the answers a clinic would rather
not have read. **If six are the right six, they fit open.** The scan rejects `<details>`, `<summary>`
and `<button>` outright — this is the first section where the temptation was a control rather than a
claim, and the rule from `S11` applies: **the workspace does not draw a control it cannot honour**,
and it does not draw one it does not need either.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe reading: six in two runs, open, in frequency order, with the money question in
  the accent because it is first.
- **`002`** — **the ordering is the argument, so the ordering is the page.** The content is
  unremarkable; six honest answers is what any careful practice could write. What is not unremarkable
  is that the money question is first, so it takes the measure at display size and the other five run
  beneath at body weight.
- **`003`** — **two voices, two grounds.** An FAQ is a transcript of two people and the sector sets it
  as one, which is why FAQ copy drifts into marketing: with no visible boundary between who asked and
  who answered, the answer can quietly stop being a reply. Twelve rows, one seam, ground changing at
  every turn — **and an answer that stopped answering would be visible as a long cell with no question
  above it.**
- **`004`** — the conversion-led reading opens on what the page cannot do. Putting the three
  unanswerables first means **the page begins by establishing the limits of its own usefulness**, in
  the place a competitor puts its strongest reassurance. Six answers read very differently after that.
- **`005`** — **the answer first, and the question underneath in small type like an attribution.**

`005` is the sector-native reading. Read down and the page is six statements; the questions arrive
afterwards as provenance, which is what they are. **These are things this practice would say whether
or not anybody asked, and the questions are only the reason we know they are worth saying.** An FAQ
that leads with its questions is asking permission to make its points; this one makes them and then
shows its working — the same move the sector makes everywhere else.

### Found only by rendering

- **`003`'s alternation was almost invisible.** The tint was `#f6f8f9` against a `#ffffff` card — a
  difference of two or three values, so the accent rule on the answer rows was doing all the work and
  the study's claim that *the ground changes at every turn* was not true on the screen. Moved to
  `#e8ecef`, which is the theme's own second ground, so the questions sit on the card and the answers
  on the paper.

  The general form: **a ground change that has to be legible as a signal cannot be a tint of the same
  value.** If two grounds carry meaning, use two grounds the theme already has.

## What This Batch Fixes For The Rest Of The Sector

- **An FAQ answers with material established elsewhere, or it is making things up.** The check is that
  every answer can be traced to a section that already committed to it.
- **The order of the questions is a claim, and it is checkable.** Money first, because it always is.
- **Where a comfort claim is banned, answer with a procedure.** *Told before each part, and we stop on
  a signal* survives every scan a reassurance fails.
- **A ground change used as a signal must be a real ground change**, not a tint of the same value.
