# The Trap

A list the clinic wishes it were asked — *do you accept new patients, how often should I visit, what
are your opening hours* — answered with reassurance. It is a marketing surface wearing question marks,
and every reader can feel that nobody said those sentences out loud.

# The Governing Idea

> **Nobody asks the questions in a normal FAQ. These are the six we are asked, in the order we are
> asked them, and the first one is money.**

The ordering is a claim anybody who works at a desk can test. *How much is it going to be* is the
most-asked question in dentistry and it appears fourth, or not at all, on every competitor's page.

# The Six, In Frequency Order

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **How much is it going to be?** | Nobody can tell you before someone looks. The number comes at the end of the first appointment, in writing |
| **Will it hurt?** | We do not know, and anyone who promises is guessing. You are told before each part, and if you raise your hand we stop |
| **Do I actually need this?** | Sometimes not yet. The plan says which, and doing nothing is priced with everything else |
| **Can I stop part way?** | Yes. You pay for what was done and nothing more |
| **How long will I be here?** | About forty minutes for the first appointment. The work varies, and the plan says |
| **Will I see the same dentist?** | Yes, unless they are away, and then we move the appointment rather than swap the person |

**Every answer repays a commitment made elsewhere** — `S10`'s price policy, `S01`'s stop signal,
`S08`'s *sometimes not yet*, `S10`'s stopping terms, `S07`'s durations, `S08`'s continuity. An FAQ
that answers with new material is an FAQ making things up.

# The Answer To "Will It Hurt?"

The contract bans *pain-free* and *gentle*, so the one question a dental FAQ exists to answer cannot
be answered the way the sector answers it. **What is given instead is a procedure**: you are told
before each part, and if you raise your hand we stop. That can be checked on the day. A reassurance
cannot.

# Three We Cannot Answer

> How long a filling will last. Whether you will need more work later. Whether the pain comes back.
>
> **We can tell you what usually happens. Nobody can tell you what will happen to you.**

The three things every patient actually wants to know and no dentist can say. No competitor prints
this, and it is the only honest limit on the whole section.

# No Accordion In Any Study

The collapsed FAQ exists to make a long list look short and to hide the answers a clinic would rather
not have read. **If six questions are the right six, they fit on the page open.** No `<details>`, no
`<summary>`, no JavaScript. The scan rejects all three.

# Media Relationship

Areas run 1 / 0 / 1 / 1 / 0. The only honest subject an FAQ has is **the desk where the questions are
actually asked**, and it is there to support the claim about where the list came from. Two studies
carry none: an FAQ is language, and a photograph of a desk beside a question about money is decoration
standing where the answer should be.
