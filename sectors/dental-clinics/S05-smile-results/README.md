# The Trap

A wall of thumbnails. Different light in each half, a wetter mouth in the second, a caption reading
*Veneers*, and a heading claiming five hundred smiles transformed. Every case looks the same as every
other case, none of them can be checked, and the reader stops believing the page before the third row.

# The Governing Idea

> **A before-and-after tells you nothing unless you know what changed between the two photographs
> besides the teeth.**

Different light is most of what a reader is actually looking at in these galleries. So is a different
angle, a different camera, and the difference between a dry mouth and a wet one. None of it is
disclosed anywhere in this sector.

# The Debt To S03

`S03` refused a before-and-after gallery outright and pointed here, promising *the rules that apply to
them*. **This section owes the reader those rules**, so every study prints all four of them, and every
case carries the field nobody prints: **when the second photograph was taken.**

# The Four Rules

1. **Same camera, same light** — both photographs, every case.
2. **Not retouched** — cropped, and nothing else.
3. **We chose these** — four shown out of sixty-one that year, and the clear ones were picked.
4. **One case each** — not a typical result.

Rule three is the admission nothing in this sector makes out loud. A gallery that states its own
selection ratio is the only one whose individual cases can be read as evidence.

# Media Relationship

Reserved **before-and-after pair** areas, split by a centre divider. The count is deliberately
variable — 4 / 1 / 4 / 2 / 4 — because the argument of `002` and `004` is that fewer cases disclosed
properly beat more cases shown badly.

**No face appears in any reserved area, in any study.** The pairs are cropped to the work and the
consent covers that crop only, which every study states in full.
