# The Trap

A wall of badges: a regulator's logo, an association shield, a tick, a string of letters. **Most of
what is in there is compulsory** — registration, sterilisation to standard, indemnity cover and a
complaints procedure are the conditions of being open at all.

# The Governing Idea

> **Half of what a trust section lists is the law, and obeying it is not an achievement. This one
> marks which half.**

# The Two Halves

| Required by law | Not required. We do it anyway |
| --- | --- |
| Every one of us is on the public register. Practising without it is a criminal offence | Last year's complaints are published here, with what changed after each one |
| Instruments are sterilised to a national standard and the log is kept | The inspection report is linked whether it reads well or not |
| The practice carries indemnity insurance | **If a treatment is the first of its kind for the person doing it, you are told before you agree** |
| There is a complaints procedure, and it is on the wall | Ask at the desk and you can read the sterilisation log |

The third item on the right is the one no competitor prints. Every dentist has a first time for every
procedure; nobody discloses it; **the disclosure costs bookings every time it is honoured**, and there
is no way to display it without also doing it.

# Credentials Stay Reserved

`S04` set it for the sector and this section keeps it:

> **No letters appear after any name on this site.** They are on the public register, which is the
> only place they mean anything.

No invented registration number, membership, inspection grade, founding year or patient count. The
scan for this section rejects letters after a name, named regulators and associations, and any badge
or grade word.

# How This Differs From S04

`S04` Dentists & Specialists is about **people** — who works here and which of them may use a
protected title. `S13` is about **the practice** — what it is obliged to do and what it chooses to.
Neither the field set nor any composition is shared; `S13` inherits only the reserved-credentials
rule.
