## The Constraint That Was Already Set

`S08` said it four batches ago: *you need someone at two in the morning — we are not open. The
out-of-hours number is on the contact page.* An emergency section is the obvious place for a sector to
quietly walk that back and put *24/7* in a red banner.

**It does not get walked back.** This section states the same closure and does the work the banner was
pretending to do: it sorts the problem, sends the worst list to a hospital, and names what the
practice actually holds open.

## The Governing Idea

> **Most of what wakes people at night can wait until morning. Three things cannot, and they are
> first.**

**An emergency section is a triage or it is nothing.** Its job is to get somebody to the right place,
and the right place is often not this one.

## The Three Lists

| | Symptoms | Instruction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hospital, not us** | Bleeding that will not stop. Swelling closing an eye, or making it hard to swallow. A blow to the head | **These are not dental appointments** |
| **Today, and ring first** | A tooth knocked out or knocked loose. A broken tooth with the nerve showing. Swelling in the face that is growing | **Ring as soon as you can; for a knocked-out tooth the first hour matters most** |
| **It can wait until morning** | A lost filling. A lost crown. A chipped edge that does not hurt. An ulcer. Cold sensitivity | **Frightening at midnight, and none of it is worse at nine** |

Two things about this list were decided carefully.

**The knocked-out tooth is in the second list, not the first.** It is the one item a reader might
expect at the top, and putting it there would have been wrong: a hospital is the wrong destination for
it and the hour it needs is a dental hour. The first list is bleeding, airway and head injury — the
three where a dentist is not the answer at all.

**The third list is the longest**, because most people reading a clinic website at midnight are on it.
*None of it is worse at nine* is the most useful sentence in the batch and the one no competitor
prints, because it talks somebody out of ringing.

## The Escape Hatch

> **If you are not sure which list you are on, ring.** Guessing from a list is not your job.

Every study carries it. A triage on a website sorts problems, not people, and the reader is not
qualified to do the sorting — nor should they have to be.

## What The Practice Actually Holds

- **We are not open at night or on Sunday.** The out-of-hours number is on the contact page, answered
  by people who do this all night.
- **We keep two appointments free every working day.** For the middle list. Not bookable in advance —
  they are for that day.

Said apart, the first is a closed door and the second is a boast. **Said together they are a usable
answer**: not now, but today, and here is how that is possible. `004` is built on exactly that
pairing.

## The Five Compositions

- **`001`** — the safe reading: three lists across one row, hardest first, with the top rule of each
  descending in weight. No image, because a person in pain is not browsing.
- **`002`** — **severity decides the order; readership decides the size.** The two urgent lists are
  short hard blocks that need to be findable rather than large, and the list that takes the measure at
  display size is the one most readers are actually on.
- **`003`** — **the instruction is at the bottom of each module, not of the page.** A triage that lists
  three sets of symptoms and puts one instruction at the foot has been laid out for somebody calm. The
  three instructions are the only filled grounds on the page — solid ink, accent, plain rule — so a
  reader scanning without reading picks the right one by darkness alone.
- **`004`** — the conversion-led reading puts the refusal and the reason in one band. The trade is the
  banner: **this section cannot be found by anybody searching for a dentist open at midnight**, because
  it is not one.
- **`005`** — **the art direction is the legibility.** One column, no second thing to look at, type at
  the size the rest of the sector reserves for headlines, and the accent spent once — on the only list
  that ends in *ring us*.

`005` is the sector-native reading. Every other study is laid out for somebody who is reading. This
one is laid out for somebody who is not reading properly: in pain, at night, on a phone, holding a
tooth. **Nothing here is decorative, and that is the design rather than the absence of one.**

### Found only by rendering

- **`005`'s column collapsed to a strip.** The measure was set as `max-width: 40ch` on the column
  wrapper — but `ch` resolves against *that element's* font size, not the display sizes inside it, so
  a column holding 3rem type was sized as though it held 1.06rem type. At 1440px the study occupied
  about a quarter of the frame with three quarters of void beside it, which reads as a broken layout
  rather than as the deliberate single column the study argues for. Replaced with an explicit
  `min(100%, 720px)`.

  The general form, worth carrying: **`ch` on a wrapper does not track the type inside it.** Where a
  measure has to hold display sizes, set it in a length or set it on the element that carries the
  type.

## What This Batch Fixes For The Rest Of The Sector

- **A refusal made in one section is binding on the section that would most like to break it.** `S08`
  said the practice is shut at night; `S12` is where that had to survive contact with a red banner.
- **A triage sends people away first.** The list a clinic cannot help with goes at the top, not in a
  footnote.
- **Urgency is weight and order, never alarm colour.** The red banner is a sector reflex and it is
  banned here by scan, not just by taste.
- **Colour in a decision section is a wayfinding budget.** Spend it on the instructions, not on the
  headings.
- **A `ch` measure on a wrapper does not scale with display type inside it.**
