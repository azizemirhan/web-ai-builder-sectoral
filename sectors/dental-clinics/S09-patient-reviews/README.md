# The Trap

Five cards with first names, five stars and a sentence about somebody being amazing, chosen by the
practice from a much larger set, under a rating badge. Unverifiable, universal, and the reader knows
exactly how it was assembled.

# The Governing Idea

> **A review can tell you how you will be treated. It cannot tell you whether the dentistry was any
> good.**

A patient can judge the wait, the phone, the explanation and the bill. They cannot judge whether a
filling was placed well — and neither can the next reader — so a wall of five-star cards is a wall of
evidence about reception, presented as evidence about dentistry.

# No Testimonial Appears Anywhere In This Batch

The contract reserves patient quotations exactly as it reserves credentials: they are statements by
real people and the workspace does not invent them. **A placeholder testimonial is a fabricated
endorsement**, and no `data-placeholder` marking makes one safe to draft.

The platform name is reserved for the same reason — naming one would assert a business relationship
that does not exist. Every study writes it as `the public listing`.

# What A Review Reaches

| Can tell you | Cannot tell you |
| --- | --- |
| How long you waited | Whether the filling was placed well |
| Whether the phone was answered | Whether the crown fits your bite |
| Whether anyone explained what they were doing | Whether the treatment was needed at all |
| Whether the bill matched the plan | |

> None of that can be judged from the chair, including by the person in it.

# The Four Policies

- **They are not here.** They are on the public listing, where we cannot delete one or move it up.
- **We do not ask at the chair.** Asking somebody while they are still numb is how a high average gets
  built.
- **The wrong ones stay up.** Including the ones we think are unfair.
- **We reply under our own name.** Never anonymously, and never twice to the same one.
