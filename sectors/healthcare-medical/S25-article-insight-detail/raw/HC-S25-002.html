How to describe a change you can see

A short guide to the first thing you will be asked, written so you can answer it.

THE TWO CHAIRS · 21:9

[Approved image caption]

Start with when

The most useful thing you can say is when it started, and what it looked like then. Not what it looks like now.

Say what changed, not what it is

Bigger, darker, raised, sore, itchy, bleeding, spreading. Plain words. Nobody expects you to know the right one.

Say what you did

Anything you put on it, anything you took, anything you stopped. This matters more than most people expect.

Say what worries you

If you are frightened of something in particular, say the word. It will not make it more likely, and it will change what gets ruled out.

What this is not

It does not say what a change is, what to do about it, or whether to worry. It says how to be understood by somebody who can answer that.
