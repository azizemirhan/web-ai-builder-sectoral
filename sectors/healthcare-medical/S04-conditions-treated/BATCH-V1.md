## The Governing Idea

> **Naming it is the easy part. What happens next is the useful part.**

A conditions page is a list of words that frighten people, with nothing after each word. This
batch names the conditions seen most in each of the six `S02` areas — because a visitor searching
for a word needs to find it — and then says what the first visit usually involves, which is the
only thing that can honestly be said about a condition before anyone has been examined. **Named,
not promised.** No condition carries a treatment claim; *a look and a conversation; a sample if
needed* is a description of a visit, not of an outcome.

**Not treated here** is in every study: *anything that needs surgery, cancer treatment or a
hospital — you are referred, and told why; emergencies go to the emergency service.* And the route
for the visitor whose word is not on the list: *not listed? tell us what is wrong.*

## Density

All five are declared **structured, 170–230**, and the batch records why rather than trimming:
this is a list-shaped role. Six areas, each with four or five named conditions and one line on the
first visit, is the minimum a conditions section can carry and still be one; cutting conditions to
reach the standard band would remove the words visitors search for. `003` and `004`, the two type
studies, are the shortest in the batch, which is the right way round.

## Media

Three reserved areas, one each in `001`, `002` and `005`: the examination room in use, the lamp
over the couch close, and the corridor with its doors open. Rooms and equipment the text names —
never a body part, never a diagram. `003` is type because the content is words and page three
already carries the doctor and nurse; `004` is type because page four owes it.
