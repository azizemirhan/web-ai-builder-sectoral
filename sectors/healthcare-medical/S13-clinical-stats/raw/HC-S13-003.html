In numbers

Numbers about the building and the diary, not about you.

Five things we count and will say out loud, each with why it matters to your visit. Nothing here is a result.

THE HANDOVER · 4:3

  • Six

    consulting rooms
    Why it matters

    A first appointment does not wait for a room.

    Where you check it

    Walk the corridor. The doors are numbered.

  • Eleven

    people on the team
    Why it matters

    You are told which one you will see.

    Where you check it

    The team page, by role, and the desk.

  • A week

    usual wait for a first appointment
    Why it matters

    Told to you when you book, not promised in advance.

    Where you check it

    The date on your letter.

  • Half an hour

    usual length of a first visit
    Why it matters

    Long enough to be examined and to ask.

    Where you check it

    The clock on the wall of the room.

  • A few days

    usual wait for results
    Why it matters

    You are told which day to expect them.

    Where you check it

    The day written on your letter.

Not counted in public: outcomes, satisfaction scores, how many people we have seen. None of them tells you anything about your visit.
